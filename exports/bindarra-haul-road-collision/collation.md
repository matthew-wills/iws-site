## Safety issues (organisational level)

### Guideline sets no review trigger and no basis for the positive-call list
- Lane: External Influences
- Tests: Existence Pass - virtually certain / Influence Pass - likely / Importance Not tested
- Note on Existence: The guideline's text and the regulation it is made under are documentary and not in dispute.
- Note on Influence: Argued, and accepted at 70% on the balance, at the standard of proof and no higher. The guideline bears on one path, the listing path: a defined trigger would have required the plan to be reopened when a road was added, and a stated basis for nomination would have given an intersection that fails the sight distance rule a claim on the list. The Against is real, because the mine could have done both on its own, and the 70% says only that the guideline made doing nothing the default. The report should say it is held at the comfort line rather than presenting it as settled.
- Finalised: yes
- Evidence: MSG-14 clause 3.2 requires the traffic management plan to be reviewed within twelve months and after any significant change to the road network, and defines neither the term nor an example of one. Clause 5.1 requires the operator to nominate the intersections and interaction zones at which positive communication is required, and sets no minimum and no basis for the nomination. Clause 3.5 provides that the plan is a site document, not approved by and not submitted to the Inspectorate except on request after an occurrence. Bindarra's plan met the guideline on its face on the day of the occurrence: it had been reviewed inside twelve months and it nominated nine interaction zones. The mine was not in breach. Clause 5.4, which offers five alternative means of making a light vehicle detectable, any one of which satisfies it, is context rather than part of this factor.
- Evidence for influence: Had the guideline defined a significant change and set a basis for choosing positive-call intersections, a new intersection on a haul road would have had a defined route onto the plan's call list between annual reviews, and the eleven Stage 4 roads never referred to the plan's owner would have had somewhere to land. As written, both terms are left to the same site document, reviewed once a year, with no outside eye on either.
- Evidence against influence: The guideline is not what stopped anybody. A site that wanted an out-of-cycle review could hold one under its own clause 9.1 at any time, and MSG-14 does not prevent a mine from listing every intersection it has. Bindarra did not fail to act because the guideline was vague; it failed to act because nothing inside the mine ever put the question in front of the plan's owner. On that reading the external clause is background and the organisational failure is the whole of it.
- Investigator notes: The mine is not in breach of anything, which is exactly why this belongs in External Influences rather than in a compliance finding. Be careful in the report not to let this read as blaming the regulator; the point is the shape of the discretion, not a failure by the Inspectorate. Its one arrow goes to the positive call list. Whether a works order is exempt from management of change is set by the mine's own procedure, not by the guideline, so there is no arrow to the works order.
- Why Mines Safety Guideline MSG-14 revision 4, made under Mining Operations Safety Regulations 2018 r 6.22 matters: The external requirement and the width of the discretion it leaves. The site must review its plan after a significant change, nominate its own interaction zones, and provide detectability by any one of five means. None of those three terms is defined and none of them is approved by anybody outside the mine.

### Spur opened under a works order, so nothing was referred to anyone
- Lane: Organisational Influences
- Tests: Existence Pass - virtually certain / Influence Pass - very likely / Importance Not tested
- Note on Existence: The procedure, the works order and the road register are documentary and agree with each other.
- Note on Influence: Had a management of change been raised, clause 5.4 would have sent the new intersection to the plan's owner, with a survey showing it failing the clause 6.2 sight distance rule and so needing a stop control and the clause 6.4 signs, and to the technical services group, who configure the geofence the intersection was cut inside. Both referrals are standing parts of the procedure, and the three roads raised under one since the last review were referred. The 10% allows that a referral is not a control: the plan's owner might have left the spur to the annual review, and no out-of-cycle review has been held in the plan's eleven years.
