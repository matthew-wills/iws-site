### Traffic management guidance leaves the review trigger undefined and the interaction zones to the site
- Lane: External Influences
- Tests: Existence Pass - virtually certain / Influence Pass - likely / Importance Not tested
- Note on Existence: The guideline's text and the regulation it is made under are documentary and not in dispute.
- Note on Influence: Argued, and accepted at 70% on the balance. A defined trigger and a stated minimum would have given the eleven unreferred roads somewhere to land, and every internal control in this occurrence failed for want of exactly that. Below the comfort of the other influence findings, and the report should say so plainly rather than presenting it as settled.
- Finalised: yes
- Evidence: MSG-14 clause 3.2 requires the traffic management plan to be reviewed within twelve months and after any significant change to the road network, and defines neither the term nor an example of one. Clause 5.1 requires the operator to nominate the intersections and interaction zones at which positive communication is required, and sets no minimum and no basis for the nomination. Clause 5.4 requires a light vehicle to be detectable by a heavy vehicle operator and offers five alternative means, any one of which satisfies it. Clause 3.5 provides that the plan is a site document, not approved by and not submitted to the Inspectorate except on request after an occurrence. Bindarra's plan met the guideline on its face on the day of the occurrence: it had been reviewed inside twelve months, it nominated nine interaction zones, and it provided for detectability by line of sight and by elevated marking. The mine was not in breach.
- Evidence for influence: Three separate terms in the one guideline are left to the site to fill in, and at Bindarra all three were filled in by the same document, reviewed once a year, with no outside eye on any of it.
- Evidence against influence: Against: the guideline is not what stopped anybody. A site that wanted an out-of-cycle review could hold one under its own clause 9.1 at any time, and MSG-14 does not prevent a mine from listing every intersection it has. Bindarra did not fail to act because the guideline was vague; it failed to act because nothing inside the mine ever put the question in front of the plan's owner. On that reading the external clause is background and the organisational failure is the whole of it.
- Investigator notes: The mine is not in breach of anything, which is exactly why this belongs in External Influences rather than in a compliance finding. Be careful in the report not to let this read as blaming the regulator; the point is the shape of the discretion, not a failure by the Inspectorate.
- Why Mines Safety Guideline MSG-14 revision 4, made under Mining Operations Safety Regulations 2018 r 6.22 matters: The external requirement and the width of the discretion it leaves. The site must review its plan after a significant change, nominate its own interaction zones, and provide detectability by any one of five means. None of those three terms is defined and none of them is approved by anybody outside the mine.
