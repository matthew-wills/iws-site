Case study, worked end to end

The same method, on a mine site instead of an airfield

Bindarra is a collision between a 220 tonne haul truck and a light vehicle at a new intersection in an open pit, on the tenth night of a fourteen night swing. What follows is that investigation in the order it happened: what came in, what the investigator did with it, and what the application recorded at each step. Every picture is a real screen from that file. Click one to see it at full size.

Nothing in the workflow changes between an aerodrome and a gold mine. The lanes are the same, the three tests are the same, and the report comes out of the same record. What changes is the evidence: a fleet management download instead of a flight recorder, a survey pickup instead of a photometric check, a pit channel instead of a tower frequency.

Everything in this case study is fictional: the mine, the operator, the contractors, the regulator, the people and every record. The documents at the end are published exactly as the application produced them, faults and all.

1 The occurrence as first notified

Two vehicles at a blind intersection, at ten to three in the morning

This is what was known by dawn on 12/08/2026, before anybody had pulled a download or opened a procedure. It is the brief the investigation started from.

A haul truck was descending the main ramp of the Stage 4 open pit, empty, at about 37 km/h. A light vehicle carrying one grade control technician came out of a side road onto the ramp in front of it. The side road had been cut eleven days earlier to reach a new dewatering bore.

The side road meets the ramp 34 m beyond a crest. The truck operator had about 41 m of sight line and four seconds. He braked and steered right into the windrow, which took the impact from 37 km/h down to 24 km/h and put the collision on the wall side of the road rather than the open side. The truck's front left struck the light vehicle's right side, pushed it 18 m down the ramp and rolled it onto its left side against the inner windrow.

The driver was conscious throughout. His door was against the ground, so he was taken out through the rear of the vehicle 27 minutes later and flown out with a fractured clavicle, three fractured ribs and a small pneumothorax. His seatbelt was fastened and the webbing carried loading marks; the roll-over structure deformed and was not breached.

The truck operator's own report, written the following afternoon under the site's just culture scheme, put three things on the record before anyone asked him. He did not know the road was open. He made no radio call, and nor does anybody else, because the site's plan does not require one there. And the collision awareness system in his cab had never once alerted him anywhere in that pit.

2 What came in first

The report that arrived seven days early

The first thing filed in this investigation was not written after the collision. It was written a week before it, by somebody else, about the same intersection.

The first material

Take both reports in as they were written

HAZ-2026-0917 was raised at ten past ten on a Tuesday night by a dewatering technician who came out of the same side road in front of a descending truck sixty metres away, reversed back in, and wrote it up when he got back. He described the rise in the road, the windrow along the side, the missing sign, and his own conclusion: the trucks do not know the road is there. It was closed two days later by reminding the crew at pre-start to take care and to give way, and the risk rating was reduced from Medium to Low on closure.

ASR-2026-0341 is the truck operator's, submitted the afternoon after the collision. One tick says it came in under the site's just culture scheme, and from then on report drafting is told the account may inform the investigation and must not be quoted into a distributed document. The audit at the end checks that it was not.

3 The evidence pack

Ten documents, and what each one settled

By the end of the gathering phase the investigation held ten source documents. They are published here as one pack, so everything below can be read against the same material the investigator had.

Safety reports

The hazard report from seven days before, with the entry that closed it, and the truck operator's own account from the afternoon after. The first is the evidence that the mine already knew; the second opened two lines of enquiry that the records then confirmed.

Occurrence notification

The first account: what happened, the injuries, the damage, the risk rating and the actions taken that night. It opened the file and seeded the first timeline events.

Fleet management and collision awareness download

The truck's own data. It fixed the speed at 37 km/h, the response at 1.4 seconds, the impact at 24 km/h, and the fact that the brakes performed to specification. It also closed the brake failure hypothesis, and it showed the detection system logging the light vehicle at 21 m and saying nothing.

Pit channel radio extract

Every transmission on the pit channel across the shift's second post-crib cycle. It is the evidence that no call was made by either vehicle about the side road, the ramp or a light vehicle being anywhere in the pit, and it timed the emergency response from the collision onwards.

Scene examination and survey record

The marks, the impact position and the roll, then the survey department's pickup of the intersection to the eye height of each vehicle. This is where the 41 m comes from, and where the wet surface hypothesis was closed.

Site procedures and policy extracts

The statutory guideline, the traffic management plan and its appendix of intersections, the management of change procedure, the works order that cut the road, and the contractor management standard. Everything in the external, organisational and risk control lanes rests on these.

Equipment, roster, training and site records

The brake tests either side of the collision, the roster and the operator's own sleep record, both drivers' training files, the plan's review history, a search of two years of hazard reports, twelve months of collision awareness statistics, and the emergency response log.

Interview transcript, haul truck operator

The operator's account in his own words, taken once the download was in hand. It is what turned the alert geofence from a system configuration detail into a factor that could be tested.

Interview notes, night shift supervisor

Not recorded, so the investigator's notes are the record. The most useful account for the organisational picture: he did not know a light vehicle was in his pit, and there is no process by which he could have.

Interview notes, light vehicle driver

Also not recorded, at his request, and read back to him at the end. He corrected himself twice, including on the point least helpful to him, and his description of the sight line matches the survey to within a few metres.

4 Interviews, and 5 the timeline

Three accounts, two of them never recorded

All three interviews were held once the records were in hand, so the questions could be about what the records did not say. Only one of the three was recorded, which is the ordinary case on a mine site and not a problem for the record.

The interviews

Import the one transcript, write up the other two

The haul truck operator's interview was conducted over Teams and its transcript came in as the .vtt download, imported straight into the record. A .docx download, or a pasted transcript, import the same way. The other two were not recorded, so the investigator's own notes are the record: free recall, additional questions and follow-up questions, each in its own window, written up while it was fresh and read back to the interviewee at the end.

De-identification is built into the import. Every speaker in a transcript is mapped to a role, and it is the role, never the name, that reaches the record, the collated export or any drafting model. On this file the speakers are lead investigator, second investigator and haul truck operator, and that is all anyone downstream ever sees. No name appears anywhere in the investigation.

Two of the three records are finalised, which locks them to read-only and tells drafting the account is settled. The injured driver's is deliberately left open, because two questions are still outstanding with him and a second conversation is planned once he is further along. An unfinalised record is not a gap in the file; it is the file saying honestly where the work has got to.

What the app recordedThe corrected transcript, the role for every speaker, the investigator's notes beside it, and whether each account has been finalised.

The timeline

Build the sequence, and say what each time rests on

The timeline does not start on the night. It starts on 02/08, when the road closed sign came down and the side road was first used, runs through the hazard report and its closure, and only then reaches the shift itself. Ten events in all, mixing sources: the works log gives the road, the reporting system gives the warning, the download gives the descent to a tenth of a second, the pit channel gives the calls, and the response log gives the extrication.

Each event carries its source and its time basis, so the sequence can be defended when two records disagree about when something happened. Here they did not: the repeater clock and the fleet management clock were checked against each other and agreed to within four tenths of a second.

What the app recordedThe ordered events, the evidence behind each one, the time basis it rests on, and which event is the critical one.

6 Building the map

The analysis, built one stage at a time

The map is the analysis. It starts with the occurrence itself, fills with everything that might have played a part, and is then cut down by testing rather than by opinion. These are three real states of this investigation's own file.

Three states of the same map

Place the critical event, and nothing else

One object goes down first: Light vehicle driver seriously injured in a collision with a haul truck on the main ramp, in the Occurrence Events lane at the bottom right, with what happened and the proof of its significance recorded against it.

Everything else on the map exists to explain this one object. Putting it down first is what stops the analysis drifting into everything that was ever wrong with the pit.

What the app recordedThe critical event as an object, in its lane, at its point in time.

Put every hypothesis up, including the ones you expect to fail

Seventeen more objects go up, scattered roughly where they belong in lane and in time. The occurrence sequence: both vehicles arrived at the intersection with neither operator aware of the other, the truck's front left struck the light vehicle's right side at 24 km/h, the light vehicle was pushed 18 m and rolled onto its side. The two operators' actions: neither made a positive call on the pit channel, the light vehicle entered the ramp without stopping, and the truck operator braked and steered into the windrow within 1.4 seconds.

The conditions they were working in: 41 m of sight line beyond a crest against a plan minimum of 120 m, dust on the ramp, and night ten of fourteen in the eleventh hour of a shift. Above them the controls and the organisation: positive calls required only at listed intersections, collision awareness alerts suppressed inside a geofence, one paramedic on night shift, the road cut under a works order with no management of change, a contractor induction accepted as equivalent, and the statutory guidance that leaves the trigger for a review undefined.

Two of them are up there to be disproved: The ramp surface was wet and the truck could not stop and The haul truck's service brakes were degraded. Nothing is filtered at this point. The method works by testing, not by intuition, and a hypothesis that is later excluded still strengthens the record.

What the app recordedEach hypothesis as an untested object, drawn with a dashed white border until someone has tested it.

Test as the evidence arrives, and let the arrows follow

Ten objects have been tested here. The occurrence sequence is joined up, convergence to impact to roll-over to the injury, and the organisational chain has started to form above it, from the guideline's undefined trigger down to the works order and the unarmed call requirement. Both hypotheses raised to be disproved have failed Existence and are struck through on the map, with the reason on the record.

An investigation does not arrive in order. An interview changes what a factor means, a records search closes a line of enquiry, a new hypothesis turns up in week three. On this one the twelve months of collision awareness statistics were only requested because the truck operator said in his interview that the system had never alerted him, and they changed what that object meant. Objects are moved, tested, connected and reconnected as that happens, and the map is never more than the current state of the evidence.

Every arrow is a claim that one thing led to another. It stays grey and dashed until the object it comes from has passed its tests, and turns green when it has, so the picture builds itself as the work is done.

What the app recordedEvery object and every connection, with the state of each test behind them.

7 Testing a factor

Nothing is a finding until it has passed its tests

Three questions are asked of every object on the map, in order, and a confidence is recorded for each answer. What the object becomes follows from the answers, not from the investigator's view of it.

Existence

Did this event or condition actually exist?

If it did, the border turns solid and the next question is asked. If it did not, the hypothesis is excluded: it stays on the map, struck through, with the reason recorded.

PassFailInconclusive
Influence

Did it influence anything else, and if so in what way?

If it did, it is a contributing safety factor and its outgoing arrows become proven causal paths. Where the evidence cuts both ways, the test is argued with evidence for and against.

PassFailArgued
Importance

If it did not influence anything, is it still important enough to raise in the report?

A factor that played no part in this occurrence may still be a risk worth addressing. If so, it is a finding in its own right, an other risk factor, and it carries no arrows.

PassFail

The three questions, on one object

Ask them of the mine's own defence

The object is Positive radio calls required only at listed intersections, and the list is rewritten once a year. A call is required before entering any of the nine intersections at Appendix B of the traffic management plan, and clause 4.3.2 requires none anywhere else. An intersection joins Appendix B by appearing on the plan's road layout drawing, and that drawing is reissued at the annual review. The new road was on neither, and the next review fell three months after the collision.

Did it exist? Yes, at 100 per cent: the plan's text, its appendix and its review history are documentary. Did it influence the occurrence? Yes, at 90 per cent. Had a call been required, the channel was open, both vehicles were on it, and the dispatcher was already tracking the truck closely enough to place it within four minutes of the loading face. The missing 10 per cent allows that a call gives knowledge, not separation.

The corner dot says how far the object's own investigation has got: amber while it is still being worked, green when the investigator has finished with it. Sixteen of the eighteen objects on this map are green, and two were deliberately left amber.

What the app recordedEach verdict with its confidence and its evidence, the investigator's reasoning, and the standard of proof the investigation set. The form warns when a pass falls short of it.

Argue the one the evidence cuts both ways on

Beside it is Collision awareness alerts suppressed inside a geofence extended to cover the new intersection. The system was fitted, working, and configured with a suppression zone drawn around the loading face in 2025 to stop nuisance alerts around the excavator, which is a reasonable thing to do. In June 2026 the face advanced and the zone was extended 180 m up the ramp, past the new intersection, as a system configuration task.

For: in twelve months that truck raised 412 alerts, every one outside the zone and none inside it, and sixty-eight events inside it were suppressed, nine of them a light vehicle on the ramp below the boundary. The operator said in his own report, before anyone asked him, that he had taken a quiet system to mean an empty road. Against: the alert would have come 1.8 seconds after he had already braked, the system is advisory only, and at 41 m of sight line the outcome was fixed before either the alert or the sighting.

Influence was accepted at 75 per cent, argued. The investigation's standard of proof is the application's default of 66 per cent, so this clears it, but not comfortably, and the report carries that as its confidence word rather than presenting the finding as settled.

What the app recordedBoth sides of the argument as separate evidence, the confidence the investigator settled on after weighing them, and the reasoning for that number.

8 The finished map

The whole investigation in one image

Eighteen objects and fifteen arrows, with a contributing safety factor in every one of the six lanes. Left to right is time, bottom to top is organisational depth, and the critical event sits at the bottom right where every chain arrives.

What the map says

Three chains converge on the two vehicles meeting, and through that on the injury. A governance chain runs from the guideline's undefined review trigger through the works order that cut the road to the call requirement that could not reach it. A road chain runs from the same works order to the 41 m sight line. A shorter induction chain runs to the driver coming out of the side road. A green arrow also runs from the positive event, the truck operator braking and steering into the windrow, because that changed the outcome as well.

The two orange objects carry no arrows at all. They failed Influence, so there is no proven causal path to draw and an arrow would be a claim the evidence does not support. They stay on the map because they are findings in their own right.

The factor that reads like the cause and is not

Night ten of fourteen, in the eleventh hour of a shift, at ten to three in the morning. It is exactly where a reader expects to find the answer, and the evidence will not carry it. The download gives a 1.4 second response, inside the 1.5 seconds the plan's own stopping distance calculation allows for. The braking and the steering that followed were correct and immediate. And the arithmetic settles it: at 41 m of sight line and 37 km/h, a response time of zero still ends in a collision.

So Influence failed at 25 per cent, argued both ways, and Importance passed at 85. It is recorded as an other risk factor, it carries no arrow, and it goes into the report anyway, because the same roster is worked on three other pits on that site.

The one unproven arrow

One arrow is grey and dashed: the claim that dust on the ramp reduced the truck operator's ability to see the light vehicle. A water cart pass was seven minutes overdue and the dust monitor read well above the shift mean. That shows a deviation. It does not show an effect on anybody's vision, and the two people who were there disagree: the operator said there was dust hanging and declined to say it made a difference, and the driver, in the same place four seconds earlier, was firm that the air was clear.

Existence was left inconclusive at 45 per cent, so the object never reached Influence and its arrow cannot be drawn as proven. The application draws it grey and dashed rather than dropping it, because an open line of enquiry is part of the record and the report has to say that it is open.

  • Blue, solid border: a contributing safety factor. It existed and it influenced the occurrence.
  • Orange: an other risk factor. It existed, it did not influence this occurrence, and it is still worth raising.
  • Struck through, dashed: an excluded hypothesis. It did not exist, and the reason is on the record.
  • Green, rounded: a positive event, something that reduced the risk or limited the outcome.
  • Red, rounded: the critical event, the occurrence itself.
  • White, dashed: not yet tested, or a test left inconclusive.
  • Green arrow: a proven causal path. The object it comes from has passed Influence.
  • Grey dashed arrow: a claim still under test.
  • Green dot: the object's investigation is complete and its evidence finalised.
  • Amber dot: still being investigated.

10 Drafting, audit and repair

The report is written from the record, then argued with

Drafting is not the end of the work. Every draft is audited against the record that produced it, and what the audit finds is acted on before anything is finalised.

The pipeline

Draft

A style is chosen and its sections are drafted from one collated copy of the whole record: an outline pass, a call per section, then a cohesion edit across the lot. The model is given the record and nothing else, and the findings, the categories and the confidence words all come from the record, so a section cannot claim more than the tests behind it support. Each section is corrected and finalised by hand, and nothing regenerates over a finalised one. This investigation was drafted through a model reached from the investigator's own machine; the endpoint is a setting, so the same pipeline runs against a local model, a hosted API with your own key, or an organisation's gateway. With AI access off the tab says so and the report is assembled from the record instead.

What the app recordedEach section with the model and the version of the record that produced it, so a draft goes visibly stale when the record moves on.

Audit, repair, then finalise

An adversarial pass then reads the finished draft back against the whole record and verdicts every claim in it, naming what it could not find and what it found contradicted. A mechanical check with no model in it runs beside it, comparing each factor's category and confidence word in the text against its recorded test, and the report's findings against the derived classification. On this investigation both earned their place, and they caught the same kind of thing: the confidence words drifting away from the record. The audit flagged six claims out of the hundred and fifty it verdicted, five of them a finding stated at a lower strength than the tests behind it support. The mechanical check went further on one of them and named exactly what had happened, that a bullet in the findings summary carried the factor's Influence confidence where its Existence confidence belonged. Nobody reading the draft would have noticed either. Both are caught by comparing the words against the record rather than by anyone noticing.

The repair pass takes only what those two flagged: it sends the section carrying a flagged item, with the record and the cited excerpt, under one rule, change what is flagged and nothing else, then re-audits the reply and keeps it only if the re-audit is clean or strictly better. Nothing lands on its own. A repaired section is shown beside the text it would replace and landed by hand, and it carries "repaired against the audit" in its provenance. The audit and the check are published with the reports below, because what the audit caught is the reason a draft is never final until an investigator has read it.

What the app recordedThe audit and the record check beside the draft, the review notes the model made about the record itself, which section each repair touched, and the investigator's decision to finalise.
