Case study, worked end to end

A grounding at night, from the first report to the published findings

Kanooka Heads is a passenger ferry that left a dredged channel in darkness and grounded on a sandbank with 38 people aboard. What follows is that investigation in the order it happened: what came in, what the investigator did with it, and what the application recorded at each step. Every picture is a real screen from that file. Click one to see it at full size.

The source documents the investigation worked from are published with it, so the analysis can be read back against the same material the investigator had.

Everything in this case study is fictional: the operator, the port, the vessel, the waterway, the people and every record. The documents at the end are published exactly as the application produced them, faults and all.

1 The occurrence as first notified

A ferry off the side of a dredged channel, in the dark, on a falling tide

This is what was known on the evening of 07/07/2026 and the morning after, before anybody had pulled a track or opened a manual. It is the brief the investigation started from.

Service TF18 was the last of the day, running from Muntari Island to the town wharf at Kanooka Heads with 38 passengers aboard. It was the first winter of a timetable that had moved that service from 16:15 to 17:35, so the transit of the Merrandi Channel was made in full darkness, 35 minutes after the end of civil twilight, with no moon.

The channel is dredged to 3.2 m and is 40 m wide. The vessel entered it on the charted centreline and held it. Ninety seconds past the No. 2 beacon the bridge chart plotter froze: the chart picture stayed on the screen, the vessel symbol stopped moving, and the display would not take a touch. The master did not stop. He went looking out of the window for the No. 4 starboard-hand beacon, which is the secondary means of position fixing named in the passage plan.

In the same second the heading began to ease five degrees to starboard, most of it while he was tapping at the frozen screen, and on his own account he never decided to make the turn. It took him 91 seconds to find the beacon, and the vessel crossed the channel's western edge 114 seconds after the freeze. When he saw the light it was dead ahead. Thinking he was just outside the edge, when he was nearly 40 m out, he eased her back to port rather than stop. The echo sounder's shallow alarm gave 16 seconds' warning, the master had both engines astern four seconds after it, and the vessel struck Boolarong Bank at 6.8 kt, 54 m outside the channel.

Nobody was seriously hurt. The starboard hull was holed and the vessel took water in one void. The master stopped the engines, refused to back a holed vessel off a bank on a falling tide, got lifejackets onto everyone and had all 38 passengers ashore by workboat within 74 minutes.

His own report, written from the vessel forty minutes later under the company's just culture scheme, was in the safety system before the operator's own notification was raised, and it is what opened this investigation. It put two things on the record before anyone asked. He had been told about the plotter at the handover and had not written it down. And the No. 4 beacon had been getting harder to see for two years, the masters had all said so to each other, and none of them had ever written it down.

2 What came in first

The first material, filed before anyone had a theory

In the days after the grounding the two safety reports, the notification and the first records arrive. They go into the file as they are, and the checklist starts the work that follows from them.

The first material

Take the safety reports in as they were written

SR-2026-0288 was submitted by the master at 18:52 local, from the vessel, while the workboat was still running passengers ashore. It goes in as written, with the submitter recorded by role, the time, and the reference in the reporting system. No name goes into the file.

One tick says the report came in under the company's just culture scheme. From then on report drafting is told the account may inform the investigation and must not be quoted into a distributed document, and the audit, the application's model check against the record, looks for any place it was.

A second report, SR-2026-0291, arrived three days later from the deckhand. It is not about the grounding at all. It is about the lifejacket locker in the forward saloon, which she had to reach by squeezing behind the kiosk counter while handing jackets out. Nobody had asked her about it. It became a finding.

What the app recordedEach account verbatim, its reference and time, the submitter's role, and the protection it was given.

File the material once, and link it from what rests on it

Seventeen items go into Captured material over the following fortnight: the notification, the shore AIS track, the engine and steering recorder, the plotter's own event log and its bench test, the echo sounder alarm log, both VHF channels and the bridge camera audio, the tide gauge record and the weather, the aids to navigation code, the port services agreement, two operations manuals, the route risk assessment and the change record, the defect book, the survey, the stowage survey, the crew records, the Port Authority's register and the foreshore lighting survey.

Each carries what it is, where it came from, whether it is held verbatim or as a description, and one line on why it matters. Nothing is filed twice. Every object on the map, every timeline event and both safety reports link to the items they rest on, so a finding can be followed back to the document that produced it and a document can be asked what is resting on it.

What the app recordedEach item once, with its category, its reference, its record type and its significance, and every link from the material that uses it.

Work the checklist, and keep the open questions where they can be seen

The checklist is the investigation's own procedure, worked question by question and answered yes, no or not applicable. Twelve of them are still answered no when the standard report is drafted, because they are honestly no: two objects are not finalised, the preliminary update has not gone out, nobody outside the investigation has read the draft, and the investigation cannot be closed.

The to-do list sits beside it. Seven items, five of them still open: the firmware fix has not been proved to be the fix, nobody has said what it would take to fund the sightline work, the operator has not answered whether a second person belongs on the bridge at night, only two of the five masters on the route have been asked whether the plotter has ever frozen on them, and the master has not yet been asked whether he identified the No. 4 beacon before passing No. 1, as the operator's passage planning manual requires. Two of the seven were raised from an object's own form and link straight back to it.

What the app recordedEvery checklist answer, the to-do list with what raised each item, the distribution list, and a running log of the investigation's own actions.

3 The evidence pack

Nine documents, and what each one settled

By the end of the gathering phase the investigation held nine source documents. They are published here as one pack, so everything below can be read against the same material the investigator had.

Safety reports, master and deckhand

The master's account, written from the vessel forty minutes after the grounding under the just culture scheme, and the deckhand's, submitted three days later about the lifejacket locker. The first opened the investigation. The second added a finding nobody had gone looking for. Both are protected, so they inform the work without being quoted into anything distributed.

Occurrence notification

The operator's own notification: what happened, where, the damage, the injuries, the risk rating and the actions taken that night. It fixed the agreed facts and seeded the first timeline events.

Voyage data, AIS track and equipment logs

The shore AIS receiver's track of the transit second by second, the engine and steering recorder, and the chart plotter's and echo sounder's own logs. It fixed the moment the vessel left the centreline, showed the set west as a held heading change rather than a current, and closed the steering failure hypothesis: every parameter was normal until the impact.

VHF and bridge audio extract

Both VHF channels and the vessel's own bridge camera audio, from the departure call to the last passenger ashore. It is the evidence behind the plotter freeze, behind the 91 seconds between "Where's four" and "There's four", and behind the decision not to refloat.

Tide, weather and light

The tide predictions and the port's own gauge record, the weather observations, and the light and moon data. It established the transit in full darkness, and it excluded the tide hypothesis: the gauge was within 0.03 m of prediction at the moment of the grounding.

Operator and port procedure extracts

The aids to navigation code, the port services agreement, the passage planning and defect reporting manuals, the route risk assessment and the change record. Everything in the external, organisational and risk control lanes rests on these seven pages.

Vessel, port and crewing records

The defect book and the maintenance system, the post-grounding survey, the lifejacket stowage survey, the crew and roster records, and the Port Authority's aids to navigation register with the range and visibility check and the foreshore lighting survey. The screened beacon, the halved range and the three deferred works rounds come from here.

Interview transcript, master

The master's account in his own words, taken once the records were in hand. It is what turned the beacon from a complaint in a tearoom into a factor that could be tested, and it is where he explains a five degree alteration he says he never decided to make.

Interview notes, deckhand and outgoing master

Two interviews that were not recorded, one at the interviewee's request and one by telephone. The investigator's notes are the record of both. The deckhand's gave the lifejacket finding and the question of who keeps a lookout; the outgoing master's gave the other half of the handover.

4 Interviews, and 5 the timeline

Three accounts, two of them without a recording, and the sequence they have to fit

The master, the deckhand and the master who had the vessel that morning were all interviewed once the records were in hand, so the questions could be about what the records did not say. The sequence of events is then built from everything at once.

The interviews

Import the one transcript, write up the other two

Three interviews, each on its own sub-tab. Only one was recorded. The master's was conducted over Teams and the transcript came in as the .vtt download, imported straight into the record; a .docx download, or a pasted transcript, import the same way.

De-identification is built into the import. Every speaker in a transcript is mapped to a role, and it is the role, never the name, that reaches the record, the collated export or any drafting model. On this file the speakers are lead investigator, second investigator and master, and that is all anyone downstream ever sees.

The other two were not recorded, one because the deckhand asked that it not be and one because it was taken by telephone. There the investigator's own three note windows are the record: free recall, additional questions, follow-up questions. Nothing pretends to be a transcript, and the collated export tells a drafting model which it is looking at.

Two of the three records are finalised. The deckhand's is deliberately not, because her question about who keeps a lookout is still out with the operator and her answer may have to be put to her again. An unfinalised record is not hidden from drafting; drafting is told to treat it as a working draft rather than a settled account.

What the app recordedThe corrected transcript where there was one, the role for every speaker, the three note windows, the investigator's notes beside them, and whether the account has been finalised.

The timeline

Build the sequence, and say what each time rests on

The timeline runs from the change of master at the town wharf at 13:05, five hours before anything went wrong, through to the last passenger stepping off the workboat. It mixes sources: the shore AIS gives the track to the second, the bridge camera gives the words, the plotter's own log gives the freeze, and the port's VHF recording gives the response.

The handover is on it for a reason. It is where the fault the vessel grounded with was passed from one man to another and written down by neither, and putting it on the same line as the grounding is what makes that visible rather than something buried in a manual finding.

Each event carries its source and its time basis, so the sequence can be defended when two records disagree about when something happened. The instruments stamp in UTC, so each of their events keeps the time its recorder wrote and carries its +10:00 correction to the local working clock in the open. And one pair disagreed: the plotter and echo sounder clocks ran two seconds fast against the AIS receiver, and that correction is recorded rather than quietly applied.

What the app recordedThe ordered events, the evidence behind each one, the time basis it rests on, and which event is the critical one.

6 Building the map

The analysis, built one stage at a time

The map is the analysis. It starts with the occurrence itself, fills with everything that might have played a part, and is then cut down by testing rather than by opinion. These are three real states of this investigation's own file.

Three states of the same map

Place the critical event, and nothing else

One object goes down first: Grounding on Boolarong Bank with 38 passengers embarked, in the Occurrence Events lane at the bottom right, with what happened and the proof of its significance recorded against it.

Everything else on the map exists to explain this one object. Putting it down first is what stops the analysis drifting into everything that was ever wrong with the operation or with the port.

What the app recordedThe critical event as an object, in its lane, at its point in time.

Put every hypothesis up, including the ones you expect to fail

Twenty more objects go up, scattered roughly where they belong in lane and in time. The occurrence sequence: the vessel left the dredged channel on the bank side, and the shallow alarm, at its 2019 setting, gave sixteen seconds' warning. The people on the night: the plotter fault passed on verbally at the change of master and not entered in the defect book, the heading eased five degrees to starboard without a decision to turn, the transit continued at eight knots from the plotter freeze until the shallow alarm, and the engines stopped with no attempt made to refloat on a falling tide.

The conditions they worked in: the No. 4 beacon screened by mangroves from the channel between 0.84 and 0.34 nm north of it, unshielded boat ramp floodlights aligned behind it from the north, the chart plotter frozen, and a master who may have been fatigued. Above them the controls and the organisations: no night passage plan or hold point for loss of the primary fix, a defect procedure that never requires a fault that cleared itself to be recorded, a forward saloon lifejacket locker blocked by a counter fitted without re-survey, a beacon whose range was halved by a solar conversion with no notice to anybody using the channel, a sightline clearing deferred unfunded for three works rounds, the last service moved into darkness without reopening the daylight-only risk assessment, a darkness transit rostered to close a split duty within 20 minutes of the spread limit, and an aids code that sets no notice or availability duty for beacons off declared channels.

Two of them are up there to be disproved: Steering or propulsion failure before the vessel left the centreline and Tide set down below prediction over Boolarong Bank. Nothing is filtered at this point. The method works by testing, not by intuition, and a hypothesis that is later excluded still strengthens the record.

What the app recordedEach hypothesis as an untested object, drawn with a dashed white border until someone has tested it.

Test as the evidence arrives, and let the arrows follow

Nine objects have been tested here. The occurrence sequence is joined up, off track to the late alarm to the grounding, and the beacon chain has started to form above it, from the deferred sightline clearing to a beacon screened from the channel. The code's scope and the solar conversion have both been argued and failed Influence, so they sit above it with no arrows, and the defect procedure has passed its argued Influence test at 75 per cent while the plotter it leads to waits on its bench test. Both hypotheses raised to be disproved have failed Existence and are struck through on the map, with the reason on the record.

An investigation does not arrive in order. Here the beacon chain was tested first because the range and visibility check had to be done at night, from a boat, before anyone touched the mangroves; the defect chain waited on a bench test that took three weeks and then found nothing. Objects are moved, tested, connected and reconnected as that happens, and the map is never more than the current state of the evidence.

Every arrow is a claim that one thing led to another. It stays grey and dashed until the object it comes from has passed its tests, and turns green when it has, so the picture builds itself as the work is done.

What the app recordedEvery object and every connection, with the state of each test behind them.

7 Testing a factor

Nothing is a finding until it has passed its tests

Three questions are asked of every object on the map, in order, and a confidence is recorded for each answer. The test results determine how the object is classified, while the investigator remains responsible for the judgements behind those results.

Existence

Did this event or condition actually exist?

If it did, the border turns solid and the next question is asked. If it did not, the hypothesis is excluded: it stays on the map, struck through, with the reason recorded.

PassFailInconclusive
Influence

Did it influence anything else, and if so in what way?

If it did, it is a contributing safety factor and its outgoing arrows become proven causal paths. Where the evidence cuts both ways, the test is argued with evidence for and against.

PassFailArgued
Importance

If it did not influence anything, is it still important enough to raise in the report?

A factor that played no part in this occurrence may still be a risk worth addressing. If so, it is a finding in its own right, an other risk factor, and it carries no arrows.

PassFail

The three questions, on one object

Ask them of the moment the primary fix was lost

The object is Chart plotter display froze during the channel transit and did not recover. The plotter's own event log records the display process not responding at 18:07:06 with no reset, and the same cause code twice that morning, when the unit recovered itself. A 46 hour bench test afterwards found no defect and could not make it happen again. The contractor identified the code as a known fault with a firmware fix issued in 11/2024, which this unit had never had applied.

Did it exist? Yes, at 100 per cent: three independent records place the freeze at 18:07:06, the unit's own log, the bridge audio to the second and the master's account. The bench test goes to what caused the freeze, not to whether it happened, so that doubt sits in the to-do list against the object rather than in its Existence grade. Did it influence the occurrence? Yes, at 95 per cent. The vessel held the centreline while the plotter worked, the heading change began at the second of the freeze, and the master's search for the beacon began 85 seconds after it, with no position fix of any kind in between.

The corner dot says how far the object's own investigation has got: amber while it is still being worked, green when the investigator has finished with it. Nineteen of the twenty-one objects on this map are green, and two were deliberately left amber.

What the app recordedEach verdict with its confidence and its evidence, the investigator's reasoning, and the standard of proof the investigation set. The form warns when a pass falls short of it.

Argue the one the evidence cuts both ways on

In the Organisational Influences lane is Beacon's range halved by solar conversion with no notice to channel users. Eight months before the occurrence the No. 4 beacon was converted from mains to a solar lantern and its nominal range cut from 6 nautical miles to 3. No local notice went out, no operator was told, and the company's passage plan still gave the beacon at 6 M on the night. Of everything in the file it is the finding everybody reaches for first.

For: the master called the beacon "a nothing" against the town and said it used to be a proper light, and for at least the last 37 seconds of his search it was clear of the islet and not yet seen, which is when a weaker light against a bright background is harder to pick up. Against: over the half mile in which the vessel left the channel the beacon was screened by the canopy, and a screened light is not made visible by being brighter. The master already knew the light was weaker, so a notice would have told him nothing he did not know, and the search ran from 0.47 to 0.25 nm, well inside even the measured range of 2.6 nm.

Influence was argued and failed at 20 per cent, well short of the investigation's standard of proof, the application's default of 70 per cent. The one mechanism left, the light's conspicuity against the floodlights once it cleared the islet, belongs to the floodlight object and stays open there. The conversion is still a finding: Importance passed at 85 per cent, because a change to an aid on a 96-passenger route reached no user of the channel, so the report carries it as an other risk factor rather than a cause.

What the app recordedBoth sides of the argument as separate evidence, the confidence the investigator settled on after weighing them, and the reasoning for that number.

8 The finished map

The whole investigation in one image

Twenty-one objects and fourteen arrows. Left to right is time, bottom to top is organisational depth, and the critical event sits at the bottom right where every chain arrives.

What the map says

Three chains converge. A beacon chain runs from the deferred sightline clearing to a beacon screened by mangroves over most of the stretch where the master was looking for it, and from there to a heading that eased five degrees to starboard without a decision to turn. A defect chain runs from a procedure that never requires a fault that cleared itself to be recorded to a plotter that froze with a known fix nobody had applied, and from the plotter both to the heading change and to the transit continuing at eight knots. A short timetable chain runs from a change record approved one level too low to a night transit with no plan for it, and on to the same continued transit. Through the vessel leaving the channel and the late alarm, all three arrive at the grounding.

A green arrow also runs from the positive event, the engines stopped with no attempt made to refloat, because that changed the outcome as well. The five orange objects carry no arrows at all, among them the aids code, the solar conversion and the omission at the handover, the three most readers reach for first. They failed Influence, so there is no proven causal path to draw and an arrow would be a claim the evidence does not support. They stay on the map because they are findings in their own right. The master's fatigue carries no arrow either: its Existence was argued and left inconclusive at 50 per cent, and a factor that may not have existed points at nothing.

The one unproven arrow

One arrow is grey and dashed: the claim that the unshielded boat ramp floodlights behind the beacon are part of why the held heading went unnoticed. Fourteen unshielded floodlights went in ten days before the winter timetable began, and from half a mile north they sit behind the beacon within four degrees of the same bearing. Both masters interviewed described the same thing in almost the same words, and for at least the last 37 seconds before the master called the beacon it was clear of the islet and not seen. That shows a condition, and a window in which it could have mattered. It does not show an effect on detection.

Over most of the search the beacon was screened by mangroves and would not have been visible against a black sky either, and the heading change began at the plotter, before the search, so the floodlights cannot have caused the turn. Existence passed at 100 per cent, because the floodlights were measured, but Influence was argued and left inconclusive at 45 per cent, so the arrow cannot be drawn as proven. The application draws it grey and dashed rather than dropping it, because an open line of enquiry is part of the record and the report has to say that it is open.

  • Blue, solid border: a contributing safety factor. It existed and it influenced the occurrence.
  • Orange: an other risk factor. It existed, it did not influence this occurrence, and it is still worth raising.
  • Struck through, dashed: an excluded hypothesis. It did not exist, and the reason is on the record.
  • Green, rounded: a positive event, something that reduced the risk or limited the outcome.
  • Red, rounded: the critical event, the occurrence itself.
  • White, dashed: not yet tested, or a test left inconclusive.
  • Green arrow: a proven causal path. The object it comes from has passed Influence.
  • Grey dashed arrow: a causal path still under testing.
  • Green dot: the object's investigation is complete and its evidence finalised.
  • Amber dot: still being investigated.

10 Drafting, audit and repair

The report is written from the record, then argued with

Drafting is not the end of the work. Every draft is audited against the record that produced it, and what the audit finds is acted on before anything is finalised.

The pipeline

Draft

A style is chosen and its sections are drafted from one collated copy of the whole record: an outline pass, a call per section, then a cohesion edit across the lot. The model drafts from the investigation record under instructions not to go beyond what the tests support, with the findings, the categories and the confidence words taken from that record. The checks that follow compare the draft back against the record before the investigator reviews it. Each section is corrected and finalised by hand, and nothing regenerates over a finalised one. This investigation was drafted through a model reached from the investigator's own machine; the endpoint is a setting, so the same pipeline runs against a local model, a hosted API with your own key, or an organisation's gateway. With AI access off the tab says so and the report is assembled from the record instead. The drafts published here were produced by a Claude-based pipeline run through the suite's own sealed command-line transport, standing in for the kind of private cloud endpoint an organisation would use.

What the app recordedEach section with the model and the version of the record that produced it, so a draft goes visibly stale when the record moves on.

Audit, repair, then finalise

The audit, a second model pass, then reads the finished draft back against the whole record and gives a verdict on the claims it identifies, naming what it could not find and what it found contradicted. It is the application's own model check rather than an independent review, and like any model output it can miss things. A mechanical check with no model in it runs beside it, comparing each factor's category and confidence word in the text against its recorded test, and the report's findings against the derived classification. The repair pass takes only what those two flagged: it sends the section carrying a flagged item, with the record and the cited excerpt, under one rule, change what is flagged and nothing else, then re-audits the reply and keeps it only if the re-audit is clean or strictly better. Nothing lands on its own. A repaired section is shown beside the text it would replace and landed by hand, and it carries "repaired against the audit" in its provenance.

The model also writes review notes as it drafts: contradictions, gaps and inconsistencies it finds in the record itself rather than in its own text. Those are published below with the audits, because on this investigation they caught things worth catching, and because what the audit finds is the reason a draft is never final until an investigator has read it.
