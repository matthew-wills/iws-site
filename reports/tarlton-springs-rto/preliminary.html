The report as drafted

Summary

On 18/06/2026, Saltbush Air SB512, a twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, was taking off from runway 09 at Tarlton Springs Aerodrome after dark with 44 passengers, 2 flight crew and 1 cabin crew on board. Two kangaroos moved onto the runway, the crew rejected the take-off, the aircraft struck the animals, and it came to rest 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area. One cabin crew member reported bruising during the disembarkation and there were no serious injuries, but the aircraft was substantially damaged, two kangaroos were killed and the aerodrome closed for 4 hours 20 minutes. The investigation is examining how the animals reached the runway, the operator's arrangements for flying there after dark, the decision to reject the take-off and the stopping performance, and the question of the runway edge lighting remains open. The evening service is suspended, a runway inspection is now required before every departure there after last light, and the aerodrome operator has installed a temporary electric wire and programmed a fence repair.

What we know

On 18/06/2026, Saltbush Air SB512, a 50-seat twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, was the last scheduled sector of the day from Tarlton Springs Aerodrome. On board were 44 passengers, 2 flight crew and 1 cabin crew. It was the fifth sector of the crew's duty, added that morning when the rostered crew reported unfit. The doors closed at 07:16 UTC (17:16 local), 40 minutes behind schedule. The aerodrome was in darkness. The only runway inspection that day had been carried out before the first scheduled arrival, and it recorded the movement area as clear.

At 07:29:10 the tower cleared SB512 for take-off on runway 09 and passed the wind as 090 at 6 kt. Neither the tower nor the crew raised a runway inspection. The first officer was flying. Take-off power was set at 07:30:02. Both engines were matched and no caution or warning was recorded. The recorded wind gave a headwind of 4 to 6 kt throughout the roll.

The pilot in command called V1 at 07:30:29, at the calculated 112 kt. Two seconds later the first officer called "Roos - roos, roos, on the left". Two kangaroos were moving onto the runway from the northern side, past the 1,100 m point, at an estimated range at first sight of around 200 m. At 07:30:32 the pilot in command said "Stopping. I have control." and rejected the take-off at 118 kt, six knots above V1, with 695 m of sealed runway remaining. About one second later the aircraft struck the animals. The occurrence notification records that both animals were struck. The pilot in command's safety report records at least one. Two carcasses were later recovered, at 1,188 m and 1,221 m from the threshold. Maximum braking was established two seconds after the decision. Reverse was obtained on No. 1 only.

The aircraft left the end of runway 09 at approximately 21 kt at 07:30:51. It came to rest at 07:30:57, 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area, upright and on the extended centreline, 28 m short of the end of the prepared area. There was no fire, and no evacuation was commanded. At the pilot in command's request, the tower activated the aerodrome emergency plan at 07:32:10 and a vehicle attended. Passengers left by the forward airstair. The notification records this as 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped. The airstair is heard operating at 07:34:40.

There were no serious injuries. One cabin crew member reported bruising during the disembarkation, and two kangaroos were killed. The aircraft was substantially damaged:

  • three No. 2 propeller blades were beyond repairable limits
  • the right main gear doors were distorted
  • both right main tyres had deflated through the fuse plugs
  • the lower right fuselage had two punctures.

The aerodrome closed for 4 hours 20 minutes. The evening Tarlton Springs service is suspended pending the outcome of the investigation.

What we are looking at

  1. How the animals reached the runway. The investigation is examining the northern perimeter fence, its repair history and the aerodrome's wildlife arrangements. The fence was recorded as damaged in 04/2023, with animals seen passing through. Its repair was deferred at three budget rounds. An animal track through it was found at the 1,050 m mark, on the same side of the runway as both carcasses. The aerodrome falls below the size threshold in AS-139 10.4.2 that would require a documented wildlife plan.
  1. The operator's arrangements for flying after dark at Tarlton Springs. The investigation is examining how the evening service was approved under CMR-2025-118. The current route risk assessment, RRA-041 issue 3, carries a daylight-only restriction, and that assessment was not reopened. The investigation is also examining why the operator's runway inspection requirement did not apply. That requirement applies only at aerodromes in Appendix 8A, and an aerodrome is listed only after a reported wildlife strike there. The aerodrome's own sighting log of kangaroos on the runway was not passed to the operator.
  1. The decision to reject the take-off above V1 (the speed after which the procedure is to continue). This decision is being examined against the operator's procedures and recurrent training. Both crew members said their rejected take-off training has covered only aircraft system failures. The syllabus contains no scenario involving an obstruction on the runway.
  1. The stopping performance. The investigation is examining the deceleration achieved against the scheduled figures. That includes the loss of No. 2 reverse thrust when the propeller struck the animals.
  1. Runway edge lighting. Whether the brightness of the runway edge lights affected the crew's ability to see the animals has been examined. The available evidence could not resolve it, and it remains open.
  1. Wider matters found during the investigation. These are being examined separately from the sequence of events. The first is the duty pattern on the evening rotation, which places the last sector late in a long duty. The second is the cabin floor emergency lighting, which was found partly dark after the occurrence. Engineering is checking whether the same component is affected across the fleet.

Containment and next steps

Interim safety action

  • Evening service suspended. On 19/06/2026 the operator's Head of Flying Operations suspended the evening Tarlton Springs service. The suspension remains in place pending the outcome of the investigation.
  • Runway inspection now required at Tarlton Springs. On 26/06/2026 the Head of Flying Operations issued temporary revision TR-2026-04, which adds Tarlton Springs to Appendix 8A of the Operations Manual. A runway inspection must now be requested before every departure there after last light. The take-off roll may not begin until the result has been passed. This is in place until a permanent amendment is made.
  • Perimeter fence. The aerodrome operator installed a temporary electric wire on 25/06/2026. It has funded the northern boundary fence repair from its 2026 contingency and programmed replacement of the 140 m section for 09/2026. This work is still being implemented. [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether the fence replacement programmed for 09/2026 has been completed]
  • Recommendation to the operator. The investigation has recommended that the operator:
  • list aerodromes in Appendix 8A on wildlife exposure rather than on a reported strike
  • require a route risk assessment for any aerodrome first operated into after last light.

A response was due by 30/09/2026. [NOT ESTABLISHED: the operator's response]

No interim action has been recorded on the cabin floor emergency lighting or on rejected take-off training.

Next steps

Engineering is establishing whether the same lighting component is affected across the fleet. The runway edge lighting question remains open. Before the final report is issued, the draft will be read by someone outside the investigation. The people and organisations it concerns will also be given the chance to correct matters of fact. Each safety action is still to be given an owner and a date. [NOT ESTABLISHED: expected date of the final report]

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

  • [What we know] - Take-off roll timeline entry: the draft outline states the roll began "five minutes after the end of civil twilight". No source establishes the time of last light or civil twilight, so this was omitted.
  • Aircraft strike timeline entry: the draft outline says the No. 2 propeller was "destroyed". The inspection records three blades damaged beyond repairable limits with one tip separation, so the source wording was used.
  • Disembarkation timeline entry: this is recorded as 07:34:40 to 07:38:48 (4 m 08 s). The notification gives 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped (07:30:57), which would be about 07:35:05. The draft outline's "clear of the aircraft four minutes after it stopped" fits neither reading. The three timings need reconciling.
  • Strike on both animals: the notification and inspection record that both animals were struck. The pilot in command's safety report records "at least one".
  • Departure speed: the notification gives approximately 21 kt. The FDR gives IAS 21 and groundspeed 20 at 07:30:51. The timeline entry drops "approximately".
  • Pilot in command duty: the duty record gives sign-on at 06:30 local. The interview background gives a duty that "began 05:10 local".
  • Pilot in command training: the crew record gives the last cyclic simulator as 11/03/2026. The interview background gives "Recurrent simulator check, February 2026".
  • Daily movement area inspection timeline entry: the supporting extract attached to it is the wildlife sighting log (01/2025 to 06/2026), not the daily inspection log cited as its source.
  • [What we are looking at] - Perimeter fence repair deferred / Two kangaroos entered the runway strip: the distance from the fence track at 1,050 m to the first carcass is given in three ways. The fence evidence says 110 m, while the kangaroo evidence and the photograph rationale say 138 m (1,188 minus 1,050). The fence significance line says "140 m apart". Only 138 m is consistent with the recorded positions.
  • Fifth sector, 11 hours into duty: the field labelled "Evidence against influence" opens with "For influence:" and holds argument on both sides. The "Evidence for influence" field holds an Importance-style statement rather than an influence argument. The labels appear to be crossed.
  • Cabin floor proximity emergency lighting: this factor appears twice. It is listed under organisational safety issues and again under other risk factors, and the draft report's category list labels it "Safety issue". It should be confirmed which heading it sits under.
  • Several factors end with "Led to: Runway overrun onto the runway end safety area", but there is no entry for that event.
  • [Containment and next steps] - Perimeter fence repair deferred for three budget cycles: the Evidence gives the animal pad as 110 m before the first carcass, and the Significance gives the pad and carcasses as 140 m apart. The photograph note and the kangaroo object give 138 m, which matches 1,188 m less 1,050 m. Three values are recorded for one distance.
  • Evening service approved as a schedule change: the collation grades Influence "Pass - likely" with no figure, but the report scaffold gives 85%. The Documented wildlife plan object is "likely" at 70% and the training object is "likely" at 75%. Please confirm the intended grade.
  • Recommendation on the Appendix 8A listing criterion: the status is still Open, but the response was due 30/09/2026 and the generation date is 02/10/2026. Please confirm whether a response has been received.
  • TR-2026-04 is recorded as Closed although it is a temporary revision "pending the permanent change". The permanent amendment has no owner, date or status recorded.
  • [Summary] - Summary outline, "What happened": the date is given as 17/06/2026, but the critical event, the notification and every other entry give the occurrence date as 18/06/2026. The draft uses 18/06/2026.
  • "Perimeter fence repair deferred for three budget cycles": the evidence places the animal pad at 1,050 m as "110 m before the point at which the first carcass was recovered", and its significance line says the pad and carcasses "are 140 m apart". The carcass at 1,188 m is 138 m from the 1,050 m mark, which matches the photograph entry and the "Two kangaroos entered the runway strip" entry. The 110 m and 140 m figures appear inconsistent with that.
  • Summary outline, "Safety issues": the cabin floor proximity emergency lighting is shown with the term "unlikely". That is its failed influence grading, not its importance grading ("very likely"), so the term next to a safety issue may mislead a reader.
  • Summary outline, "Contributing factors": the terms "extremely likely" on the kangaroo-entry and reject-above-V1 factors have not been narrowed to "very likely" as the findings scale requires.
  • The style calls for the report to open with the italic line "Preliminary update - the information in this report is based on the evidence available at the time of writing and may change as the investigation proceeds." It is not in the approved sections. If the Summary is the first section, the investigator may wish to place that line above it.
