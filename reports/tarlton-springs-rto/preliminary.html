Summary

Preliminary update - the information in this report is based on the evidence available at the time of writing and may change as the investigation proceeds.

On 18/06/2026 Saltbush Air SB512, a 50-seat twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, rejected its take-off on runway 09 at Tarlton Springs Aerodrome after two kangaroos were seen on the runway. It struck both animals, left the end of the runway at approximately 21 kt and stopped 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area, upright and on the extended centreline. Of the 44 passengers, 2 flight crew and 1 cabin crew on board, none was seriously injured and one cabin crew member reported bruising. The aircraft sustained substantial damage and the aerodrome closed for 4 hours 20 minutes. The investigation is continuing and has made no findings, and the operator has suspended its evening service to Tarlton Springs.

What we know

Saltbush Air flight SB512, a 50-seat twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, was the fifth sector of the crew's duty on 18/06/2026. It was to depart runway 09 at Tarlton Springs Aerodrome for Port Carrick with 44 passengers, 2 flight crew and 1 cabin crew. Times are local time (UTC+10). Tarlton Springs has no control tower, so the crew made their own broadcasts. The aerodrome's only runway inspection that day was at 07:35, before the first arrival, and its result was "movement area clear". Sunset was at 16:58, civil twilight ended at 17:25, and moonrise was not until 22:41, with the moon 14% illuminated. The crew switched on the pilot-activated runway lighting at 17:21:50. The wind was 090 at 6 kt on the common traffic frequency, and the recorded headwind component was 4 to 6 kt throughout the roll.

The doors closed at 17:16, and the aircraft was due off blocks at 17:20. The take-off roll began at 17:30:02 with the first officer as pilot flying, and both engines were at 98.4% and 98.5% torque. The pilot in command called V1 at 112 kt at 17:30:29. Two seconds later the first officer called, "Roos - roos, roos, on the left, they're on the -", and the pilot in command replied, "Stopping. I have control." (Figure 5). The flight data recorder showed the take-off was rejected at 118 kt, 6 kt and 3 seconds past V1, with 695 m of sealed runway remaining.

At 17:30:33 the aircraft struck both animals at 114 kt, and the right main gear and No. 2 propeller took the impact. Maximum braking was established at 17:30:34, 2 seconds after the decision, against a fleet mean of 3.2 seconds. Reverse was selected on No. 1 only at 17:30:36 because No. 2 reverse was unavailable. The aircraft left the sealed surface at approximately 21 kt at 17:30:51 and stopped at 17:30:57, 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area and 28 m short of the end of the prepared area. It was upright and on the extended centreline, and there was no fire.

The stopping distance achieved from 118 kt was 757 m. The scheduled accelerate-stop distance was 1,780 m against an ASDA of 1,847 m, a margin of 67 m. Mean deceleration from maximum braking was 2.6 m/s2, approximately 15% below the value used in the certified data. Two kangaroo carcasses were recovered at 1,188 m and 1,221 m from the threshold, and continuous tyre marks ran from 1,160 m to the runway end (Figure 3).

The aerodrome emergency plan was activated at 17:32:10 at the pilot in command's request. No evacuation was commanded, and the passengers disembarked by the forward airstair between 17:34:40 and 17:38:48. There were no serious injuries, and one cabin crew member reported bruising during the disembarkation. Both flight crew were drug and alcohol tested, and both results were negative. The pilot in command had 8,410 hours, 2,140 of them on type, and the first officer had 1,655 hours, 405 of them on type.

The aircraft sustained substantial damage. Three No. 2 propeller blades were beyond repairable limits, and the engine required a shock-load inspection. The right inboard flap, the wing lower surface aft of the No. 2 nacelle, the right main gear doors and the lower right fuselage were also damaged. Both right main tyres deflated through the fuse plugs, and brake energy reached 84% of the certified maximum. The aerodrome was closed for 4 hours 20 minutes.

What we are looking at

The following lines of enquiry are open.

Wildlife and the aerodrome's arrangements. The aerodrome's sighting log for 01/01/2025 to 18/06/2026 has 46 entries, 41 of them kangaroos. Of those 41, 38 are timed between one hour before and two hours after last light, and 11 record animals on the sealed surface. Works register item WR-221 (04/2023) records a northern boundary fence section with animals observed passing through. It was deferred at three budget rounds, and a site inspection on 23/06/2026 found a 140 m gap with an animal pad at the 1,050 m mark (Figure 2). [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether a replacement fence would exclude kangaroos]. Aerodrome Standard AS-139 10.4 requires a documented wildlife plan only above 27,000 kg or 350,000 passenger movements a year. Tarlton Springs' heaviest scheduled aeroplane is 22,900 kg and it handled 61,400 passenger movements in 2025, so the aerodrome is not in breach.

The operator's runway inspection procedure. Operations Manual Part A 8.14.2 requires an inspection request before the first departure after last light only at aerodromes listed in Appendix 8A (Figure 1). Listing needs a reported company wildlife strike within 24 months. Nine aerodromes are listed and Tarlton Springs is not. No inspection was requested or offered on the day, and the reporting officer confirmed one would have been carried out if asked.

The evening service and darkness. RRA-041 issue 3 carries the restriction "daylight operations only pending review of night lighting and diversion fuel policy". CMR-2025-118 (approved 22/05/2025) introduced the evening rotation from 14/07/2025, assessed it as a "schedule change only", and answered "No" to route risk assessment review required. The approver has left the company and cannot be interviewed. Four northern edge lights measured 118% to 131% of nominal intensity for stage 3 on 25/06/2026, seven days after the occurrence, and were likely at that intensity on the night. Whether this affected detection of the animals could not be settled.

The reject decision, training and procedure. Part B 4.6.4 reads "At or above V1 the take-off shall be continued." Part B 4.6 has no guidance on an obstruction on the runway at any speed (Figure 4). TRG-SYL-07 module 4 has four scripted scenarios, all aircraft system events, and no crew has been given an external obstruction scenario in the five cycles held. Performance assessment PEA-2026-0417 reconstructs a continued take-off, which did not happen, and it states what it did not assess.

Crew duty and rostering. The evening rotation was rostered on 289 days from 14/07/2025 to 18/06/2026, with planned duties of 12 hours 45 minutes to 13 hours 15 minutes against a 13 hour 30 minute maximum. On 18/06/2026 the crew took the rotation that morning after a rostered crew member reported unfit, in a duty planned at 13 hours 00 minutes, the sixth such reassignment. Whether the flight crew were fatigued at the time of the take-off could not be settled and is about as likely as not.

Cabin emergency lighting. A functional check on 24/06/2026 found two of six floor proximity escape path segments dark, traced to a failed common ballast. No evacuation was commanded and the cabin lights were on. Whether the same ballast part is fitted across the fleet is still with engineering.

A tailwind component during the roll and stop was very unlikely, as was an engine power loss before the reject decision.

Containment and next steps

Actions taken or under way

After the occurrence the aerodrome operator closed the aerodrome for 4 hours 20 minutes, the aircraft was quarantined at Tarlton Springs, and both flight crew were stood down from duty.

The Head of Flying Operations suspended the evening Tarlton Springs service after the occurrence. The notification records the suspension as "pending the outcome of this investigation". The suspension is in place.

On 26/06/2026 the operator amended Appendix 8A by temporary revision TR-2026-04 to list Tarlton Springs. The Part A 8.14.2 requirement for a runway inspection before departure after last light now applies to every departure there, pending the permanent change. This action is closed.

The aerodrome operator funded works register item WR-221 from the 2026 contingency. A temporary electric wire was installed on the northern boundary on 25/06/2026, and the 140 m northern boundary fence replacement is programmed for 09/2026. This action is in progress. Next steps

The flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder were removed by the operator's engineering representative and couriered to the operator's flight data section. Four reports have been issued so far: FDA-2026-0417-R1, ESR-2026-0417, PTR-2026-0417-02 and PEA-2026-0417.

Two further interviews are to be finalised. Engineering is checking whether the same ballast part, which failed in the cabin floor lighting on VH-SBQ, is fitted across the fleet.

Two matters remain open. These are whether the flight crew were fatigued at the time of the take-off, and whether four northern edge lights above nominal intensity affected the detection of the animals.

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

- [What we know] - Disembarkation timeline entry (07:34:40 to 07:38:48, "4 m 08 s"): the notification says passengers were disembarked 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped at 07:30:57, which would be about 07:35:05. The timeline instead runs 4 minutes 8 seconds from the airstair operating at 07:34:40, ending at 07:38:48, which is about 7 minutes 51 seconds after the stop. The two readings are incompatible. The draft states what each source records. - Scaffold line for 07:30:33 says the No. 2 propeller was "destroyed". The inspection report records three blades damaged beyond repairable limits, which is not the same thing. The draft follows the inspection report. - Scaffold line for 07:30:02 says the roll began "five minutes after the end of civil twilight". The source material refers only to "last light" (a causal-chain factor title) and to darkness. Civil twilight is not mentioned anywhere, so the draft says darkness only. - Scaffold line for 07:29:10 expands AWIS as "automatic weather broadcast". The source never gives that expansion, so the draft keeps AWIS as written. - Runway departure speed: the notification gives approximately 21 kt, while the FDR row at 07:30:51 gives 21 kt IAS and 20 kt groundspeed. The draft uses the notification's approximate figure. - Number of animals struck: the notification and inspection (two carcasses, tissue in two blade roots) support both being struck. The pilot in command's CVR remark and safety report refer to striking "at least one". The draft follows the physical evidence. - [What we are looking at] - Four northern edge lights above nominal intensity on the night: the factor carries "Led to: Take-off rejected 6 kt above V1", and the reject lists it under "Led from", yet its Influence test is Inconclusive (about as likely as not). An inconclusive factor carrying a causal link to the reject looks inconsistent with how the fatigue factor, also inconclusive, was deliberately left with no link. - Flight crew fatigued at the time of the take-off: the category is given as "Hypothesis (untested)", but the Existence test was argued and recorded as Inconclusive, and the Influence reasoning was set out in the test note. "Untested" does not match what was done. - No training or guidance for a runway obstruction during the take-off roll: Existence is recorded as "extremely likely". Under the reporting scale this narrows to "very likely" wherever it is graded in the final report. - [Containment and next steps] - Recommendation on the Appendix 8A listing criterion: the response was due 30/09/2026 and the status is still "Open". The current date is past that due date, so the record may not reflect whether a response has been received. - Fence safety action (WR-221): the replacement was programmed for 09/2026, but the status is still "In progress" as at 25/06/2026. The record does not show whether the replacement has been completed. - Investigation log, 19/06/2026: the evening Tarlton Springs service was suspended, but the record holds no later entry on whether it has resumed. TR-2026-04 is framed around departures there after last light, which suggests resumption may have been contemplated. The current status needs confirming. - Floor escape path lighting: this object is still in draft pending the fleet ballast answer from engineering, and no target date for that answer is recorded. - [Summary] - Summary outline "Date" line: it gives 17/06/2026, but the critical event, the notification and every factor place the occurrence on 18/06/2026 (07:30 UTC, 17:30 local). The draft uses 18/06/2026. The 17/06/2026 date appears to come from the morning inspection (21:35 UTC on 17/06/2026, 07:35 local on 18/06/2026) and should be corrected. - Summary outline "Safety issues" list: it carries the roster factor and the floor escape path lighting factor with the term "unlikely". That is their failed Influence grade. Their Importance grades are "likely" and "very likely". Taken out of context, the term misstates them as unlikely safety issues. - "Contributing factors" list: it tags the fence factor and the wildlife-plan threshold factor as safety issues, as well as the factors listed under safety issues. It also gives "extremely likely" for two factors without narrowing them to "very likely".
