Executive summary

At 02:47:12 on 12/08/2026 a 220 t class rigid [the other attendee 1] descending the main ramp of the Stage 4 open pit at Bindarra Gold Mine, empty, struck the right side of a light vehicle, LV-118, as it came out of the RL 312 dewatering spur. All times in this report are local time (UTC+8). The [the other attendee 1] had braked from 37 km/h and struck at 24 km/h, pushed LV-118 18.4 m down the ramp and rolled it onto its left side against the inner windrow. The light vehicle driver, a grade control technician, was seriously injured with a fractured left clavicle, three fractured left ribs and a small pneumothorax. The driver was extricated 27 minutes after the collision, treated at the surface medical facility and flown out at 07:40. LV-118 was destroyed, the Stage 4 pit stood down for 9 hours 15 minutes, and the main ramp below RL 340 was closed for 22 hours.

Several things worked. The [the other attendee 1] [the other attendee 1] braked and steered right within 1.4 seconds of the beacon appearing, which took about 58 per cent of the impact energy out and kept the push against the pit side windrow. The roll-over protection structure was deformed but not breached, the seatbelt was fastened, and dispatch stopped all Stage 4 traffic 49 seconds after the collision.

The spur was cut under works order WO-26-4471, classified as routine earthworks, so no management of change record was raised and none of its three referrals was made. That very likely led to the intersection having no sign, give-way or stop control although its 41 m sight line fell far short of the 96 m stopping distance, and to the collision awareness system being suppressed at the spur by the S4-LOAD geofence, of which operators knew nothing. The short sight line very likely contributed to neither driver being aware of the other until the [the other attendee 1] came over the crest. The traffic management plan required positive calls only at its nine Appendix B intersections and allowed additions only at the annual review, which very likely led to neither driver calling. The suppressed alert, with operators' belief that a quiet system meant an empty ramp, likely contributed to the same lack of awareness. The light vehicle driver slowed but did not stop at the spur mouth and had never done the Bindarra light vehicle pit driving assessment, because the gatehouse issued access cards without checking for it. The absence of a stop control or sign, and the unchecked assessment, each likely contributed to the driver leaving the spur without stopping.

The systemic safety issues are the review of the plan and the classification of works. Of 14 new Stage 4 roads since the last review, the 3 raised under a management of change record were referred to the plan's owner and none of the 11 raised under a works order was. The guideline MSG-14 set no trigger for reviewing the plan and no basis for the call list, and likely contributed to calls being added only at the annual review, a conclusion held at the standard of proof and no higher.

Immediate actions were a site-wide direction at 05:10 requiring a positive call before entering any Stage 4 intersection, listed or not, the barricading and closure of the spur, and the stand-down of the pit. Open recommendations, with responses due 30/09/2026, address a route for adding intersections between reviews, the works order exclusion, and geofence changes and [the other attendee 1] notification.

The extrication arrangement on night shift, which waited about 9 minutes for a second responder, is still being examined, as is the question of airborne dust. Whether the [the other attendee 1] [the other attendee 1] was fatigued could not be determined either way.

Analysis

This analysis follows the chain from the mine's documents to the two drivers' lack of awareness of each other, then to the collision, and then turns to the factors that did not contribute or could not be settled.

Organisation

The management of change procedure ORR-PRO-022 required that "A management of change record shall be raised before any of the following: a change to the mine's road network; the introduction of a new vehicle type; a change to a pit design; a change to a documented control." Clause 5.3 then provided that "Clause 5.2 does not apply to routine earthworks carried out under a works order within an approved pit design, where no new plant or vehicle type is introduced."

Works order WO-26-4471, raised on 28/07/2026, cut 240 m of access road to dewatering bore DW-14 at RL 312. It classified the work as routine earthworks within the approved Stage 4 design and recorded management of change as not required under clause 5.3. Its handover field read "N/A". The work therefore never reached the three referrals in clause 5.4: to the traffic management plan owner, to the survey department for the fleet management system road network, and to the contractor's technical services group for the collision awareness system geofences. Those referrals existed only inside a management of change record, and the works order form at Schedule 4 had no equivalent fields.

The consequences were specific. The in-cab road network published on 24/07/2026 stayed current and showed the main ramp with no intersection at RL 312. No survey checked the new intersection against the sight distance rule in clause 6.2. The suppression geofence S4-LOAD, carried up the ramp on 09/06/2026, already covered the place where the spur joined it, and nobody reviewed it against the new road.

This was a pattern and not a single lapse. Of the 14 new roads cut in Stage 4 since the review of 12/11/2025, the 3 raised under a management of change record were referred to the plan's owner and none of the 11 raised under a works order was. A referral is not in itself a control, and the plan's owner might have left the spur to the annual review, because no out-of-cycle review has been held in the plan's eleven year history. However, the referrals are standing parts of the procedure and the roads raised under it were referred. It is very likely that opening the spur under a works order, with nothing referred, led to the missing stop control and signs and to the geofence suppressing alerts at the spur.

The external guideline sat behind this as background. Clause 3.2 of MSG-14 required the plan to be reviewed "at intervals not exceeding twelve months, and following any significant change to the mine's road network, vehicle fleet or operating pattern", but defined no significant change and gave no example. Clause 5.1 required the mine to nominate the intersections at which positive communication was required, with no minimum and no stated basis. Under clause 3.5 the plan was a site document, not approved by the Inspectorate. Bindarra's plan met the guideline on its face on the day of the occurrence, because it had been reviewed inside twelve months and nominated nine interaction zones, and the mine was not in breach.

The guideline did not stop anyone from acting. A site wanting an out-of-cycle review could hold one under its own clause 9.1 at any time, and nothing prevented the mine listing every intersection. Bindarra did not act because nothing inside the mine put the question in front of the plan's owner, so on one reading the external clause is background only. Against that, leaving both terms to the same site document, reviewed once a year with no outside check, made doing nothing the default. The investigation concluded that the guideline likely contributed to the call list being changed only at the annual review, and that this conclusion is held at the standard of proof and no higher.

The access card process added a further gap. Clause 7.4 of ORR-STD-009 stated that equivalence of a contractor's general induction "does not extend to the Bindarra site-specific supplement, or to the Bindarra light vehicle pit driving assessment, which shall be completed at site by every person who will drive a vehicle in an operating pit and shall not be substituted". Clause 7.5 made the engaging department responsible for verifying this before a card was issued.

The gatehouse issued cards against the recognition agreement that Hallorn Survey & Geotechnical had held since 2023. It held no record of either item, was not asked for one, and its card system did not distinguish people who might drive in an operating pit from those who might not. The light vehicle driver's card was issued on 09/08/2026 after a 20 minute briefing on muster points, speed limits and personal protective equipment. The driver's training file recorded the Hallorn online module of 24/07/2026 and no pit driving assessment, and the driver had never seen BIN-TMP-004.

The assessment would have added how to approach a mouth that cannot be seen out of, which is to stop and look again rather than slow. The driver said they would have called if told to, but the plan required no call at an unlisted intersection, so that statement cannot be stretched to RL 312. The driver was also already applying the first rule the assessment teaches, which is to give way to heavy vehicles. It is likely that issuing the card without checking the assessment contributed to the light vehicle driving out of the spur without stopping.

Procedures

An intersection was added only where it appeared on the road layout drawing at Schedule 1, which was dated 12/11/2025 and did not show the spur.
