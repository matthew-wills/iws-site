Summary

Preliminary update - the information in this report is based on the evidence available at the time of writing and may change as the investigation proceeds.

At 02:47:12 on 12/08/2026 (times are local time, UTC+8), a 220 t class rigid haul truck (HT-407) descending the main ramp of the Stage 4 open pit at Bindarra Gold Mine, empty, struck a light vehicle (LV-118) coming out of the RL 312 dewatering spur. The light vehicle driver was seriously injured and flown to the metropolitan trauma centre at 07:40, and the light vehicle was destroyed. The Stage 4 pit stood down for 9 hours 15 minutes, and the occurrence was reported to the State Mines Inspectorate as a reportable injury (notification ORR-OCC-2026-0332). This update sets out what is known, what is being examined and what is being done now, including the closure of the spur, and it contains no findings.

What we know

The occurrence took place on 12/08/2026 on the main ramp of the Stage 4 open pit at Bindarra Gold Mine.

It was dark at the time, and the Stage 4 pit has no fixed lighting. No rain fell that night, the ramp surface was dry, the wind was south-east at 8 km/h and the temperature was 9 degrees.

HT-407, a 220 t class rigid haul truck, was returning down the main ramp empty to the Stage 4 loading face for its second load after the crib break. The light vehicle, LV-118, was on the RL 312 dewatering spur, a 240 m access road to bore DW-14. The driver was a grade control technician employed by a survey subcontractor, and had finished a pickup at the bore collar. At 02:46:21 the haul truck operator called at the RL 360 crossover. The pit channel recording shows no transmission by either vehicle about the spur.

At 02:47:12 the front left of the truck struck the right side of the light vehicle at 24 km/h, after the truck had braked from 37 km/h. The impact was 6.2 m into the ramp from the spur mouth. The light vehicle was pushed 18.4 m down the ramp and rolled onto its left side against the inner windrow. The truck stopped 19 m below the intersection at about 02:47:19.

The survey pickup of 13/08/2026 measured the geometry. The spur meets the ramp 34 m below a convex grade break that the crews call the crest. Available sight distance at the intersection is 41 m, and the beacon whip on a light vehicle approaching the spur mouth is first in view from the truck at 53 m. The stopping distance of a 220 t class truck empty at the posted 40 km/h on a 9 per cent descending grade is 96 m. Clause 6.2 of BIN-TMP-004 sets a minimum of 120 m.

The RL 312 spur is not one of the nine intersections at which BIN-TMP-004 requires a positive call. It is not on the plan's road layout drawing, and it was not on the in-cab road network last published on 24/07/2026. It had no warning sign, no give-way or stop control, no delineation and no posted speed.

The haul truck operator called the collision in, stayed in the cab on the dispatcher's instruction until the ramp was stopped, and then went down to the driver. Dispatch stopped all Stage 4 traffic at 02:48:01, and the emergency response team was paged at 02:48. The first response vehicle reached the scene at 02:54 and the second responder arrived at 03:07. The driver was extricated through the rear of the vehicle at 03:14, 27 minutes after the collision, and reached the surface medical facility at 03:41. The driver was flown out at 07:40. The haul truck operator and the night shift supervisor were stood down, and both drug and alcohol tests were negative.

The driver was conscious throughout and sustained a fractured left clavicle, three fractured left ribs and a small pneumothorax. The light vehicle was destroyed. The haul truck was damaged at the front left and returned to service on 14/08/2026. The Stage 4 pit stood down for 9 hours 15 minutes, and the main ramp below RL 340 was closed for 22 hours. The occurrence was notified to the State Mines Inspectorate as a reportable injury.

What we are looking at

The investigation is examining how two vehicles came to be at the same place on the main ramp of the Stage 4 pit without either driver being aware of the other until seconds before impact. The matters below are under examination and no conclusion has been reached on any of them.

Matters under examination

  1. How the RL 312 spur came to be opened. It was cut under works order WO-26-4471 and classified as routine earthworks, so the Management of Change procedure ORR-PRO-022 and what it requires for changes to the road network are being examined, together with the hazard report of 04/08/2026 and how it was handled.
  1. What controls applied at the intersection. These are the traffic management plan's positive-call list (Appendix B, nine intersections), its sight distance and signage clauses, and the in-cab road network.
  1. The collision awareness system. It operated at Level 8 (operator advisory), and a suppression geofence (S4-LOAD) was extended on 09/06/2026. The event log shows a proximity event at 02:47:09.2 with the alert suppressed. The investigation is examining how the geofence was changed and who knew of it.   1. Two arrangements that are under review: the haul operator roster (14 nights on, 7 off) and the night shift emergency extrication arrangement.

Matters still open

Whether airborne dust on the ramp affected the haul truck operator's view was examined and could not be settled. Whether the haul truck operator was fatigued during the approach to the intersection was also examined and could not be settled.

Matters examined and not supported

A wet or slippery ramp surface was examined and was very unlikely. Reduced service brake performance was also examined and was very unlikely, with brake tests giving 0.80 m/s2 net on 03/08/2026 and 0.82 on 14/08/2026, against 0.78 in the occurrence.

Containment and next steps

The actions already taken are reported first, followed by the work still to be done.

Actions already taken

  • Stage 4 pit and main ramp. The Stage 4 pit stood down for 9 hours 15 minutes and the main ramp below RL 340 was closed for 22 hours.
  • RL 312 spur. The spur was barricaded and closed to all traffic on the night and remains closed.
  • Scene and data. Both vehicles were isolated and left in position, and the scene was photographed and surveyed. The HT-407 data was downloaded at 09:30 and provided to the investigation unopened.
  • Site-wide direction. The Mining Manager directed at 05:10 on 12/08/2026 that a positive call on the pit channel is required before entering any intersection in the Stage 4 pit, listed or not, until further notice. The direction was read at the following four pre-starts and posted beside the Appendix B board in the Stage 4 crib room. It stands until BIN-TMP-004 is amended.
  • Sight distance survey. On 14/08/2026 survey measured the sight distance at the other ten unlisted roads cut in Stage 4 since the last review. Two of the ten are below the clause 6.2 minimum and have been closed pending a stop control.
  • Collision awareness reporting. On 28/08/2026 technical services added suppressed proximity events to the monthly collision awareness report, broken down by geofence and by vehicle class. The first report is due 30/09/2026.

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

- [What we know] - Timeline entry "First sighting, the beacon at 53 m" lists the factor "Haul truck operator fatigued during the approach to the intersection". That factor does not appear in the causal chains or among the factors named as failing Influence, so its category is not stated. - Causal chain "Truck operator braked and steered right within 1.4 seconds of the beacon appearing" links directly to the critical event with no intermediate step. Braking reduced the collision speed from 37 km/h to 24 km/h, so the edge may need its direction or label checked. - Light vehicle departure, timed "about 02:45 to about 02:47 (2 m 06 s)": a precise duration is given between two approximate times. The driver's own "about ten to three" is later than the recorded collision and is correctly set aside. - Distance at first sighting: the operator's interview and safety report give about 40 m, the survey and download give 53 m. Both are reported above. - Push distance: the notification says approximately 18 m and the scene survey says 18.4 m. These are consistent, and the measured figure is used. - The light vehicle driver's interview is not finalised. His statements on speed and on not stopping are summarised as provisional. - [What we are looking at] - "Airborne dust on the ramp above the intersection": the Influence test is recorded as Inconclusive, yet the factor carries a "Led to" edge to "Neither driver aware of the other until the truck came over the crest" and appears in that event's "Led from" list. The fatigue factor's notes say an unestablished factor deliberately has no arrow. The dust edge is inconsistent with that approach. - "Haul truck operator fatigued during the approach": the test note says a detailed fatigue assessment "was not pursued".
