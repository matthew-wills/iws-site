The report as drafted

What happened

On the evening of 7 July 2026, the passenger ferry MV Tarrakin Star, a 24.4 m aluminium catamaran, was running service TF18 from Muntari Island to Kanooka Heads. It carried 38 passengers and three crew: a master, a deckhand and a kiosk attendant. This was the last service of the day. Under the winter timetable, this return trip had been made in darkness every day since 1 June 2026. The ferry departed at 17:35, after sunset. Earlier that day, at a change of master at about 13:05, the outgoing master told the relieving master that the chart plotter (the electronic chart display used to track the vessel's position) had frozen twice that morning and recovered both times. The fault was not entered in the defect book. The ferry entered the Weerona Channel at 18:03, on the charted centreline at about 8 knots. At 18:04 the deckhand left the bridge to collect fares. At 18:07 the chart plotter froze again and did not recover. The ferry continued at about 8 knots. From about 18:09 its heading was held a few degrees west of the channel's line, and at about that time it crossed the western edge of the dredged channel. From 18:08 the master searched for the No. 4 beacon, a navigation marker at the channel, and sighted it at 18:10. He opened the wheelhouse window during the search in case the glass was the problem. A later check found that the beacon cannot be seen from the channel over a stretch north of it that included where the search began.

At 18:11:48 the echo sounder (the instrument that measures water depth under the hull) sounded its shallow-water alarm. Four seconds later both engines were put astern together. At 18:12:04, 16 seconds after the alarm, the ferry ran onto Boolarong Bank at 6.8 knots, 74 m west of the channel centreline and 54 m outside the dredged channel. It stopped within 11 m, leaning 4 degrees to starboard on a falling tide. The engines were stopped at 18:12:31 and no attempt was made to refloat. At 18:13 the master made an urgency radio call. Port Control acknowledged it and sent the port workboat. All passengers were in lifejackets by about 18:22. Donning was delayed in the forward saloon, where the lifejacket locker was behind the kiosk counter. The workboat came alongside at 18:41, and the last passenger was ashore at 19:26. There were no serious injuries. One passenger reported a grazed shin and two were treated at the wharf for distress. The starboard hull was split and one internal compartment partly flooded. The starboard propeller, shaft and rudder were damaged and the starboard skeg (a fin under the hull) was torn away. The ferry refloated at 00:20 and was withdrawn from service. The channel was closed to commercial traffic for 19 hours.

Why it happened

The ferry grounded because it left the dredged channel in darkness without this being recognised, and the shallowing water under the hull was detected only 16 seconds before contact.

The most significant factor was what happened after the chart plotter froze at 18:07 and did not recover. It was virtually certain that the plotter froze, and extremely likely that this influenced what followed. The ferry continued at about eight knots rather than stopping in the channel. It was extremely likely that continuing at that speed contributed both to the ferry leaving the dredged channel and to the shoaling not being detected until 16 seconds before contact. It was virtually certain that the ferry left the channel and tracked onto Boolarong Bank, and virtually certain that this contributed to the late detection. It was extremely likely that the late detection contributed to the grounding.

The decision to continue was shaped by the operator's arrangements. There was no night passage plan for the Weerona Channel. There was also no hold point (a pre-agreed point at which the vessel stops and waits) for when the primary means of fixing the vessel's position was lost. It was very likely that this contributed to the transit continuing at eight knots (Safety issue). Behind it lay a further gap. When the winter timetable moved the last service into darkness, the route risk assessment was not reopened, and it was likely that this contributed to the absence of a night passage plan and hold point (Safety issue).

The plotter fault had been passed on verbally at the change of master and was not entered in the defect book. It was likely that this contributed to the plotter freezing during the channel transit. The change-of-master defect brief was verbal, with nothing checking that what was said was written down, and it was likely that this contributed to the fault going unrecorded (Safety issue).

With the plotter lost, the master looked for the No. 4 beacon. It was extremely likely that the beacon could not be seen from the channel over the half mile before the vessel left it. It was very likely that this contributed to the vessel leaving the channel. Two safety issues lay behind this. The beacon had been converted to solar power, halving its nominal range (the distance at which it is designed to be seen), and no operator was told. It was likely that this contributed (Safety issue). Clearing of the beacon's sightline had also been deferred, unfunded, for three works rounds, and it was very likely that this contributed (Safety issue). Both stemmed from a wider condition. Aids to navigation on this waterway carry no availability standard and no duty to tell users anything, and it was likely that this contributed to both (Safety issue).

The investigation also examined whether new foreshore floodlighting behind the beacon reduced the master's ability to pick it up. This matter could not be resolved and is not a finding. The floodlighting is aligned behind the beacon, and the masters' accounts were consistent and independent. However, the available evidence could not establish whether the floodlighting affected detection, and the investigation assessed its effect as about as likely as not. The question may be unanswerable while the mangroves remain. Regardless of this outcome, a lighting assessment of the channel is warranted.

What we found

A finding stated without a likelihood term was established as fact.

Contributing safety factors

  • When the winter timetable moved the last service into darkness, the route risk assessment was not reopened, and it is likely that this contributed to the occurrence. (Safety issue)
  • It is virtually certain that there was no night passage plan for the channel and no hold point (a pre-agreed point at which the vessel stops and waits) if the primary means of fixing the vessel's position was lost, and it is very likely that this contributed. (Safety issue)
  • The change-of-master defect brief was verbal, with nothing checking that what was said got written down, and this probably contributed to the occurrence. (Safety issue)
  • On the balance of probabilities, the plotter fault being passed on verbally at the change of master and not entered in the defect book contributed to the occurrence.
  • With virtual certainty, the chart plotter display froze during the channel transit and did not recover, and this almost certainly contributed.
  • The transit continued at eight knots after the plotter froze, rather than stopping in the channel, and it is highly likely that this contributed.
  • Aids to navigation on this waterway carry no availability standard and no duty to tell users anything, a condition the evidence establishes conclusively, and it is probable that this contributed. (Safety issue)
  • The No. 4 beacon was converted to solar power, its nominal range (the distance at which it is designed to be seen) was halved and no operator was told, and in all likelihood this contributed. (Safety issue)
  • Clearing of the beacon's sightline was deferred, unfunded, for three works rounds, and this very probably contributed. (Safety issue)
  • It is very likely that the No. 4 beacon was not visible from the channel over the half mile before the vessel left it, and very likely that this contributed.
  • The vessel left the dredged channel and tracked onto Boolarong Bank, and this contributed to the grounding.
  • It is virtually certain that shoaling (shallowing water under the hull) was not detected until sixteen seconds before contact, and very likely that this contributed.

Other factors that increased risk

  • The forward saloon lifejacket locker was not reachable from most of the saloon it serves, and this very likely increased risk. (Safety issue)
  • On the day of the occurrence the master was on day nine of twelve, on a split duty across a thirteen hour forty minute spread, and this probably increased risk.

Other findings

  • The investigation examined whether a water level below prediction reduced the depth over Boolarong Bank, and ruled it out.
  • A steering or propulsion failure causing the vessel to sheer off track was examined and ruled out.
  • Stopping the engines and making no attempt to refloat on a falling tide was examined and found to have reduced risk.

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

  • [What happened] - Timeline line "Master begins looking for the No. 4 beacon": the note says the whole search took place inside the screened arc (0.84 nm to 0.34 nm north of the beacon). However, the beacon was sighted at 0.25 nm, which is outside that arc. The search from 0.34 nm to 0.25 nm was therefore not screened, on the stated figures.
  • Timeline line "Vessel crosses the channel's western edge" (about 18:09): at the stated 12 m of westing a minute, moving from the 20 m half width to 74 m west at contact (18:12:04) takes about 4.5 minutes. That places the crossing at about 18:07:30, not about 18:09. The crossing time, the westing rate, or both need reconciling.
  • Contact timeline line cites "the survey of 08/07/2026". The survey and slipping report is dated 09/07/2026 to 14/07/2026.
  • Causal chain "Engines stopped and no attempt made to refloat on a falling tide -> Grounding on Boolarong Bank" runs backwards in time. The engines were stopped at 18:12:31, after contact at 18:12:04, so this factor cannot be upstream of the critical event.
  • [Why it happened] - "Plotter fault passed on verbally at the change of master and not entered in the defect book" is recorded as leading to "Chart plotter display froze during the channel transit and did not recover". Not recording a defect cannot itself cause a display to freeze. The intended link may be to the vessel sailing with an unrectified or unflagged plotter. The investigator may wish to reword the factor or the link.
  • Positive event "Engines stopped and no attempt made to refloat on a falling tide" is recorded as leading to "Grounding on Boolarong Bank with 38 passengers embarked". The engines were stopped at 18:12:31, after contact at 18:12:04, so this link runs backwards in time. It probably belongs downstream of the grounding, for example as limiting further damage.
  • "Forward saloon lifejacket locker not reachable from most of the saloon it serves" appears both under Safety issues and under Other risk factors, and is marked Finalised: no in both places. Its status needs confirming before findings are drafted.
  • The approved "What happened" text states the master sighted the No. 4 beacon at 18:10, and that the vessel crossed the channel edge at about 18:09. The factor states the beacon was not visible over the half mile before the vessel left the channel. These are consistent, but the investigator may wish to confirm that the timings and the half-mile distance align at the recorded speed.
  • [What we found] - Approved "What happened" text: it states that the heading was held a few degrees west "from about 18:09". The AIS readout AIS-2026-0093-R1 records the heading change beginning at 18:07:06, with rate of turn peaking at 18:07:40 and the new heading held from about 18:08:50. At about 18:09 the vessel reached the channel's western edge. The investigator may wish to reconcile the approved wording with the track.
  • "Forward saloon lifejacket locker not reachable from most of the saloon it serves": the object is marked "Finalised: no" (pending the fleet stowage answer). It appears both under the safety issues heading and under other risk factors labelled "(safety issue)". It has been tagged "(Safety issue)" here on that basis. The investigator should confirm the tag and its finalised status before release.
  • "Vessel left the dredged channel and tracked onto Boolarong Bank": the evidence text gives 38 m west of the centreline "by 18:10:00". The AIS table gives 39 m west at 18:10:02 and has no 18:10:00 entry. This is a minor difference in the figure quoted.
  • [What is being done] - Approved "What we found" section: several likelihood expressions are not the recorded terms and are not permitted. These are "probably" (change-of-master brief, recorded likely), "On the balance of probabilities" (plotter fault not booked, likely), "almost certainly" (plotter freeze influence, extremely likely), "highly likely" (transit continued, extremely likely), "conclusively" and "probable" (aids to navigation, virtually certain and likely), "in all likelihood" (solar conversion, likely), "very probably" (sightline deferral, very likely) and "probably increased risk" (fatigue, Importance likely).
  • Approved "What we found": the No. 4 beacon existence is given as "very likely", but the recorded existence is "Pass - extremely likely". The shoaling influence is given as "very likely", but the recorded influence is "extremely likely".
  • Approved "What happened" states the heading was held west "from about 18:09". The AIS evidence has the heading change beginning at 18:07:06 and held from about a minute later, with the channel edge reached at about 18:09:00.
  • Approved "What happened" includes details I could not find in the summarised evidence: the opened wheelhouse window, stopping within 11 m, the 4 degree list, the workboat alongside at 18:41, and the injuries. These may be in extracts held on file, and should be checked.
  • Lifejacket timing: the locker factor records all passengers in lifejackets "within about eight minutes" of the grounding, which is about 18:20. Approved "What happened" gives "by about 18:22".
