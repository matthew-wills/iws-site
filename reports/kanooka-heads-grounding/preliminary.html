Preliminary update - the information in this report is based on the evidence available at the time of writing and may change as the investigation proceeds.

Summary

On 7 July 2026, the passenger ferry MV Tarrakin Star, a 24.4 m aluminium catamaran certificated for 96 passengers, ran aground on Boolarong Bank in the Merrandi Channel, Kanooka Heads, in darkness on a falling tide while inbound on service TF18 with 38 passengers and three crew. There were nil serious injuries, but the hull was substantially damaged, all 38 passengers were ashore by evening, and the vessel refloated and was withdrawn from service. The Merrandi Channel was closed to commercial traffic for 19 hours. The cause is still being examined and no findings have been reached. Interim actions already taken include trimming the canopy that screened the No. 4 beacon and issuing a local notice to waterway users.

What we know

MV Tarrakin Star is a 24.4 m aluminium catamaran passenger ferry certificated for 96 passengers. On 07/07/2026 it departed Muntari Island at 17:35:02 on service TF18 to Kanooka Heads with 38 passengers, a master, a deckhand and a kiosk attendant on board. All times in this update are local time (UTC+10). Sunset was at 17:02 and civil twilight ended at 17:29. The moon was a waning crescent, 2% illuminated. At 18:00 the wind was 305 degrees at 8 kt, visibility was over 10 km and the sea state in the channel was slight. The winter timetable had moved TF18 into darkness from 01/06/2026, and this was the thirty-first darkness transit.

The Merrandi Channel is 40 m wide and dredged to 3.2 m, with Boolarong Bank to its west. The vessel entered the channel at 18:03:58 and was on the charted centreline abeam the No. 2 beacon at 18:05:30, 1 m west of it at 8.4 kt on a course of 195 degrees. The chart plotter's event log records the display process not responding at 18:07:06 with cause code 0x51, and no reset was recorded. The master said the chart picture stayed on the screen with the vessel symbol stationary and no response to touch. The master then looked for the No. 4 beacon and did not find it until it was dead ahead. The bridge audio has "Right. Where's four" at 18:08:31 and "There's four" at 18:10:02.

From 18:07:06 the heading increased from 195 to 200 degrees over about a minute and was then held. The vessel crossed the channel's western edge at about 18:09:00 (estimated), 20 m west of the centreline. Speed over the ground fell from 8.4 kt to 7.1 kt by 18:11:48, with no throttle movement recorded until 18:11:52. The echo sounder's shallow alarm, set to 0.9 m under the keel since commissioning in 2019, sounded at 18:11:48. Both throttles went astern at 18:11:52. Contact with Boolarong Bank came at 18:12:04 at 6.8 kt, 16 seconds after the alarm. The vessel travelled 58 m in that time, against the trials crash-stop figure of 92 m from 8 kt. It came to rest within 11 m of first contact, heeled 4 degrees to starboard, 74 m west of the charted centreline and 54 m outside the dredged channel.

Both engines were stopped at 18:12:31 and were not restarted. At 18:13:04 the master broadcast a pan pan and stated that there would be no attempt to refloat. The master's statement in that broadcast was "no pollution", and nothing further on pollution is held. The workboat came alongside at 18:41. All 38 passengers were in lifejackets by about 18:22 and ashore by 19:26. The vessel refloated on the rising tide at 00:20 on 08/07/2026 and was towed to the town wharf.

There were no serious injuries. One passenger reported a grazed shin and two were treated at the wharf for distress. The starboard hull bottom plating was set up over 4.1 m, with a 180 mm split in way of the No. 2 void, and flooding of that void reached approximately 260 mm by the time of refloating. The starboard propeller blades were bent, the shaft was bent 1.4 mm, the starboard rudder stock was distorted 3 degrees and the starboard skeg was torn away. The vessel was withdrawn from service, and the Merrandi Channel was closed to commercial traffic for 19 hours. [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether the vessel has been repaired or returned to service.]

Two explanations have been examined and are very unlikely. Steering or propulsion failure before the vessel left the centreline is very unlikely because engine speeds, steering pressure of 96 to 99 bar and rudder response were normal throughout, and the steering system was removed and function tested on 12/07/2026. Tide set down below prediction is very unlikely because the gauge read 0.91 m at 18:12 against a predicted 0.94 m.

What we are looking at

Six matters are being examined, and none has been settled.

  1. The chart plotter and defect reporting. The 46 hour bench test on 21/07/2026 did not reproduce the fault and found no defect. The contractor reported that cause code 0x51 is addressed by firmware issued in 11/2024, which this unit had not had applied. The unit logged the same code at 10:42 and 11:17 on the morning of the occurrence and recovered itself both times. The outgoing master, morning shift, told the relieving master at about 13:05, and nothing was entered in the defect book. We are examining how OPS-9 treats faults that clear themselves.
  1. Night operation and passage planning. RRA-07 issue 2 carries a "daylight transits only" restriction, and the passage plan appendix (issue 2) says night transits are not scheduled and names no hold point. CHG-2026-014, approved on 22/04/2026, assessed the move of TF18 to 17:35 as a "timetable change only". We are examining how that assessment was made. The Operations Manager has given a verbal account only.
  1. The No. 4 beacon and its surroundings. The check on 18/07/2026 was made on the inbound track, and the vessel was west of that track. It found the beacon not visible from the channel between 0.84 nm and 0.34 nm north of it, through mangrove canopy on Merrandi Islet. The canopy measured 5.8 m against a 4.2 m lantern. After a solar conversion in 11/2025 from a lantern rated at 6, the luminous range measured 2.6 nm against a nominal 3 nm, and the passage plan still records 6 M. The register holds WO-118 (raised 09/2023) and WO-176 (raised 03/2026). Fourteen unshielded floodlights lie behind the beacon from the inbound track, and whether they affected sighting was examined and could not be resolved. [NOT ESTABLISHED: the outcome of any examination of the Aids code WAN-2 and the services agreement]
  1. The echo sounder alarm. The alarm was set to 0.9 m under the keel, the value written in at commissioning in 2019. It sounded 16 seconds before contact, during which the vessel covered 58 m, against a trials crash stop distance of 92 m from 8 kt. We are examining the setting against the speed and the stopping distance.
  1. Duty and rostering. The master was on day nine of twelve, on a split duty of 05:40 to 08:40 and 13:00 to 19:20, and recorded 6 hours 15 minutes of sleep. Both the duty and the spread were inside the operator's limits. We are examining how the winter roster places the darkness transit. Whether the master was fatigued during the transit was also examined and could not be resolved.
  1. The forward saloon lifejacket locker. The locker is behind the kiosk counter and cannot be reached from 22 of the 34 seats without passing behind the counter. The operator says the same counter is fitted to its two other 24 m vessels, and we are examining that.

Containment and next steps

The vessel was withdrawn from service after it refloated at 00:20 on 08/07/2026. The Harbour Master, Kanooka Heads, closed work order WO-176 on 24/07/2026 after it was funded from the 2026 waterways contingency and the canopy on Merrandi Islet was trimmed to 2 m. A three-yearly trimming cycle has been programmed, and the sightline is to be re-measured from the inbound track on completion.

The Aids to Navigation Officer, Kanooka Shire Council, issued local notice to waterway users LNM-2026-14 on 22/07/2026, giving the No. 4 beacon's solar conversion and its 3 M nominal range. That action is in progress. The Port Authority's aids to navigation register was made available to every commercial operator using the channel, and Tarrakin Ferries reissued the Merrandi Channel passage plan appendix as issue 3 with the corrected range. These are interim actions and are not evidence of a cause.

On 20/07/2026 the Port Authority advised that it holds no record of any operator asking about the channel's aids and that it has never provided the register to Tarrakin Ferries. The company's "navigation hazard" reporting category exists and has not been used on this route in three years.

On 27/07/2026 the investigator in charge asked Tarrakin Ferries to write a night passage plan naming a hold point before any further darkness transit. The investigator in charge also asked that the services agreement replace the 50 m vegetation scope at schedule 2.4 with a sightline test. Both are open, with responses due by 30/09/2026. [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether the operator and the Port Authority have responded, since the response date has passed.]

[NOT ESTABLISHED: any action on the plotter firmware, the echo sounder alarm setting, the defect reporting procedure, the route risk assessment, the roster or the lifejacket locker.]

Enquiries still open:

  • Whether any master on the route has been given a night passage plan for any channel, which is to be put to the operator.
  • The operator's and the regulator's answers on the lifejacket counter across the fleet.
  • One further interview, which has not yet been finalised.
  • [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether a lighting assessment of the foreshore has been commissioned.]
  • [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether and when a formal interview with the Operations Manager will be taken.]

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

- [What we know] - Channel transit, "about 18:09" western edge crossing: the outline states the crossing as "one hundred and fourteen seconds after the plotter froze". That figure takes its precision from a time the source marks as estimated. The draft keeps "about 18:09". - Grounding and response: the occurrence notification and AIS-2026-0093-R1 record the vessel heeled 4 degrees to starboard. The master's VHF 16 broadcast at 08:13:04 UTC says "we are upright". The draft omits the heel rather than choose between the two, and the final text may need to state both. - Occurrence notification TFP-OCC-2026-0093 says the vessel "set progressively to starboard". AIS-2026-0093-R1 states the movement was not a current effect but a held heading change of five degrees. The notification's wording could be read as describing a current set. The draft follows the recorded heading data. - Deckhand interview notes (not finalised) say she "was called up to the bridge at about 18:04". The bridge audio at 08:04:10 UTC shows the deckhand asking "Want me to stay up?" and the master sending her to do fares. The two accounts differ on who initiated the exchange. - [What we are looking at] - Master fatigued during the channel transit, and the roster factor's evidence against influence: both say the decision not to refloat "was made in about thirty seconds". The positive event "Engines stopped and no attempt made to refloat" says the pan pan stating that decision was made 60 seconds after the vessel came to rest, and that the engines were stopped 27 seconds after contact. These timings should be reconciled. - Darkness transit roster factor, Master fatigued factor and Heading eased factor: the investigator notes are dated 09/10/2026 ("Split out of the old roster object on 09/10/2026", "Added 09/10/2026"). That is after the generation date of 08/10/2026. The date may be wrong, or it may be written in a different date order. - [Containment and next steps] - Investigation log, 08/07/2026: the suspension of evening Muntari services has no later status entry. It is therefore unclear whether TF18 darkness transits have resumed. This bears on the open recommendation's condition "before any further darkness transit". - Night passage plan and services agreement recommendations: both show a response due 30/09/2026 and a status of Open. That date has passed as of the generation date of 08/10/2026. - Lifejacket locker object, Influence note: it says all passengers were in lifejackets "within about eight minutes". The approved "What we know" section gives about 18:22 against contact at 18:12:04, which is about ten minutes. - Roster and fatigue objects: the investigator notes say "Split out of the old roster object on 09/10/2026". That date is after the generation date of 08/10/2026. - Decision not to refloat: the roster and fatigue objects give "about thirty seconds". The positive event places the pan pan stating the decision 60 seconds after the vessel came to rest, and describes the decision as made "inside a minute". - [Summary] - Causal chains: the chain "Unshielded boat ramp floodlights aligned behind the No. 4 beacon from the north -> Heading eased five degrees to starboard..." and that factor's "Led to" line draw an arrow from a factor whose Influence test is Inconclusive (about as likely as not). The factor's own evidence against influence also states that the heading change began at the plotter, before the search, so the floodlights "cannot have caused the turn". The edge appears to run against the recorded test and the timing. - Lifejacket timing: the lifejacket locker object gives all 38 passengers in lifejackets "within about eight minutes". The approved "What we know" section gives about 18:22, which is about ten minutes after contact at 18:12:04. One of the two values may need reconciling.
