The report as drafted

Summary

On 18 June 2026, Saltbush Air SB512, a twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, rejected its take-off from runway 09 at Tarlton Springs Aerodrome after dark. The crew rejected at 118 kt, 6 kt above V1, when two kangaroos were seen on the runway. The aircraft struck the animals and came to rest 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area. None of the 44 passengers, 2 flight crew or 1 cabin crew was seriously injured, although one cabin crew member reported bruising. The aircraft was substantially damaged, including the No. 2 propeller, the right main gear doors and the lower right fuselage, and the aerodrome was closed for 4 hours 20 minutes. The investigation is examining:

  • how the animals reached the runway
  • the wildlife management and runway inspection arrangements
  • how the evening service was introduced
  • the rejected take-off decision and stopping performance.

The operator suspended its evening Tarlton Springs service on 19 June 2026.

What we know

On 18 June 2026, Saltbush Air SB512, a 50-seat twin-turboprop registered VH-SBQ, was preparing to depart Tarlton Springs Aerodrome with 44 passengers, 2 flight crew and 1 cabin crew on board. It was the last scheduled sector of the day and the fifth sector of the crew's duty. The sector had been added that morning when the rostered crew reported unfit. The aerodrome was in darkness.

The aerodrome's only runway inspection that day was carried out at 21:35 UTC on 17 June, before the first scheduled arrival. It recorded the movement area as clear.

The doors closed at 07:16 UTC, 40 minutes behind schedule. At 07:29:10 the tower cleared the aircraft for take-off on runway 09, with the wind at 090 at 6 kt. The tower did not offer a runway inspection and the crew did not request one. The first officer was flying.

Take-off power was set at 07:30:02. The flight data shows both engines at matching torque, with no caution or warning, throughout the roll. The pilot in command called V1, calculated at 112 kt, at 07:30:29.

At 07:30:31 the first officer called two kangaroos on the runway. They were crossing from the northern side, at an estimated range of around 200 m. At 07:30:32 the pilot in command took control and rejected the take-off at 118 kt. This was 6 kt above V1, with 695 m of sealed runway remaining.

About one second later the aircraft struck the animals with the right main landing gear and the No. 2 propeller. The pilot in command's report describes striking at least one animal. Two carcasses were later recovered from the runway.

Maximum braking was established 2 seconds after the decision. Reverse was obtained on No. 1 only. The No. 2 reverse did not engage, and No. 2 propeller speed was recorded decaying from the moment of impact.

The aircraft left the end of runway 09 at approximately 21 kt at 07:30:51. It came to rest at 07:30:57, 62 m into the 90 m runway end safety area. It was upright, on the extended centreline, and 28 m short of the end of the prepared area. There was no fire.

The crew shut down both engines and did not order an evacuation. At the pilot in command's request, the tower activated the aerodrome emergency plan and a vehicle attended. Passengers left by the forward airstair. The operator's notification records the disembarkation as 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped.

The aircraft sustained the following damage:

  • three No. 2 propeller blades damaged beyond limits
  • right main gear doors distorted
  • both right main tyres deflated through their fuse plugs
  • two punctures in the lower right fuselage.

There were no serious injuries. One cabin crew member later reported bruising during the disembarkation. Two kangaroos were killed, and the aerodrome was closed for 4 hours 20 minutes.

What we are looking at

  1. How the animals reached the runway. The investigation is examining the condition of the northern perimeter fence, the history of its repair on the aerodrome works register, and the aerodrome's wildlife sighting log. This is because both animals were recovered on the northern half of the runway, and an animal pad was found through the fence on that side.
  1. The framework for wildlife management at smaller aerodromes. The investigation is examining how Aerodrome Standard AS-139 section 10.4 applies to an aerodrome of this size. Tarlton Springs falls below the thresholds that require a documented wildlife hazard management plan.
  1. Why no runway inspection was requested before departure. The investigation is examining the operator's procedure at Operations Manual Part A 8.14. That procedure requires a runway inspection after last light only at aerodromes listed in Appendix 8A. The investigation is also examining how an aerodrome comes to be placed on that list.
  1. How the evening service was introduced. The investigation is examining how change management record CMR-2025-118 assessed the new evening rotation. The route risk assessment for Tarlton Springs, RRA-041, carried a daylight-only restriction.
  1. The rejected take-off decision and stopping performance. The investigation is examining:
  • the decision to reject above V1
  • the procedures and recurrent training that apply to that decision
  • how the strike and the loss of No. 2 reverse affected the aircraft's deceleration against the scheduled figures.
  1. Other matters.
  • The investigation is examining the crew's duty day and the way the evening rotation is rostered.
  • It is also examining a fault found after the occurrence in the cabin floor proximity emergency lighting. The fleet-wide position on that fault is being established with engineering.
  • The investigation examined whether the intensity of the runway edge lighting affected the crew's ability to see the animals. Some of the lights measured above nominal intensity when checked seven days later. This question could not be resolved on the evidence available and remains open.

Containment and next steps

Interim action and containment

  • Evening Tarlton Springs service suspended. On 19 June 2026 the operator's Head of Flying Operations suspended the evening Tarlton Springs service. It applies to the operator's scheduled evening rotation into the aerodrome. [NOT ESTABLISHED: whether the suspension remains in place, and any conditions for resuming the service]
  • Crew stood down. Both crew members were stood down and tested after the occurrence. The results were negative.
  • Matters referred. The investigation has raised with the operator's Safety Department how an aerodrome comes to be listed in Appendix 8A of Operations Manual Part A. It has also referred the fleet-wide position on the cabin floor proximity lighting ballast to engineering. No safety action has been recorded in response to either matter.

No other interim safety action or containment has been recorded.

Next steps

The investigation is:

  • awaiting engineering's answer on whether the same ballast fault affects other aircraft of the type
  • keeping open the question of whether runway edge lighting intensity affected the crew's ability to see the animals, which cannot be settled on the evidence now available
  • completing its analysis of the matters set out above

The draft final report will be:

  • provided to the people and organisations it discusses so they can correct matters of fact

Any safety action that follows will be assigned an owner and a date.

Review notes

Observations the model made about the record itself while drafting, kept apart from the prose for the investigator to resolve.

  • [What we know] - Disembarkation timing: the timeline entry spans 07:34:40 to 07:38:48 (4 m 08 s). The notification says passengers were disembarked 4 minutes 8 seconds after the aircraft stopped (07:30:57), which would be about 07:35:05. These cannot both be right.
  • The draft outline for this section says "five minutes after the end of civil twilight". No last-light or twilight time appears in any source, so it was omitted.
  • The draft outline describes the No. 2 propeller as "destroyed". The inspection records three blades damaged beyond repairable limits, so the draft uses that wording.
  • The draft outline says passengers were clear "four minutes after it stopped". The source gives 4 minutes 8 seconds and does not say "clear", so the source figure is used.
  • Duty sign-on: the flight crew duty record gives 06:30 local. The pilot in command's interview background gives a duty that began at 05:10 local.
  • The pilot in command's most recent check: the crew record gives the last cyclic simulator as 11/03/2026. The interview background gives a recurrent simulator check in February 2026.
  • Number of animals struck: the pilot in command's ASR says "at least one". The notification says both, and two carcasses were recovered.
  • Causal chain "Maximum braking and No. 1 reverse applied without delay -> Runway overrun onto the runway end safety area": this places a prompt, mitigating crew response as upstream of the overrun. The edge should be checked.
  • [What we are looking at] - Perimeter fence repair / Two kangaroos entered the runway: the distance from the fence pad to the first carcass is recorded three ways. It is 110 m in the fence factor's evidence and 138 m in the kangaroo factor's evidence (1,188 m less 1,050 m). The fence factor's significance line gives it as 140 m "apart".
  • Fifth sector, 11 hours into duty: the "Evidence against influence" field opens with argument for influence ("For influence: ..."). The "Evidence for influence" field reads as an Importance argument, so the two fields appear mislabelled.
  • Cabin floor proximity emergency lighting: this factor appears twice, under both safety issues and other risk factors, with differing category labels.
  • [Containment and next steps] - "Perimeter fence repair deferred for three budget cycles": the evidence places the animal pad 110 m before the first carcass. The "Two kangaroos entered the runway strip" factor gives 138 m. The significance line gives 140 m. The positions recorded (1,050 m and 1,188 m) give 138 m.
  • "Evening service approved as a schedule change, route risk assessment not reopened": the Influence result is recorded as "likely", but the report outline gives 85%. That figure does not fit the "likely" grading used elsewhere (70% and 75%).
  • "Cabin floor proximity emergency lighting partially unserviceable": this item is categorised both as a safety issue and as an other risk factor, and it remains unfinalised.
  • "Fifth sector, 11 hours into duty, 40 minutes behind schedule": the "Evidence against influence" field contains both the for and the against arguments. The "Evidence for influence" field contains an Importance argument rather than an Influence one.
  • Investigation log, 19 June 2026: no later entry confirms whether the suspension of the evening service remains in force.
  • [Summary] - Summary scaffold, "What happened": the date is given as 17/06/2026. The critical event evidence and the notification both give 18/06/2026 at 07:30 UTC, which is 17:30 local on the same day. The draft uses 18 June 2026.
  • The distance from the fence pad to the first carcass is recorded three different ways:
  • 110 m in the "Perimeter fence repair deferred" evidence
  • 138 m in the "Two kangaroos entered the runway strip" evidence
  • 140 m in the fence object's significance line, which measures to the second carcass at 1,221 m, not the first.

The 1,050 m and 1,188 m positions give 138 m.

  • "Fifth sector, 11 hours into duty": the "Evidence for influence" field holds importance-type argument. The "Evidence against influence" field opens with "For influence:" and contains both sides. The two fields appear to be transposed or merged.
  • Summary scaffold, "Safety issues": the cabin floor proximity lighting is listed with the grade "(unlikely)". That is its failed Influence grade, not a safety issue confidence, and its Importance grade is very likely.
  • Causal chains: "Maximum braking and No. 1 reverse applied without delay" is shown leading to the overrun, although it is recorded as a positive event that reduced risk.
