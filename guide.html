Using this guide

What this guide covers, how it is organised, and where the first-run walkthrough and the method itself are explained.

#What this guide covers

The Investigation Workflow Suite maps a safety occurrence as an AcciMap. Every suspected event or condition becomes an object on the map, is tested against evidence for Existence, Influence and Importance, and is connected to its consequences with causal arrows.

A finished map reads in two directions at once. Left to right is time: the sequence that led to the occurrence. Bottom to top is organisational depth: from what individuals did, up through the conditions and controls that shaped their actions, to the organisational influences behind those controls. The causal chains converge on the critical event at the bottom right.

The discipline of the method is that nothing is a finding until it has passed its tests. Objects carry their test state visibly, so anyone reading the map can distinguish a proven contributing factor from an untested idea at a glance.

This User Guide is the reference you come back to. It is organised the way the app is: one section for each tab of an investigation, in the order the tabs run, then Settings, exporting, files, the licence, installing and the keyboard and mouse controls. Each control is described where you meet it, and each part of the method is explained where it operates in the app. Every subsection has its own link, and the filter above the contents finds any word in the guide, headings and text alike.

The pictures are of the worked example investigation that ships with the app, taken by the application itself rather than drawn, so what is in them is what you will see. Click one to enlarge it.

#New to IWS: Getting started

New to the app? Getting started takes one investigation from the start screen to an exported document, in the order the work is done, and is the place to begin. This guide assumes you have been through it, or are looking something up. The reasoning behind the method itself is on the Methodology page.

#The menu bar

The menu bar across the top of the window works as in any Windows application. File holds New investigation, Open, Reopen last, Save, Save as, Export, Open exports folder and Exit. Edit holds Undo and Redo, named with the step each would reverse, then Recycle bin and Settings. View lists the tabs in the order the tab bar shows them, with the open one ticked, so you can change tab from the keyboard. Help opens the Help window on any of its pages. At the right of the row sit the AI run, your licence level, the file size once it passes 10 MB, and the investigation's name, with a dot while there are unsaved changes.

Press and release Alt to move to the menu bar and show each menu's underlined letter, or press Alt with that letter (Alt+F for File) to open a menu straight away. The arrow keys move between the menus and through the items, Enter or an item's underlined letter runs it, and Esc closes the menu; a second Esc goes back to where you were. The shortcuts are shown beside their items: Ctrl+N New investigation, Ctrl+O Open, Ctrl+S Save, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y Undo and Redo, Ctrl+, Settings and F1 the User Guide. Exit asks about unsaved changes exactly as closing the window does. Anything that needs an open investigation is greyed out until one is open.

#The tabs of an investigation

An open investigation shows a row of tabs, each one workspace of the suite: Details, Dashboard, Admin & Comms, Evidence, Timeline, AcciMap, Record, Draft report and Log. Record is hidden when AI access is Connected AI, where Draft my report writes from the record. Everything they hold travels in the one investigation file (.iwsx; an older .sfm opens and keeps saving under its own name). Drag the tabs into whatever order suits how you work; the order is remembered on this machine.

#The Help window

Help opens from the Help menu, each of its items going straight to that page, or with F1 to the User Guide. Getting started is the first-run walkthrough, and User Guide is this guide. Three references follow, which the app draws live from its own rules: Object key, the colour and shape of every object state with the path that leads to it; Decision tree, the order the Existence, Influence and Importance questions are asked in; and Canvas layers, what sits on the map and in what order. Where the edition ships one, Example report (PDF) opens the worked example report in your PDF reader. About names the version, the edition, your licence level and the build, which is what to quote when you ask for support, and gives the licence terms and a button that lists the third-party notices. Close shuts the window, and so does Escape.

#Names used in this guide

The map is the AcciMap, the name its tab carries. Its three tests are Existence, Influence and Importance, always in that order; the short form E/I/I appears only where it names something in the app, such as the Decision tree. The record is everything you have entered: the objects and their tests, the evidence, the timeline, the interviews and the safety actions. AI access has three tiers (No AI, Copy and paste and Connected AI), and a licence has levels (Free, Professional and Organisation), so "tier" always means AI access and "level" always means the licence.

1Dashboard

The investigation at a glance: eleven tiles, the checklist and one to-do list.

Where the investigation stands, on the tab you open first. A band of tiles across the top counts what has been gathered and what has been settled: checklist questions answered, outstanding to-dos, evidence items (with the similar events register as a line within that tile), interviews, timeline events, objects on the map and how many are finalised, report sections drafted and finalised in the style you have chosen, and the errors and warnings the record checks are currently reporting. Every figure is read live from the file, so none of it has to be kept up to date by hand. Click a tile to go to the tab where that work is done. Under the tiles sit the checklist and the to-do list, with the first checklist section and the To do panel starting on the same line. The Blocking draft tile counts anything that stops drafting, and the Draft report tab lists it, one line each, with a Take me there link. An amber count also sits on the Draft report tab itself while something blocks drafting, and goes once nothing does (drafted sections that are not finalised do not count). Amber counts also sit on the Evidence sub-tab or interview concerned, so an unfinalised interview or register cannot be missed. On the Evidence tab a note names each interview, or tool started on one, that is still open, with an Open link that goes straight to it.

#The tiles, in order

Ten tiles run left to right, with Blocking draft between the map tile and Drafted. It reads 0 when nothing stops drafting, and turns amber above zero. Checklist counts questions answered out of the total, and To-dos counts how many are still outstanding; clicking either does nothing, since both lists are already on this tab. Evidence counts finalised evidence items out of the total and jumps to Evidence; the line under its label counts the entries in the similar events register, and the tile turns amber when either is not settled. Interviews counts finalised interviews out of the total and also jumps to Evidence. Timeline counts the number of timeline events and jumps to Timeline. The objects tile is named after your map (for example AcciMap objects) and counts how many objects on it are finalised out of the total, and jumps to the map tab. Blocking draft counts what stops Draft my report starting (an object not finalised, no title, a Details value not valid yet), the same list the Is the record ready card shows, and jumps to Draft report. Drafted sections that are not finalised are not counted. It is amber whenever it is above zero. Objects, Evidence and Interviews turn amber when the first figure is below the second. Drafted counts report sections drafted out of the chosen style's total and jumps to Draft report, and Finalised does the same for sections finalised. Errors and Warnings count the record checks currently sitting at each severity, and both jump to Record (at Connected AI, where Record is hidden, to Draft report).

#The checklist

The checklist is a sequential question flow for the whole investigation, answered Yes, No, or N/A (click an answer again to clear it). Guidance notes sit under the questions where a step needs prompting, including an evidence-gathering list (CCTV, recorded data, audio, records of recent change, impairment and fatigue assessment and so on) to help you think of sources before they are overwritten or lost. It seeds from the template in Settings > Method and wording > Checklists and interview text when the investigation is created, and from then on belongs to the investigation.

The panel is titled Checklist, with a count reading "{n} of {n} addressed" beside it. + Section adds a new section and asks for its title. Each section carries a chevron that collapses it to its header (clicking the title does the same), so completed sections tuck away, a count of how many of its items are answered, and a × that deletes the whole section after asking. Within a section, each item offers Yes, No and N/A as its answer and a × that removes the item. At the foot of each section + Add item opens a New item… box for another question: Enter adds it, Escape cancels.

Updating to the current checklist

The checklist is copied from the checklist template when the investigation starts, and does not change when the template does. When your organisation's checklist changes (or this investigation was started on an older version of the app), a line under the checklist heading says the checklist differs from the current checklist template, with an Update to the current checklist… button. The button opens a preview, and nothing changes until you choose Update checklist in it. Dismiss hides the line for that version of the template, on this computer; it comes back when the template changes again, and the Update to the current checklist… button stays beside the checklist heading meanwhile.

  • FOR each question in the current template
    • IF the template names it with a key your checklist also carries, or failing that the wording matches (ignoring capitals, spacing and punctuation), under the same section first and then anywhere
      • THEN your answer stays with it, in the template's own wording and section
    • ELSE it arrives as a new question, unanswered
  • FOR each question in your checklist the template no longer asks
    • IF you added it yourself with + Add item
      • THEN it stays where it is, in its section after the question it followed, answered or not (a question added before the app started marking them is treated like a template question)
    • ELSE IF you answered it
      • THEN it is kept, with its answer, in a Former items section at the end, naming the section it was under
    • ELSE it is removed, and the preview lists it first

A reworded question with no key cannot be told apart from a new one, so it shows as one question kept under Former items with your answer and one new question to answer again. The preview lists every change by kind: wording changed, moved to another section, kept under Former items, removed and new. The update is one step, so Undo (Ctrl+Z) puts the whole checklist back as it was, and it writes one entry on the Log saying how many questions were kept, added, kept under Former items and removed.

Former items are part of the record and appear in the app's own exports, but they are not counted in the checklist's total and are not written into your organisation's checklist form, which no longer asks them: their answers are listed at the end of the filled form instead, each with the section it was asked under, so nothing you answered is left out. If a later template asks one of them again, the next update puts it back in its section with its answer. Otherwise, answer the matching new question yourself, reading the answer across from Former items.

#The to-do list

The to-do list is the other half of the same idea. The checklist asks what an investigation of this kind should cover and is the same every time; a to-do is whatever this one needs next, in your own words, and no template could anticipate most of it: ring someone back on a number, chase the CCTV before it is overwritten, ask the roster office for the prior fortnight. It is the one running list of what still needs doing, fed from the checklist, from objects on the map and from anything you type here. The list is yours as a working tool; nothing on it reaches a report.

The panel is titled To do, with a count reading either "nothing yet" or "{n} of {n} left". Type into Add something to do… and click Add to raise one. Items stay in the order you added them. Each row carries its own tick, its text and a × that drops it. Ticking an item moves it into a collapsed Completed section beneath the list, which you can expand to check or untick; unticking moves it straight back. Adding, ticking and unticking an item each write a line to the Log. Dropping an item with the cross removes it without logging anything, because it was never done.

A to-do can also be raised from inside an object's form on the AcciMap, just under its notepad, for whatever you need to chase up before you can finish that object. It is the same list, not a separate one: it shows here too, with a from: <object> line you can click to jump straight to that object on the AcciMap, which is brought into view and flashed so you can see exactly which one it is (double-click the line to open its form instead). What still needs doing is visible whether you are working the to-do list or the map, and it is easy to hand a job to another investigator.

#The distribution list (on the Details tab)

The distribution list lives on the Details tab, which sits just before the Dashboard; the Dashboard only points to it. Distribution is who this investigation's outputs go to, as a table with a Title column and a Name column. Each row carries Move up, Move down and Remove this row, and + Row adds another beneath them. The list is appended automatically to the foot of every exported report, so it is never left off by hand. Copy distribution list puts the same rows on the clipboard as a table for pasting into an email or a document the app did not produce, with plain text alongside for anything that takes only text. A new investigation starts with whatever rows the edition ships as defaults, if any; existing investigations are never changed.

When this computer has distribution lists (from the organisation folder, edited on this computer; see Distribution lists), the panel adds Build from organisation lists…. It shows every list with a tick, none ticked to begin with: tick the lists this investigation needs, and the rows common to every list, then the rows that would result, each role once. A row to check before use (one that depends on the aircraft type or the port, for example) is shown amber with its note, there and in the list once applied. Replace the list starts afresh; Add to the list keeps what is there and adds only the rows it does not already hold. Each row is the role's title and the name of the person who holds it, which is what a template's distribution table prints. A line under the list then says what it was built from, the lists' revision and the date, and says so when the organisation's lists have moved on since.

The investigation keeps its own copy: nothing in it changes when someone edits the organisation's lists. Refresh from organisation lists… shows what differs now, row by row (added, removed or changed), and takes only what you tick. Rows you typed yourself are never offered. A build and a refresh each write one Log entry naming the lists and the number of rows, never a person, and the list is never part of what the AI is given to draft from.

#Bringing finalised text into Word

Copy text sits on every finalised section of the Draft report tab (in the section's own controls and above it in Read as a document) and under each object's Analysis paragraph on the map. It puts the wording on the clipboard as plain text, ready to paste under the matching heading in Word: the section's own title, the bold and italic markers and the review notes are left behind, and each paragraph comes across as one paragraph, so it takes the template's own style. A placeholder the drafting left for you to resolve, such as [NOT ESTABLISHED: ...], comes across as it is, so the gap stays visible.

This is the way to work with a chat AI: draft each object's paragraph with its Analysis prompt, finalise it, and paste it into the document as you build it.

#Emails

Emails lists the email templates built into the app until you put your own in the Email subfolder of your organisation folder (Settings > Organisation > Organisation folder); from then on it lists only the folder's. A Word document (.docx or .dotx) or a plain-text file (.txt) both work. Choose one from the Email list under the header (a drop-down, grouped by stage and showing names only) to see it filled in from Details. The header counts them and says whether they are built in or from your Email folder. Maintain templates, at the right of the header, holds Reload templates, Open email templates folder (the Email subfolder in the system file manager) and the Token reference. The details the email asks for sit in a box headed Details for this email, in as many columns as fit, above the email itself, which runs the full width of the tab: a Subject line and a Message line, each with its own Copy button and its result beside it. Copy message puts a close copy on the clipboard, keeping tables with their borders and column widths, paragraph spacing, fonts, bold and italic, for pasting into Outlook. A template's empty lines go across as space between its paragraphs rather than as empty paragraphs, so the message pastes into Outlook or Word with one gap between paragraphs, not two, whichever paste option you choose. For an interview invite, Copy message then offers "Set up this interview in Evidence?", showing the role, name, date, time, venue and investigators it would record; Add planned interview (or Update planned interview when one matches) creates it, and Not now leaves Evidence alone. Nothing is added until you choose it. Esc is Not now, and returns you to Copy message. The email has no scroll box of its own: the page scrolls as one. Copy subject copies the subject line on its own, and says so beside its button. Reload templates reads the folder afresh after you add a template. Choosing another template clears what you typed for the recipient and the request and keeps your name and role. The app never sends email: you complete the message and send it yourself. To write one of your own, see Making your own email templates; to change or add a template safely, see Maintaining your organisation's templates.

In a template, a detail the record holds is written as its name in double braces, such as {{reference}}, {{title}}, {{occurrence_date}} or {{lead_investigator}}; the Token reference lists every one. Dates come out as DD/MM/YYYY. A token the record has no value for yet shows as a highlighted blank named for it, such as [Date of joining], in both routes, as do the template's own blanks: text in [square brackets], in «guillemets», or a line of underscores. If the template's first line reads "Subject: ...", that line is the subject rather than part of the message. A template with no Subject line gets one built from its name (without any leading number), the reference and the title, with the interviewee's role in brackets after the name when an interview is chosen, such as Interview record for review (Witness), and Copy subject copies that.

A template that opens "Good ..." with a morning or afternoon drop-down, or uses {{greeting}}, shows a Greeting choice. It starts as Good morning before midday and Good afternoon after, and you can change it.

An invitation template (one with "invite" in its name that uses interview details) has no Interview list. It asks instead for the interviewee's role (suggestions from this investigation and common roles), the interviewee's name, the proposed date and time, and the venue (a place or a Teams meeting, with the venues you have used before offered). It also asks for the lead investigator and the other investigators, suggested from Details (or from the planned interview when one matches) and yours to change, because the interview team shifts during an investigation. They fill {{lead_investigator}} and {{assisting_investigators}} in the invitation only; other templates keep reading Details. The email fills from those. Copy message then adds the interview to Evidence > Interviews, or updates the one for the same person, with its role, name, date, time, venue and interviewing team, marked (planned) until you record it, and says so under the preview. The Log notes it.

Some tokens need a little more from you. A template that uses an interview's details ({{interviewee_name}}, {{interview_date}}, {{interview_time}} and the interviewee's background rows such as {{interviewee_base}}) shows an Interview list (an invitation excepted), and fills from the one you pick. Details you type for this email appear only when the template uses them: {{recipient_name}}, {{sender_name}} and {{sender_role}} (both kept on this computer for next time, the role starting as Safety Investigator), {{request_details}} (a box of several lines, each becoming a paragraph in the message), {{feedback_due_date}} (today plus a number of days you can change, 7 to start with), {{feedback_timeframe}}, {{report_type}} and {{interview_venue}}, which an invitation asks for with the rest of the interview. In a Word template, a content control whose tag or title is one of these names is filled the same way. Anything the record cannot fill is named in one amber line above the Subject, such as 2 details the record does not hold yet, and stays highlighted in the message. The exception is the table an invitation sends the interviewee to complete (base, time on type, total time and the like): those cells stay plain and empty, are never listed and are never highlighted, and still fill where the record holds a value.

#Making your own email templates

The Emails section on Admin & Comms starts with the email templates built into the app. You can change any of them, or add your own, without a developer: an email template is an ordinary Word document or text file in a folder you choose.

The built-in emails

  • Opening: Notification of an investigation, Request for information
  • Interviews: Interview invitation, Interview record for review
  • Reporting: Draft report for review, Final report distribution
  • Closing: Investigation closed

They are written to fit any organisation and any industry, so read each one before you send it. A phrase in [square brackets] is there for you to complete or delete, and stays highlighted until you do.

Where your own templates go

  1. Choose an organisation folder in Settings > Organisation > Organisation folder. Email templates live in its Email subfolder.
  2. Use Copy built-in templates to folder on the same page. It makes the Email subfolder if it is missing and puts a copy of every built-in email in it (it adds missing files and never replaces one), so you start from working examples.
  3. Edit those copies, or add new files beside them. Maintain templates > Open email templates folder on Admin & Comms opens the subfolder, and Reload templates reads it again.
  4. IF the Email subfolder holds any template
    • THEN the Emails list shows only the folder's templates, and none of the built-in ones. That is why the copy step comes first.

File types and names

  • A plain-text file (.txt) is the simplest: open it in Notepad or any text editor. Put a blank line between paragraphs and keep each paragraph on one line, because a single line break stays a line break in the message.
  • A Word document (.docx or .dotx) keeps its formatting (tables, bold, fonts) when the message is copied into Outlook. A .doc file is not read.
  • The file name, without its extension, is what the Emails list shows. A leading number ("03 ") is dropped from the list but sets the order, so number your files to keep them in the order you work.
  • To replace a built-in email, give your file exactly the same name (capital letters do not matter), such as "03 Interview invitation.txt". Any other name is a new template.
  • An interview invitation needs "invite" or "invitation" in its name and an interview token in its text. The app then asks for the interviewee, date, time and venue, and offers to plan the interview in Evidence.

The first lines

  • IF the first line reads "Stage: ..."
    • THEN the Emails list files the template under that stage. Any name works (Opening, Interviews, Reporting). Stages appear in the order of their first template, and a template with no stage goes under Other, at the end.
  • IF the next line (or the first, with no stage) reads "Subject: ..."
    • THEN that line is the subject, and Copy subject copies it.
  • Neither line is part of the message.

Tokens

A token is a name in double braces that the app replaces with a value when you pick the template. Case, spaces and underscores are ignored, so {{Occurrence Date}} works as well as {{occurrence_date}}. A token the investigation has no value for yet, or a misspelt one, shows as a highlighted blank and is listed under the preview. These are the tokens the app fills:

{{reference}} {{title}} {{occurrence_date}} {{occurrence_time}} {{location}} {{flight}} {{registration}} {{aircraft_type}} {{departure}} {{destination}} {{lead_investigator}} {{classification}} {{version}} {{publish_date}} {{operator}} {{occurrence_time_basis}} {{launch_date}} {{investigators}} {{status}} {{assisting_investigators}} {{interviewees}} {{greeting}} {{recipient_name}} {{sender_name}} {{sender_role}} {{feedback_due_date}} {{feedback_timeframe}} {{report_type}} {{interview_venue}} {{request_details}} {{interviewee_name}} {{interviewee_role}} {{interview_date}} {{interview_time}}

What each one fills, and which ones ask you to type or pick something, is in the token reference.

A worked example

To add a request for site access, make a text file called "02 Site access request.txt" in the Email subfolder, containing:

Stage: Opening
Subject: Site access for investigation {{reference}}

{{greeting}} {{recipient_name}},

As part of investigation {{reference}} into {{title}}, I would like to visit {{location}} on [proposed date] to look at the area and take photographs.

Please let me know whether that date suits, and who I should report to when I arrive.

Kind regards,
{{sender_name}}
{{sender_role}}
  1. Choose Reload templates on Admin & Comms. The email appears under Opening, after Request for information.
  2. Pick it. The reference, title and location fill from Details, the greeting starts as Good morning or Good afternoon by the clock, and boxes appear for the recipient's name and your own name and role.
  3. [proposed date] stays highlighted. Copy message, paste it into a new message, replace the highlighted date, and send it yourself. The app never sends email.

For changing templates an organisation shares, testing a change and rolling one back, see Maintaining your organisation's templates.

#Maintaining your organisation's templates

Your organisation's emails and Word documents are templates the app fills from the investigation. When your processes change, you change the templates, and you do not need a developer to do it. The app carries a built-in copy of every template, so a change that goes wrong never leaves you without one: the worst case is that the app keeps using the built-in copy and tells you why. This topic is the routine for changing a template safely. Where the templates live is described under Organisation folder.

How the app chooses a copy

  • The organisation folder has four subfolders: Email, Reports, Forms (the organisation's own forms) and Checklists.
  • A Word file in Reports, Forms or Checklists with the same file name as a built-in template replaces it (capital letters are ignored). A file with any other name is not used, and nothing says so, so keep the name exactly.
  • Email works differently. As soon as the Email subfolder holds any template, the Emails list shows only the folder's templates and none of the built-in ones. A folder file with the same name as a built-in one replaces it.
  • Only .docx and .dotx files are read, and .txt as well for email. A file saved as .doc is invisible to the app. Word's own lock files (names starting ~$) are ignored, and so is any file over 2 MB.
  • The folder is read again each time the Settings page opens, each time the Emails section opens and before every export. A file you drop in is used straight away, with no restart.

Editing an email template

An email template is a Word document or a text file with tokens in it. A token is a detail written in double braces, such as {{reference}}. When someone picks the template on Admin & Comms, the app swaps each token for the value from the investigation, shows a preview, and puts the finished message on the clipboard. The app never sends the email.

  1. Copy the current file to a working folder outside the organisation folder, and keep the original. It is your way back.
  2. Edit the working copy in Word. Editing the file in the shared folder makes every save live to everyone, half-finished or not.
  3. Change the words around the tokens freely. Keep each token spelled as in the token tables below. Case, spaces and underscores do not matter ({{Occurrence Date}} works), but a different word matches nothing.
  4. IF the first line reads "Subject: ..."
    • THEN that line is the subject. Keep it as the very first line.
  5. Save it as .docx or .dotx, or .txt for a plain message.
  6. Test it (see Test before you roll out), then copy it over the old one in the folder.

Adding an email template

  1. Write it in Word or in a text file, using the tokens you need.
  2. Name the file for what it is, because the file name without its extension is what the Emails drop-down shows (a leading number is dropped). An interview invitation needs the word "invite" in its name, since that is how the app knows to ask for the invitee's role, name, date, time and venue.
  3. Optionally, give it a stage. Put a first line reading "Stage: Interviews" (any name you like) ahead of the Subject line; the Emails drop-down groups templates by stage, in the order the first template of each stage appears, and the line is not part of the message. A template with no stage is listed under Other, at the end. A folder copy of a built-in template keeps the built-in one's stage unless it has its own Stage line. A template pack sets the stage of its built-in templates directly.
  4. Test it, then put it in the Email subfolder and choose Check again on Admin & Comms.
  5. IF the Email subfolder was empty until now
    • THEN the built-in email templates leave the list as soon as this file arrives. To keep them, first use Copy built-in templates to folder in Settings > Organisation > Organisation folder.

What the app does with an email template

  • A token the investigation has no value for yet, and a token the app does not know (a misspelling), both show as a highlighted blank named for the token and are listed under the preview as not filled. Nothing is dropped silently.
  • The template's own blanks (text in [square brackets], text in «guillemets», a line of three or more underscores) also show highlighted, so they are not sent by mistake.
  • In a Word template, a content control whose tag matches a token name is filled like that token. An untagged control still showing its placeholder text is filled by its title or its placeholder text, if that matches a token name. An untagged control someone has already answered keeps its text. A control that holds whole paragraphs or tables is left alone.
  • A greeting drop-down ("Good" followed by a morning or afternoon choice) or {{greeting}} shows a Greeting choice, which starts as Good morning before midday and Good afternoon after. Keep the drop-down's entries as they are.
  • A file the app cannot read falls back to the built-in template of the same name when there is one, with a note. With no built-in of that name, it is listed with the reason it could not be read.

Editing a report, checklist or form template

These are Word templates the app fills and opens as a new document (Open filled on Admin & Comms, and the report export on the Draft report tab). The app does not rewrite your template. It fills a copy, and it finds each place to fill by what the template shows, in this order of preference:

  • a content control's title or tag (its name in Word)
  • the placeholder text the control shows, such as "Occurrence Date"
  • the label in front of it, in the same paragraph or the cell before it
  • a heading's text, where the heading uses the template's heading style
  • a table by the words its first row starts with (the findings tables, the distribution table)
  • a checklist question by its wording, ignoring capitals, spacing and punctuation, within its own section
  • last of all, for a few boxes with nothing else to go on, the box's position among its neighbours

Which headings and controls matter differs from one organisation's templates to another's, so this topic cannot list them. The rule is that whatever the template showed when it was set up has to still be there. The app also fills, where the template has them, the Table of Abbreviations under its heading (built from your Abbreviations list), the findings tables (the first findings table is the pattern, copied once per finding), the distribution rows, the similar events register, front-page tick boxes (found by the words after each box), and an Appendices part added at the end with the maps on landscape pages.

  1. Use Copy built-in templates to folder (Settings > Organisation > Organisation folder) so the folder holds the built-in file under its right name. Copy that file out to a working folder outside the organisation folder, and edit the copy.
  2. Keep the file name exactly, and keep the format .docx or .dotx.
  3. Change wording, logos, fonts, colours and layout as you need. Keep every heading the template had, in its heading style, with the same text. Keep every content control, with its title and tag, and every table the app fills. Keep each checklist question's wording. Keep the choices in each drop-down: the app picks the entry that matches the value, and a value no entry matches is not written.
  4. A new heading or box of your own is fine. The app leaves it as you wrote it and does not tell you it is not filled.
  5. Test it (see Test before you roll out), then copy it over the old one in the folder.

What breaks a report template

  • IF a heading is renamed, deleted, or retyped as bold Normal text instead of the heading style
    • THEN the app cannot find its place under it.
  • IF a content control is deleted or replaced by plain text
    • THEN the place is lost, unless its placeholder text or label still identifies it.
  • IF a checklist question is reworded, or a table's first row is changed
    • THEN the app cannot match the answer or the table, until the checklist template in the app is changed to the same wording and each open investigation's checklist is updated to it from the Dashboard.
  • IF the file is saved as .doc, or under another name
    • THEN it is not seen and the built-in copy is used, with no message.
  • IF the look of a style changes (font, size, spacing, colour)
    • THEN the filled text takes the new look. This is safe while the styles keep their names and the headings still use them. Fonts are not stored in the file, so a computer without a font substitutes another and the page breaks may move.

What you see when a folder copy no longer provides a place:

  1. The app compares the folder copy with the built-in one. A place the built-in copy finds exactly once must be found exactly once in the folder copy.
  2. IF a place is missing, or found more than once
    • THEN the folder copy is refused and the built-in one is used. The template's row in Settings shows Built-in, with a message beginning "The folder copy ... has no single place for:" and naming each place. Every export from that template carries the same note.
  3. IF every place is found
    • THEN the row reads, for example, "Folder: 36 of 36 places found". If the built-in copy's saved date is later than the folder copy's, the row also says the built-in copy is newer.

Two limits. The check only asks for what the built-in copy finds, so a heading or box you add is never reported. And an email template has no check at all: a problem shows only when it is filled, as a highlighted blank and an entry under the preview.

When a value fits no place (a drop-down with no matching entry, a recipient with no table), the export still runs and lists the value under "Not placed in the template" in the document, after the template's own content, with a note on the tab. Nothing is lost. A contents list is built by Word, so update the field in the filled document.

Test before you roll out

  1. Make a test folder away from the shared one, with the subfolder your change belongs in (Email, Reports, Forms or Checklists) and the changed file in it.
  2. On your own computer, choose the test folder in Settings > Organisation > Organisation folder with Choose folder.
  3. Read the template's row.
    • IF it reads Built-in with a message naming places
      • THEN the template was refused. Fix those places and check the folder again.
    • ELSE IF it reads Folder with every place found
      • THEN the app accepts it. Go on.
  4. Open the worked example investigation from the start screen and go to Admin & Comms. Use Open filled on a document template. For an email, pick it, read the preview and the line of anything not filled.
  5. For a report template, also run the report export from the Draft report tab. The example has no folder of its own, so its copies go to a folder under the system's temporary files, and the section names the place.
  6. Open the result in Word. Check the front page, every heading you changed, the tables, the tick boxes, and the end of the document for a "Not placed in the template" list.
  7. When it is right, copy the file into the shared folder, and choose your usual folder again in Settings.

Rolling back

  1. For a Reports, Forms or Checklists template, remove the folder copy, or move it out of the folder. The built-in copy is used at once.
  2. For an email template, put back your saved earlier copy, or remove the file. If it was the last file in the Email subfolder, the built-in email templates return.

The built-in templates are part of the app and nothing you do in the folder can damage them. The app only reads the folder, apart from Copy built-in templates to folder, which adds files that are missing and never replaces one.

Sharing one folder across investigators

  1. Put the organisation folder on a shared drive every investigator can read. Let only the people who look after the templates write to it, because who can change a template is decided by who can write to the folder.
  2. Each investigator chooses that folder once, in Settings > Organisation > Organisation folder on their own computer.
  3. Change a template by copying the new file in. Each investigator picks it up the next time they open the Settings page or the Emails section, or run an export. An investigation that is already open does not need closing.
  4. IF the shared drive is out of reach
    • THEN the built-in templates are used until it comes back.

What not to do

  • Do not edit the only copy of a template, or edit in the shared folder.
  • Do not rename the file, or save it as .doc, .pdf or a macro-enabled format.
  • Do not rename or retype headings, or turn them into body text.
  • Do not delete a content control to tidy the page.
  • Do not reword a checklist question, or change a table's first row.
  • Do not put the first file in the Email subfolder without keeping the built-in emails you still need.
  • Do not send an email with a highlighted blank left in it.

#Contacts

Contacts is a scratch pad of the people this investigation needs, inside your organisation or outside it. + Contact adds a card with a name, a role or organisation, a phone number, an email address, what to contact them about, and notes; × removes it, and Undo brings it back. The list is saved with the investigation and goes into no report, export or prompt.

3Log

Your running sheet, plus the entries the app adds itself when a decision is made.

The running sheet: a timestamped record of investigation activity, displayed as message bubbles grouped by day, each day being the calendar day on this computer's clock, the same one the times use. Type a line, press Enter, and it is on the record. Entries cannot be edited afterwards (one added in error can be removed), which is what makes the log worth citing. The log is kept for looking back on: it is not sent to a drafting pass, it is not part of the Report view and it does not go out in an export.

The application writes to the same sheet as you work. A checklist question answered, a to-do added or ticked, an object created, renamed or removed, a test result recorded, evidence captured or finalised, an interview finalised, a draft landed, an audit run, a report exported, a standard of proof set or changed, a name or abbreviation decided, a shared-file conflict resolved, among others: each one appears as its own entry, marked "auto" and shown in a muted bubble. Typing in a text box is never logged, only the change it settles. An automatic entry cannot be edited or removed, which is the point of it. Both kinds stay on this tab, so you can follow the investigation's own course when you look back; neither reaches a draft or the report.

The log sits outside undo. Adding an entry is not an undo step, and Ctrl+Z never takes an entry away. Undoing a step that wrote automatic entries leaves them in place and adds one more, "Undone: …" followed by what was undone, and redoing it adds "Redone: …". Undoing a step nothing was logged for, such as a move or a burst of typing, adds nothing.

#Reading an entry

The tab opens on Manual, your own entries. Choose System for the entries the application wrote, or All for both together, at the top of the tab. An entry that names an object still on the map carries a → <object> link that shows it on the map; entries the application wrote have these too. An abbreviation entry carries a link to Names & Abbreviations. A manual entry also carries a ✕ for removing it; a system entry has no cross. Removing an entry asks first ("Remove this log entry?"), with Cancel the default, because a removed entry is gone for good: Undo cannot restore it.

#Adding an entry

The composer sits at the foot of the tab: a box reading Log an action, request, receipt, or decision… (Enter to add), with an Add entry button at the far right for the same thing. Either way the line lands on today's date, in its own bubble, timestamped to the minute. The scope of the investigation (the questions it must answer, and what it will not examine) is worth recording here as an early entry, so there is a dated record of what was decided.

4Evidence

Everything gathered for the investigation, under five sub-tabs: Names & Abbreviations, Interviews, Safety reports, Captured material and Similar events.

#Names & Abbreviations

Names & Abbreviations, first on the Evidence tab, holds the words this investigation uses for people and for short forms. It has two parts with two jobs, and each part says whether it prints. The People part keeps names out of every prompt and never prints. The Abbreviations part reaches the drafting model and prints in the report. One search box at the top filters both parts, by name, role, short form or what it stands for.

People (does not print)

Who is who is the investigation's list of the people the interviews name (interviewees, support people and the people they talk about) and anyone you add, each with the role the report calls them by. The investigators are not listed; their names are still kept from every prompt. Add a person takes a name, a role and an optional note of your own, which is never sent and never printed. The interviewees and support people appear from the interview attendees and speakers without you entering them; edit them there. Each name on the list is swapped for its role in every prompt.

Open on a person's row opens their panel: the name, the role they are described as, other spellings, a category, their organisation, your notes, how the name appears in prompts and where it is named, and a warning when another entry looks like the same person.

Names found in the interviews holds the names the transcripts, notes and attendees mention that are not on the list yet; nothing outside the interviews is read for it. It opens by itself while it has names to decide. Each row offers the four choices every found word on this page has, in a fixed order: Person (key P) gives it a role, Abbreviation (A) makes it a short form, Description (D) describes it as a word that is not a person, and Dismiss (X) leaves it as written. Click a row or move with the arrow keys, then press the letter; once you confirm, the next word takes the focus. A name that reads as a misheard spelling of someone already listed says who it looks like and offers Same as (key S), which adds it to that person's spellings. Words that only begin a sentence are tucked under Show N weaker matches and are not counted, and a long list shows its first rows with Show all. Where an interview title or a typed role is not one the app knows, a question row at the top asks whether it is a role (It's a role) or a person (It's a person). Words treated as not people, closed below, holds the places and organisations you have described, whose descriptions reach prompts as glossary lines, and the words you dismissed, each with Bring back; Add a term is there too.

After each decision and each Bring back, a message at the foot of the page says what happened and where the word is now, with Show to go to it and Undo to reverse the step. It stays until your next action or Close. A word brought back is offered again only where an interview still open or the record mentions it; a finalised interview suggests nothing, so when nothing does, the message says there is nothing to decide.

A transcript sometimes writes a short form as if it were a name: "the Nigs was unserviceable" for NIGS. A name's Abbreviation opens a strip with the short form upper-cased and a box for what it stands for, filled in only when the record spells it out somewhere. Add to abbreviations moves it to this investigation's abbreviations and keeps the word as the record writes it as another spelling, so those mentions still count. Remove on that abbreviation later brings the word back as a name.

Abbreviations (prints)

This investigation's abbreviations are the short forms defined for this investigation, and they travel with the file. The Table of Abbreviations lists each one the report uses, and the first mention is written in full, "Runway End Safety Area (RESA)", then "RESA" after that. The full form carries capital initials wherever it shows or prints; what you typed is kept as you typed it. Click a meaning to change it. Add an abbreviation takes a short form of 2 to 8 letters and digits and what it stands for; nothing is ever guessed.

Found in the record lists the capitalised short forms the record uses that are on no list yet, most used first. It reads the evidence items (extracts included), the safety reports, the timeline, the map objects, the narratives, and each interview's transcript and notes while it is open, or only its summary once it is finalised. Drafts, the notebook, the checklist and the to-dos are not read. Flight numbers, registrations, document codes, people's names, ports and anything ending in a digit are never offered. A short form seen only once is folded under Show seen once and not counted. Where the record itself writes "accelerate-stop distance available (ASDA)", Abbreviation, the filled choice on these rows, offers that meaning and says where it came from. A short form found only inside a quoted extract can be defined too: it goes in the Table of Abbreviations, the quote is never rewritten, and nothing in a quote counts as the first use. A People term that is a capitalised short form, such as NIGS held as a term, is listed here as held as a People term, with Move to abbreviations to move it across in one step.

Your Settings list of abbreviations applies as well. Known from Settings lists the short forms on that list that this record uses. Where the record gives one a different meaning, "the risk assessment (RA)" against Settings' "Resolution Advisory", the row says the record says otherwise, and Different meaning here sets this investigation's own meaning. This investigation's meaning wins here and nowhere else: the prompts, the Table of Abbreviations and the first use in this report use it, and every other investigation keeps the Settings meaning. To change the machine's meaning, edit it in Settings.

A short form found here has Person and Description as well, for a word that is really a name or a term. Dismiss marks a word that only looks like one (a callout written in capitals, say) as not an abbreviation. It is remembered in the file, left as written, and neither this page nor the record check offers it again; it is listed under Not abbreviations with Bring back. The Draft report tab's record check has a quick Add for a short form on no list, which writes to this investigation's abbreviations in the same way, and its own Not an abbreviation.

Abbreviation and Add an abbreviation carry a tick box, Also add to my Settings list, unticked; an abbreviation already on the machine's list offers Also add to Settings on its row instead. Ticked, the short form is added to the list on this machine as well, for every later investigation. Every change on the page is one undo step with a line in the Log; undo reverts the investigation and leaves a Settings entry the tick box added, which you remove in Settings if it is not wanted.

#Safety reports

Reports submitted by the people involved: safety reports, written statements, self-reports. Paste each one in as it was written, with the submitter's role, the date, and the report reference so it stays traceable. Do not tidy the wording; how someone described what happened is itself evidence. These are first-person accounts written close to the event, often before anyone has been interviewed, which makes them some of the best narrative material an investigation has, and they feed report drafting the same way interviews do. They need no writing-up pass of their own: a submitted report is already prose, in the submitter's own words.

Each report is marked as submitted under a confidential or just-culture scheme by default. That flag travels with it into drafting, where the model is told it may use the account to understand what happened and attribute it by role, but must not quote it verbatim or reproduce identifying detail from it. Those protections are the reason people report at all, and a drafted report is a document that gets distributed. Untick it only for a statement given without them.

+ Report adds a new sub-tab, titled by the report once you give it one. A report submitted under a confidential or just-culture scheme carries a small ● lock mark on its sub-tab, so the protected ones are visible at a glance without opening each in turn.

The safety report form

Report details carries the fields that place the report: Title, Submitted by (role), Submitted, and Reference. A Link to the original field and its Open original button sit beside them, for a report that is also filed somewhere else, such as a safety database or a shared drive, so you can get back to the original without hunting for it. Open original opens the link in your browser once it holds a full web address (starting http or https). The confidentiality tick is below them, as described above.

Report text is the single box the account goes into, pasted in as it was written. Supporting material is the same capture panel an object carries on the map, for anything that came with the report itself. Delete report, at the foot, asks before it removes the report.

#Interviews: the record

The names an interview mentions are kept on the Names & Abbreviations page, first on the Evidence tab, which swaps each one for its role in every prompt.

One record per interview. + Interview adds a new sub-tab. Double-click a sub-tab to rename it to the interviewee's name once you have it; Delete interview, at the foot, removes the interview after asking.

The record section opens with its finalise control: a Finalise interview button while the record is still open, or a green Finalised state with Re-open beside it once it is settled. Anything that can be finalised carries a stripe down its left edge: green once it is finalised, amber while it is not, so what is still outstanding stands out. Below it sits Attendees (Lead interviewer, Assisting interviewer, Interviewee and Support person), then the transcript, the note windows and your own notes.

Each interview has two pages, switched at the top: Interview, for everything before a record exists, and Record, the record itself. A new interview opens on Interview. One that is finalised, one recorded with a transcript, or one not recorded whose must-cover questions are all answered opens on Record. A strip at the top says what to do now, with a button for it: Start the interview moves to During, and Interview over: go to the record moves to the Record page. Interview has two views. Before the interview shows the invitation where one was sent from Admin & Comms, the attendees, Preparation, a notepad for preparing your questions, and the interviewee background table, in the rows the background template in Settings sets. Start the interview switches to During the interview: the interview guide beside the notepad, or the notepad above the guide in a narrow window beside a video call.

The guide is the Opening template in Settings, laid out in sections with a jump list and a reminder at the top. Each section is a few dot points to say in your own words, the questions that must be covered, answered Yes, No or N/A, and an If stuck line with words to fall back on. Preparation is answered the same way. A new interview gets the lists in Settings as they are then; an interview keeps the lists it started with. If you edited the Opening template yourself, your version stays in use: Reset it in Settings to take the built-in guide.

The notepad writes the same boxes as the Record page. Part of the record holds the three note windows, Free recall, Additional questions and Follow-up questions, which go to AI only to draft the summary, with every name swapped for a role. Your own notes holds Your notes, the Record page's Investigator's notes, which never goes to AI: prepare your questions there as a ticked list and tick them off as you go. In a recorded interview Your notes leads, since the transcript will be the record, and the note windows wait behind Add your own account. In one that is not recorded, Free recall leads: your notes are the record. Once the record is finalised the guide's answers, the notepad and the background are shown read only; Preparation stays live.

An interview here is a record, and the app asks one question about it first: was it recorded? Recorded means the transcript is the record (see Transcripts and speaker roles). Not recorded means your notes are the record: write them up while the interview is fresh. If it turns out there was a recording after all, importing a transcript switches the interview to Recorded. A new interview starts as Recorded unless your organisation's interview practice says otherwise (Settings > Organisation > Shared practice); you can switch any interview either way.

#Transcripts and speaker roles

A recorded interview's record is its corrected transcript, and the steps across the top of the record section say where it is up to: Import, Replace names with roles, Correct against the audio, Finalise the record, then the Summary where your organisation uses one. An interview that was not recorded has Write up (the three note windows), Finalise the record and Summary. Each step shows its state, so what is left to do is never a matter of which box happens to be open.

Import transcript… brings in a transcript as a .docx or .vtt file (the Teams download format), or paste it straight in. It is what was actually said rather than what you caught and remembered. Your own notes sit alongside it as your observations. The transcript box and the note boxes grow with their text to about the height of an A4 page (less in a small window) before they scroll, and can still be dragged taller or shorter.

What you imported is kept exactly as it arrived, names and all, under Original transcript above the box you work in. You are correcting a working copy; the source stays where it is, so a correction can always be checked against what the recording actually produced, and Show original transcript opens it. Nothing edits the original and nothing sends it anywhere: only the working copy is finalised, exported or read when a report is drafted, so a mishearing you have already fixed cannot come back as evidence. Remove transcript takes both away, since a source with nothing left to compare against is only clutter.

Give every speaker in a transcript a role in the table under it. Each speaker then appears in the record, the report and anything sent to a model by that role, not by the name the transcript gave them. The mapping is deliberately yours to make: a model that guesses which speaker was the captain can attribute a remark to the wrong person, and nothing downstream would catch it. The fold The record by role shows the transcript as the record will read it. Then decide every name the Words to decide card lists, and replace the names with roles (see Replacing names and correcting the transcript).

#Replacing names and correcting the transcript

Replace names with roles writes the roles into the working copy itself, once. Every speaker label becomes the role you gave that speaker, and every name said inside a turn that the investigation ties to a role (the People register, the attendees and the speakers' own names) becomes that role in square brackets, "[the Pilot in command]", the way a transcript already marks "[inaudible]", so a reader can see where the record was edited. From then on the working copy names nobody, and the Words to decide card has no name left to list for it. The prompts still swap names on the way out as a second guard. A speaker whose name no attendee and no register entry already covers is added to the People register with the role you gave them, in the same step, so their name is still swapped wherever else it turns up.

It waits until it can do the whole job: a speaker without a role, or a name the card still lists with no decision, holds it back, and the button says which. Pressing it with a name undecided brings the card into view. Give the person a role, describe the word, or dismiss it, then press it again. It is one step, so Ctrl+Z puts the names back straight away. After that the original is the only copy with names, by design; importing the transcript again is the way back, and it discards every correction, which the import asks you to confirm.

Do it before correcting, not after. Once names are replaced the plain-text box gives way to Correct the transcript: the original on the left, read-only, in its own turns, and the working copy on the right, one turn at a time. Correct a turn's words in place where the transcription is wrong, because Teams mishears names, readbacks, numbers and technical words. Change a turn's speaker from the roles in use (or Other… for a new one, which must be a role: a person's name is refused) where Teams gave the line to the wrong person; a turn given to the speaker beside it joins their turn. Tab moves to the next turn. The time beside each turn is where to find it in the recording; the audio itself stays outside the app.

A turn whose words or speaker differ from the original carries an edited mark with the original's words under it, and the count of edited turns shows in the steps and on the original transcript card. Nothing is stored for the marks: the original and the working copy are the comparison. Hide original gives the working copy the full width. A turn cannot be emptied, and turns are not added or removed here.

Under the workspace, Check against audio asks a model where the transcription is probably wrong, so you listen to those places rather than the whole recording. The model never hears the audio and never sees a name: it is sent this working copy by role, with the interview's role, date and attendees by role and the abbreviations and described terms it uses, and nothing else (not your notes, the three note windows, the original or the event overview; the What is sent page lists it). It returns a list, each item with the turn's time, the words as the transcript has them and what was probably said, and a few questions where it could not tell. Every item is checked against the working copy before it is shown: one whose words are not in the turn at its time is listed under Could not be matched and cannot be accepted. Accept writes the proposed words into that turn (one step, so Ctrl+Z takes it back), Reject sets it aside, and Ask opens a box under the item for your own note, which stays on screen for this sitting and is not sent anywhere; act on it with the turn's own controls. A question has only Resolve. With Connected AI the button runs the check; with Copy and paste it is Copy check prompt and a box to paste the whole reply into, read and checked the same way; with No AI it is not offered. It waits until the names are replaced and every name the card lists is decided, and is not offered once the record is finalised. Nothing from the check is saved: the Log records one line when a check lands, and Finalise records how many corrections were accepted from it.

Replacing names changes the record's text, so a finalised interview summary drafted before it can go out of date with the record, as any change to the record does. Where the only names were the speakers and attendees, the summary reads exactly the same text and stays current.

#Writing the interview up

You write the interview up in three windows. Free recall is what the interviewee told you in their own words, before you asked anything, in the order they told it. Additional questions is what you asked once the free recall was finished, and what they answered. Follow-up questions is what is still open: what to put to this person again, or to check with someone else. Under them is your own notes box, for what you observed rather than what was said. Where the interview was recorded the transcript is the record and the three windows wait behind Add your own notes, for your own additions to it.

Where the interview was run by another investigator, add their notes with Add notes from another investigator rather than pasting them into your own box, so whose account is whose stays clear in the record and in drafting.

Investigator's notes, at the foot of the record, has its own small toolbar (B, I and • List) for notes written in your own words rather than in the structured windows above, formatted enough to stay readable without being another document to maintain.

#Finalising an interview, and reading it back

Finalise interview when the record says what was said. Report drafting is told the account is settled rather than still moving, and the page stops being a form: the boxes give way to the record itself, read as one document: who was interviewed and when, the background you noted, then the corrected transcript or the three sections in order, then your own notes, with anything you left empty simply not there.

From then on, clicking the interview opens that document straight away rather than the form it was filled in on. A recorded interview carries a long transcript and a page of notes, so a small control above it chooses what to read: Summary, Transcript, Notes or Both, which is where it opens, with the interview summary leading and the record under it. The original transcript stays reachable under the document for comparison. The speaker's name at the start of each turn is shown in bold, here and in the transcript box while it is being edited, so the finished interview is easy to follow. The bold is only how it is shown: the transcript itself is stored as plain text. An interview that was not recorded has no transcript, so Transcript is not offered.

Finalise interview becomes a green Finalised state with Re-open beside it. Re-open puts the boxes back, and is the only way to correct a finalised record: the lock is there so an account cannot drift underneath a draft that already quotes it.

#The interview summary

Under the record, each interview has an Interview summary card: a short written account of what the person said, in the order they said it, made from the record and finalised on its own. It has two headings, Account and Points raised by the interviewee (which reads None raised. when there were none). The date, attendees and interviewee are not typed into it; they come from the record. Write it yourself, or draft it and then correct it. Either way you read it against the record and finalise it, and nothing you did not finalise reaches a model.

What the card offers depends on the AI access set in Settings. With none, it is the editor alone. Otherwise it offers Copy prompt, Save prompt as file and a box to paste the chat tool's reply into, and Draft with AI, which sends the same prompt to the model in use. Draft with AI is shown at every tier, and says why where it cannot run (no connected model, or a licence without AI drafting, or a finalised summary, which you Re-open first). Under it are the model, where it sends, the size and cost of the request and What is sent. A draft keeps running if you move to the Interview page or another tab, and the chip at the top shows it with Stop. A reply lands unfinalised and marked with where it came from: Drafted by the model, or Pasted from chat. If you typed in the summary while the model worked, the draft is held beside it instead, with Use the draft and Keep mine. Anything the model was unsure of is listed beside the editor under Uncertainties for the investigator, for that sitting only; it is never saved with the summary.

While a name found in the interview has no decision on Words to decide, Copy prompt, Save prompt as file and Draft with AI are held, with Go to Words to decide; a reply you already have can still be pasted. The finder reads every notes box, Your notes included. Settings, AI drafting, Undecided names can set this to warn instead, and then each undecided name goes to AI as written.

Finalise, on the card, is available once the record itself is finalised, and the card says so while it is not. If the record (the transcript or a note window, not your own notes) changes after the summary was finalised, the summary is marked out of date until you read it against the record and finalise it again. Finalising and re-opening are written to the Log, and Ctrl+Z undoes either. Once a summary is finalised and current, it stands in for the record where report drafting reads that interview.

What is sent: a recorded interview sends the corrected transcript with each speaker by role, and the three note windows (free recall, additional questions, follow-up questions) where you have filled them in; one that was not recorded sends the note windows. Either way it carries the interview's date and the attendees by role, with every person named swapped for a role, and the writing rules for faithful summaries. It never carries your own notes, notes from another investigator or the original transcript. It goes only when you press Draft with AI or Copy prompt, even while the record is still open. The What is sent page lists it, and a small tag on the card, the record and your notes says which of them reach a model.

#Captured material

Evidence pasted into an object's form, or captured from the timeline or directly, is held once: it shows here too, as a grid of tiles that fills the width of the window, so you see what rests on what without leaving the map. The grid runs newest capture first, within each group, so the file you just dropped in is the one you see.

Each tile carries its caption as its title and the material itself below it: an image as a picture, a text extract as its first few lines, and a file as an icon for its type (PDF, document, spreadsheet, audio, video) with its name. A text capture renders as formatted text rather than raw markdown, so a pasted quote reads as an indented extract. Rest the pointer on a tile, or move to it with Tab, and the rest of the record comes up over the material: category, significance, reference, when it was captured, whether it is finalised, and what rests on it; for a text extract, also whether it is verbatim and the line printed under it. Each object listed there is a jump: clicking it takes you to the object on the AcciMap, brought into view and flashed. Edit (Open, once finalised) opens the item's form, View shows the capture at full size, and Delete removes the item from the register after showing you everything that rests on it. Click a tile, or press Enter on it, to open the item's own form.

Group by, above the grid, chooses how it is split: Nothing (one grid, the default), Where it lives, Category, Lane, or Kind, each group headed with its count. Size beside it sets the tiles at Compact, Standard or Large; the grid fits as many across as the window allows, and Full size cards brings back the original cards with every detail on show. This computer remembers the size, but not the grouping. Evidence attached to an object, a timeline event or a safety report appears as the same small tile in its own form.

Captured material also files by category, shown as sub-tabs across the top, starting with All, each carrying a count of what is filed under it. Every category on the list is shown; one with nothing filed under it is greyed out, where it works as a prompt: nothing under CCTV yet, on an occurrence that happened in view of a camera. An Uncategorised chip appears while anything has no category. The category list itself is edited in Settings > Method and wording > Evidence categories: add, rename, reorder, or remove one, or reset back to the built-in list. To add one without leaving the page, use + Category at the end of the chip row: it turns into a text box, Enter adds the name and Escape cancels. A blank name, or one already on the list in any capitals, is refused with a note beside the box. The new category takes its place in the sorted row and is selected, so the next capture files straight into it. A category you add here is added to this machine's list, the one under Settings, Evidence categories, so it is offered in every investigation on this computer; it travels with a file to other machines once something is filed under it. An item always keeps its category as its own text, so renaming or removing one there only changes what the picker offers for the next item filed, never material already captured. Capture view on the Timeline files its pictures under a category of their own, Timeline captures, which is always on the list; a capture made before that category existed shows there too, recognised by its file name, with its file left as it was.

#Capturing: paste, drop and size limits

Add to the evidence register is the capture strip itself, a slim dashed row above the grid on All with the Select file… button at its right end; rest the pointer on it for the longer explanation. With a category sub-tab selected it reads Add to <category> instead, and anything that lands there is filed under that category from the moment it is captured. Click it and paste a screenshot or a block of text, or drag a file from Explorer and drop it on the strip (PDFs included; the strip lights up as the file comes over it). The capture panel on an object's own form, on a timeline event and on a safety report works the same way. Each capture takes a short note on why it matters.

Text keeps its shape on the way in. Paste from Word, Outlook or a web page and its headings, bold and italic, bulleted and numbered lists and tables come with it; a range pasted from Excel arrives as a table rather than a picture of one. Text pasted from a PDF has its broken lines joined back into paragraphs, while list items, headings, table rows and short lines such as an address keep their own lines. The item's form then says Broken lines joined, with an Undo (until the app is closed) that puts the paste back exactly as it came, and marking the item Verbatim puts it back too. A transcript, where lines open with a time or a speaker's label, is never joined. A very large formatted paste (over 8 MB) is refused, with a note to copy a smaller passage. Select file… on the capture strip opens the Windows file dialog, so files can be chosen rather than dragged: each is taken in exactly as if it had been dropped on the strip (a Word document is filed as a file, like any other), within the same size limits, and any that is refused says why.

Everything captured is a copy held inside the .iwsx file (files to 2 MB, images to 5 MB, pasted text to 1 MB), so nothing breaks if the originals move. Anything larger is refused. The app is a tool, not a repository: capture the relevant page or passage, not the manual, record where the original is held in the item's Reference, and keep whole documents in your organisation's investigation folder, which remains the system of record.

The investigation's size shows in the header from 10 MB, and turns amber from 25 MB. Past 50 MB the app asks before it adds another file or image, and at 100 MB it stops accepting new ones, with a message saying why. Nothing else is limited: you can still open, edit, save and export an investigation of any size, and write text into it freely.

#Verbatim, or my description

The application holds no recordings. Audio, CCTV and recorded data arrive as text somebody typed, so a CCTV item is a time and what happened at it, with a screenshot if it helps. That makes one distinction worth stating on every text extract, and each one carries it: Verbatim for the source's own words (a transcript, a quotation from a manual, the text of a message) and My description for your account of what you watched or heard. A report may quote the first. The second is already your interpretation, so a draft will summarise it and never put it in quotation marks or attribute it as anyone's speech. Anything you have not marked is treated as a description, because putting your words in someone else's mouth is the mistake you cannot defend and a reader cannot detect.

That note is for the drafting pass only. Where the record prints an extract itself (the Record tab, its Word export and an organisation template filled from it), the extract appears under its caption as written, with its headings (set as bold lines), bold, lists, quotations and tables kept, and the line breaks of text pasted from a PDF or an email joined back into paragraphs. A blank line still starts a new paragraph, and a line break the joining leaves alone stays a line break. A verbatim record keeps every line break the source has. The card, the form and Read in full show the extract the same way.

#Captures as report figures

An image or a text extract can also go into the report. Its form carries one tick, Include in the report as a figure, off until you set it, and the card then shows a Figure badge on its tile, so you can see at a glance which captures are headed for the document. A dropped file gets no tick, and its form says why: there is nothing in a file that can be put on a page, so capture the page you want as an image instead. A HEIC, HEIF or TIFF photo, or an SVG drawing, is refused when you paste or drop it, with a message to save it as JPEG or PNG first, since neither the screen nor Word can draw it. One an older version of IWS filed keeps its tick and its figure number, its card warns that it cannot be shown, and the Word export prints a framed placeholder naming it in the same place, so the numbering holds. A ticked capture is numbered in the order the report argues the objects the captures belong to, so Figure 1 is the first one a reader meets, whichever kind it is, and its caption and reference are what print under it. An image prints inside a thin frame with the caption below; an extract prints as the words themselves in a frame of the same weight, set out as the report body sets text out: a table as a Word table, a heading as a bold line, lists and quoted lines as they read on screen, with the reference as a source line under them. Write the caption as you want it read, because that is the wording that goes into the report. Figures in the report covers where each one lands.

#The evidence item form

The form is four headed sections in one scroll. The first is the capture itself, headed with its type (Image, Text extract, PDF and so on), exactly as it was captured and never editable here, because it is what was captured. An image shows at a readable size with its shape kept; click it, or View full size, to see it whole. A text extract grows with its content up to about half the window and then scrolls, fading at the foot while there is more to read; Read in full opens it in the viewer, at a readable width with its caption and reference under the text, and Copy text puts it on the clipboard. A file is one row with its type, name and size, and Open writes a copy into the investigation folder's evidence subfolder, beside exports (or a folder of its own in the system's temporary folder while the investigation has not been saved), marks it as downloaded the way an email attachment is, and opens it in whatever this computer uses for that kind of file. Where the drive cannot hold that mark (some network shares and USB sticks), Open says so. Your own PDF reader beats anything the app could show you.

Found a better copy of an image or file? Replace… in the capture's heading chooses one, or paste an image or drop a file on the capture itself. The new content shows at once, marked Replaced, not yet saved, and takes effect with Save like any other change in the form; Cancel discards it. The item keeps its caption, notes, category, reference, links and everything that rests on it, and the Log records the old and new file name and size. It is one undo step, and the same size limits apply as for a new capture. A finalised item must be re-opened first: press Re-open in its form, and Replace… becomes available; while the item is locked it is greyed out with the reason. A text extract is edited as text instead.

Open does that for files with nothing active in them: PDF, Word, Excel and PowerPoint in their current formats (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx), text, CSV and log files, Outlook and email messages, and PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP and WebP images. Older Office files (.doc, .xls, .ppt), macro-enabled ones, RTF, OpenDocument files, web pages, SVG drawings, HEIC, TIFF and camera RAW photos, audio and video, and track and data files such as KML, GPX and DAT can carry macros or scripts or need another program, so they are not opened directly: the copy is shown selected in its folder, and you open it from there if you mean to. An archive, or a type IWS does not recognise, is written out the same way but as an inert .bin copy that nothing will act on, and is never unpacked; an export writes it the same way. IWS does not store programs or scripts, so a dropped .exe, script, shortcut or similar is refused, and one that arrives inside an investigation from elsewhere is never written out. An image or file whose stored content is not held inside the investigation (a web address in a hand-edited file, say) is not shown, opened or exported, and its card says so; it stays in the file unchanged.

About this evidence holds Caption and Why it matters to the investigation (the Significance), which start small and grow as you write. Category is a drop-down of every category, with Uncategorised first. Choose New category… to type a new name: Enter or leaving the box files the item under it and adds it to this computer's list, and Escape goes back to the list. Reference is where this extract came from, so a reader can find the original: its title, document number, revision or date, the page, section, paragraph or time, and who holds it. For example, Operations Manual, Part C, Rev 12 (03/2026), section 4.2, p. 37; original held by the operator's document control. The app keeps the extract, not the original, so this line is how the original is traced. It is one free-text field, printed as you write it on the card, under a report figure and in the tables, so keep it short.

In the report holds the tick Include in the report as a figure for an image or a text extract; a file's form says why it has none. On a text extract, choose My description or Verbatim to say whose words these are, and use Line printed with this extract in the report for the one line, under 30 words, that the report prints under the extract, with the draft and prompt buttons on the section's heading. The two are different jobs: Why it matters is for you and the AI drafting (what the extract shows and how much weight it carries) and is not printed as its own line, while the printed line is what a reader of the report sees, marked Printed in the report on the form.

Beside each label on the form a small tag says whether the AI drafting model is given that field: Sent to drafting or Not sent, with the reason on hover. The tags follow what drafting actually reads, so they change with the item. A text extract is sent in full, with its caption, significance, category, reference and whether it is verbatim, wherever it is linked or not; its summary goes to the Context section of the report. An image or file is never sent itself, because the model cannot see it: it receives the caption, and the significance when the item is linked to an object or not linked at all. The category and reference reach it only while nothing links to the item, and an item linked only to a safety report sends neither caption nor significance.

Used by lists everything that rests on the item, each a jump to it. Opened from an object, that object's row carries Unlink from this object, which takes the item off that object without removing it from the register.

Link to an object…, at the head of Used by, closes the form and takes you to the AcciMap with a banner: "Click the object this evidence supports, Esc to cancel". Click one object, with the mouse or by tabbing to it and pressing Enter, and it is linked there and then; the form comes back with the new Used by row flashed and Undo beside the message. The link is one step, which Ctrl+Z takes back too, and the Log records it. An object the item already rests on shows Linked already and cannot be picked. A finalised one shows Finalised, click to re-open it and link: clicking it asks in the banner, and Re-open and link there re-opens the object, as Re-open in its form does, and links the item, the two as one step that one Ctrl+Z takes back. Esc, the banner's Cancel, or another tab stops picking with nothing changed. The button waits while the form has unsaved changes, and a finalised item can still be linked, since the link is kept on the object.

Link to a timeline event…, beside it, does the same for the Timeline. It takes you to the Timeline tab with the banner "Click the timeline event this evidence supports, then Link, Esc to cancel". One click on an event, on the drawn timeline or in the table, selects and marks it, and the banner names it; Link in the banner then links it, as one step with its Log entry, and the form comes back with a message naming the event, marked Timeline so it cannot be taken for an AcciMap object, and Undo beside it. An event the item already rests on shows Linked already and is not linked twice.

Delete item sits at the left of the foot, and asks first, showing everything that rests on the item. At the right, Cancel closes the form without saving, Finalise saves and settles the item, and Save writes your changes and closes it. Ctrl+Enter saves as well. A finalised item's tile carries a Final badge, and its form opens locked, its fields shown as plain text, with the green Finalised state, Re-open beside it, and Close at the right. Delete item fades, with "Finalised. Re-open to change it." on hover. Re-open asks nothing: it reopens the item there and then (the Log records it) and unlocks the form for editing. On the Evidence tab's card, a finalised item likewise shows Finalised with Re-open beside it, and Edit becomes Open, which opens the read-only form. Opened from a finalised object's form, an item's form offers neither Unlink nor Delete item.

Opened from an object, an event or a safety report, the item's form says Delete… instead of Delete item, and so does the tile beside its Unlink: neither deletes there. Each takes you to this tab with the item's form open at Used by, so you see everything that rests on it, and Delete item here is the one that removes it. From an object's form it waits until that form's changes are saved or cancelled. If the item has gone from the register by the time you arrive, the tab says so instead of opening a form.

Untick it and the register stays in the investigation but out of all three. Ticking and unticking is logged and is one undo step.

5Timeline

The sequence of events: when each thing happened, where that time came from, and how firmly it is held.

The investigation's sequence of events: when each thing happened, where that time came from, and how firmly it is held. It is both a working tool and a deliverable. On screen it is a drawn timeline you arrange, with a table under it you edit like a spreadsheet, and the two are views of one record, so a correction in either moves both. At the end it is the sequence-of-events table in the report, and the chronology the drafting model is given.

#Entering a time

Type a time the way you would write it. The app reads 18:05, 1805, 18:05:30, 180530, about 1805 (or ~1805, c. 1805), an optional leading date like 28/08 or 28/08/1998, and a date on its own. What you type also says what you know: typing 18:05 records a time known to the minute, and the report writes it as 18:05 rather than inventing a 00 on the end. Typing 18:05:30 records a recorded stamp, and about 1805 records an approximation, which reads as "about 18:05" wherever it appears. The Accuracy picker on the event card sets the same thing by hand, and adds Day only for an event you can place on a date but not in a clock. An investigation that has never touched this reads to the second everywhere, exactly as it always did.

#Time bases and corrections to UTC

Sources rarely agree, so the card says plainly where a time was read from. Source, beside Time, is one list: UTC, each zone of the investigation (Melbourne (UTC+10)), each named clock (the FDR clock, a voice recording), One-off clock…, Add a zone… and Add a clock…. Pick the source and type the time as that source showed it; the form works out the correction to the timeline's stored clock, names the source in the trail and shows the result in one line under the row, such as 08:06:35 UTC, with the steps for a clock (the reading on its face, the correction at each clock it was lined up against, then UTC). A clock whose error is not established yet says so there. A new event opens on the source set by the investigation details' time basis on the Details tab, or failing that the clock the timeline stores its times in.

A One-off clock is for a clock you will use once: type its name, how far it reads (0:45, 2:13) and whether that is ahead or behind UTC, a zone or a named clock. Save as a named clock turns it into a clock of the investigation and, if other entries used the same name, offers to tie them to it. Add a zone… and Add a clock… open a short row in place: a name and offset for a zone, a name, the zone its face shows and its error for a clock. They save to the investigation, as Timeline settings would, and pick the result. An event from an earlier version keeps exactly what it had: its old Other clock shows as a one-off, and nothing is rewritten until you edit it. The card shows the effective time on the stored clock as you work. The corrected time drives the ordering and every output, while the recorded time and its correction survive into the report, so the trail can be audited: "06:45:30 (recorded 06:47:30 ARFF watch room clock, correction -0:02)". Corrected events are starred on the axis and chipped in the table.

A timeline stores its times in UTC or in local time, chosen in Timeline settings (Times are stored in) while it is still empty, and fixed once the first event is entered. The toolbar carries a Times in switch, which only changes the clock the canvas and the table are shown in and never touches the record. The Local button carries the local zone's name, for example Adelaide (local). Timeline settings is setup only. If the record is in local time and no Local zone is set, the times cannot be converted to UTC or another zone. The tab then says "The Local zone is not set, so times are shown as recorded", the time column headings say the same, and a button for each listed zone, such as Use AEST (UTC+10) as the Local zone, sets it in one click (Undo puts it back). Nothing in the file changes until you choose. The window has three sections: Time zones, Clocks and Precision, each with its own Add button at the right of its heading, and a message about a row (a bad offset, what a change would move, the warning below) appears under the row or card it belongs to. Add zone and Add clock put the cursor in the new name, selected, so you type over it. Time zones lists UTC first, as the reference, then each zone you add with its name and offset (UTC+10, UTC+5:30; fixed offsets only, so a zone that changed offset during the record is entered as two zones). One zone can be marked Local: that is the investigation's local time, and its offset is the one the Local view and a local timeline use. An investigation from an earlier version shows its local offset as a Local row already. Clocks has a card for each of the device and record clocks (a camera, the FDR, a voice recording): the zone its face shows, and how far it reads fast or slow of the correct time, or ahead or behind another clock it was lined up against, typed the way you would say it (134, 2:13, 2m13s), with a line for how that was established. The card's heading line also shows a status (lined up, error not established, reference missing, or lined up in a loop). A clock lined up against another chains through it, so the card reads "reads 7 s ahead of FDR clock (3 s ahead of UTC in all)", and a clock cannot be lined up against one that is already lined up against it. Each row or card says how many events use it. Changing a zone's offset, or a clock's error, face or reference, re-corrects only the events tied to it and to every clock lined up through it, and is one undo step with a Log entry. Once events are on it, the change waits for you first: a warning says how many events move, on which zone or clock, and by how much ("Changing this moves 4 events on FDR clock 3 s earlier"), with Cancel and Change. A change that moves nothing is made straight away. Older entries that were never tied to a zone or clock are left exactly as they are. On a timeline stored in local time, the local zone's offset can only be changed before any events are entered, so no stored time ever moves. A clock lined up against one whose error is not yet established is not established either: its events say so and carry no correction beyond the zone its face shows. Removing a zone or clock that events use keeps their corrected times; a zone a clock shows, or a clock another is lined up against, cannot be removed until that clock is changed. When any clock exists the report and the drafting list the clocks once ahead of the sequence of events, and an event read on a clock carries the whole chain in its correction note. Times in, on the toolbar, offers UTC (the default), Local and each of your zones by name. Clocks are not listed: a clock is how a time came in, not a way of reading the timeline. Changing the view never moves a stored time, and every correction note still states the event's own recorded clock. The report, the timeline image and report drafting always use the stored clock, whichever way it is set. The heading, the axis, the bubbles and the table's Time column all name the zone in use.

Tenths of a second, under Precision in Timeline settings, is for flight data and other recordings stamped finer than a second. It belongs to the investigation, not the machine, and is off by default. Switched on, a time can be typed with one decimal (18:05:30.2 or 180530.2), a clock error too (7.3), and every time known to the second reads to the tenth on the canvas, in the table, on the event card, in the timeline images, the report and report drafting (a time with no tenth reads .0). Times known to the minute or approximate are unchanged, and durations stay in whole seconds. Clock errors and lining clocks up then work in tenths too, and switching it on re-works the correction of every event read on a clock whose error has a tenth (one undo step). Switched off, everything reads to the second as before, and a time typed with a decimal is refused with "tenths are off for this investigation (Timeline settings)". Nothing is deleted when you switch it off: tenths already entered are kept, still order events within the same second, and show again when you switch it back on, and the settings line says how many events and clocks carry them. Retyping such an event's time with tenths off stores what you typed. A spreadsheet import keeps the first decimal of a time with tenths on; with them off it reads the time to the second and says how many rows carried tenths.

Capture view, the camera with a small arrow for its menu, sits in the band along the top of the map, at its right end after the overview strip, Shorter, Taller, zoom, Fit, Arrange and Reset layout; in portrait the band holds the overview strip, Arrange and Capture view. It files a picture of the timeline as you have it framed into Evidence, under the Timeline captures category. Choose Chart only or Chart and table. It is drawn from the record by the timeline image export's renderer, in the spacing the tab is showing (Sequence, Proportional, Compress gaps) over the stretch in view, with the cards placed as the tab places them, and no selection, hover state or scroll bars. The lane displays are drawn on the One line, never a grab of the window, and Chart and table adds those events as rows beneath. That is the landscape picture. With the orientation set to Portrait, the picture follows it, shaped for a report page: Chart and table draws the time line down the left with each point joined to its row, and the table carries the time, entity and event, plus Phase and Status where you show those columns and a row has one (no Open or delete buttons). Chart only runs the line down the middle with each event as a card in its entity's colour, alternating left and right, each card giving the time and the title. Both keep your focus, spacing, Compress gaps, clock, Arrange, Phase bands and Accuracy, and the title bar with the fictional-case label, and name the day the picture starts on. A portrait picture is the same width whatever its length, so placed 16 cm wide in a report its card and row text reads at about 8 pt and its times at about 7 pt; a long timeline makes a taller picture. A focus with no events in it is not captured. The new item is an image, captioned like "Timeline, 07:30:02 to 07:30:57, captured 29/10/2026" (edit it on the item), with Significance empty and not ticked for the report. A line under the toolbar confirms it and Open it goes straight to the item. It is one undo step and writes the usual Log entry. A picture over the 5 MB image limit is drawn smaller until it fits, and refused with the usual guidance if it cannot.

Line up… on a clock's row works its error out for you from the record itself, rather than typing it. Choose what to line it up against: the correct time (every event typed in UTC or a zone) or another clock. Two lists appear side by side, this clock's readings and the other's; click one in each that shows the same moment, such as a radio call heard on the voice recording and the transmit marked on the FDR. One pair is enough, and the dialog says what it gives ("Voice recording reads 7 s ahead of FDR clock, matched on 'Ready for pushback' = 'VHF 1 transmit'"). A reading known only to the minute, or approximately, is shown greyed, since it cannot fix a clock to the second. Below, both lists are merged into one table in UTC with the error applied, so you can watch this clock's events fall into line with the other's before anything is written, along with how many events will move. Where a second moment shows on both records, pair it too: the dialog shows how far apart the second pair is once the first pair's error is taken off, and asks whether that is acceptable. Accept and apply records the separation in the clock's basis and the Log; Reject changes nothing and notes the attempt in the Log. No fixed tolerance decides it for you, because a drifting clock is your call. Applying sets the clock's error and reference, fills in how it was established (you can edit that line first), stores the pairs on the clock and re-corrects every event on it and on any clock lined up through it, as one undo step with one Log entry. Until a clock is lined up, or its error typed, its events say “error not established”. Check alignment on the row re-reads the stored pairs against the events as they stand now, so a time retyped on a paired event since shows up, and a paired event since deleted reads as removed.

To read the timeline in another zone, add the zone in Timeline settings (a name such as AEST and an offset: +10, UTC+10, UTC+9:30 or -8) and pick it under Times in on the toolbar. The axis, the bubbles and the table all move together and the heading names the zone ("times in AEST (UTC+10), converted from the stored UTC"). A time that crosses midnight moves to the next or previous date. This is a view only: no stored time changes, the report and drafting keep using the stored clock, and the choice is remembered on this computer for this investigation. The event card shows the UTC time beneath the effective time when the timeline is stored in local time.

#What an event records

The event itself is the one line that appears on the bubble and in the sequence; detail is what happened more fully, including anything uncertain about the time. Entity is who or what the event belongs to, and gives the bubble its colour. Duration turns a point in time into a span, and an event can carry several, each measured from its start: take-off to aircraft stopped, and take-off to V1. Location is where it happened: a position, a berth, a chainage, a runway.

Two fields say how much weight the entry carries. Kind of source is what sort of source it is (recorded data, voice recording, a traffic service record, surveillance, CCTV, physical evidence, a maintenance or operator record, an interview or account, a document), offered as a list you can type over, and source of information is the specific reference that lets a reader go and check it. They print together: "[Source: Recorded data: FDR channel 12]". Status says how firmly the entry is held: established for something supported by a recorded or physical source, reported for an account that has not been independently confirmed, and estimated for a time you derived yourself. It is blank until you set it, and a blank one claims nothing.

Report narrative is the event in report voice, one or two sentences. The occurrence section of the report is built from these; an event left blank there renders as its plain timeline line instead, and the Record page shows it as a gap to fill.

#The event card

+ Event opens a blank card; Open on a table row or a double-click on a bubble opens that event's own. The card runs in four headed sections, in the order you would fill them in. Event comes first. Date (DD/MM/YYYY), Time, Source and Accuracy share the top row, with the effective UTC line under it, and a one-off clock or an inline zone or clock row under that when you are using one. Time takes digits without colons: 1500 is 15:00:00, 151345 is 15:13:45, 930 is 09:30:00, and colons still work. A line under the box says how it is being read while you type, the box tidies itself to hh:mm:ss when you leave it, and a time out of range (1575) is named and not stored. Accuracy follows what you typed, to the minute with no seconds and to the second with them, and you can still change it by hand. The Event line, what happened, comes next (a new event starts untitled), then Entity, Phase and Location, then This investigation's own columns where the investigation has added any. Duration is last and folded away: it opens when the event has a duration, or when you open it. It is a short list, one entry per span: What it spans (optional, such as Take-off to V1), Length, or end time or until event, which are other ways into the same length (the event picker lists the events that start later and sets the length to the gap between the two starts; it stores only that length, not a link, so moving either event later does not move the span), then a colour (Entity colour, the default, one of six standard colours (red, orange, green, blue, purple and grey) kept apart from the entity colours, or Custom… for any colour from the picker) and where it is drawn, On the axis or Below. A new duration starts Below; spans saved earlier keep the placement they had. The arrows reorder the list and × removes an entry; + Add duration adds another. An event with no duration shows one blank entry, so a first span is just a length typed in. The first entry with a length is the event's main span: it is the end the report's chronology prints, and the one an older copy of the app reads.

Evidence holds Kind of source and Status together, then Source of information, Detail and Supporting material. Report narrative carries the narrative box, with the prompt actions (Copy AI prompt and Save prompt as file, at the copy and paste tier) on the heading's line and the prompt's size estimate in small type beneath them. Analysis links ends the card with Linked analysis objects: Link an object… adds one and a × beside each takes it off. The foot stays in view however far the card scrolls, with Delete event on the left, which removes the whole entry, and Add another entry and Done on the right. Done closes the card. Add another entry keeps this one and opens the next on the same source, with the same date, time, accuracy, phase and entity, an empty Event line, and the Time box selected so typing replaces the time; Duration opens if this entry had one. It is one undo step, and Ctrl+Enter does the same from anywhere on the card. + Event on the toolbar likewise starts on the source you used last in this session.

#Phases, and the order events are read in

Phase is a free label for the part of the sequence an event belongs to: pre-departure, the transit, the occurrence, the response. Set it on the card or in the table, and the chronology in the report, the exports and the drafting collation breaks into subheadings wherever it changes. Phases already in use are offered as you type, so one phase does not end up with four spellings. A phase that comes round again gets its heading again: the sequence is never reordered to suit the grouping, and an investigation that uses no phases prints with no headings at all.

Order follows the times, always. Correct a time and the sequence re-sorts; drag a bubble and only its layout moves, with the stalk still pointing at the true tick. Where two events share the same corrected time, and only there, the table offers arrows to put them in the order they actually happened.

#Linking to the map and to the evidence

An event can carry supporting material the same way a map object does: paste or drop it in, and it is filed once in the evidence register and linked here. Binary captures are written out beside the report when you export.

Link evidence…, at the top of Supporting material on the card, works as it does from a map object. The card closes and you go to the Evidence tab with a banner, find the items in the register as you usually would (its categories and other sections stay open to you), tick any number of tiles (one the event already links shows Linked already and cannot be ticked), then press Link N items. You come back to the card with the new items flashed. The links are made there and then, as one step that Ctrl+Z takes back, and the Log records each as evidence linked to a timeline event. Esc or Cancel comes back with nothing changed.

From an evidence item's form, Link to a timeline event… brings you here with a banner: "Click the timeline event this evidence supports, then Link, Esc to cancel". A single click on an event, on the drawn timeline or in the table, selects and marks it and the banner names it; press Link in the banner to link it, as one step with its Log entry. A double-click, or Open in the event's table row, links at once. An event the item already rests on shows Linked already and Link waits. Esc or Cancel goes back to the item's form with nothing changed.

An event can also be linked to the objects on the map it bears on, under Linked analysis objects, which is you saying that this is the moment that factor acted. Two things follow. The chronology names the factors beside the event, so the sequence and the analysis cannot be read apart, and the record check that looks for a causal arrow pointing backwards in time reads the linked event first, ahead of the weaker readings it otherwise has to fall back on (a shared document, a time written into the evidence text, or where the box sits on the canvas).

#Your own columns, and the Columns panel

Every organisation keeps one or two things on a timeline that no application can guess: an occurrence reference, a flight phase code, a crew position. Columns on the toolbar adds them to this investigation. A column has a name and a kind, where text takes anything, number refuses what is not a number, and choice offers a list you can still type over. They appear as columns in the table, as fields on the event card, and in the chronology as "[Occurrence reference: 2026/0412]". They belong to the one investigation, so adding one needs no new version of the app, and deleting one says how many events carry a value before it takes them with it.

Showing, hiding and sizing the table's columns

The event table's columns work the way a spreadsheet's do. Columns on the toolbar ticks each of Date, Time as recorded, Time on the view's clock, Phase, Entity, Status and your own columns; a column left unticked is hidden, and Event and the row's buttons are always shown. The two time columns hide separately, so you can keep only the time as it was recorded or only the time on the clock you are viewing. The clock column appears only when the view converts or a correction is in play. If you hide Time as recorded you cannot edit the time in the table; open the event to change it. The portrait page and the Capture view follow the same choice. Drag the right edge of a heading to resize that column as you go, or double-click the edge to fit the column to its widest content, the heading included. With the keyboard, Tab to an edge and press the left or right arrow (Shift for bigger steps) or Enter to fit it. Reset widths in the Columns panel goes back to the standard widths, with Event taking what is left. The choices are remembered for this investigation on this computer and are not saved in the investigation file.

The Columns panel

Columns on the toolbar opens one panel for all of this. It lists every column with a tick, the standard ones and your own, and Reset widths sits below the list. Each column of your own also shows its name, its kind (Text, Number or Choice), the choices it offers where the kind is Choice, and Delete, which first says how many events already carry a value in that column before it takes them with it. At the foot, type a new column's name, pick its kind and press Add column (or Enter): the new column appears ticked in the list at once and as a heading in the event table. Escape or a click away closes the panel. The columns show in the event table and on the event card, so in Portrait, which does not draw the table, the panel says so.

#Working the timeline

The toolbar carries the controls you reach for most, as one row of blue buttons that wraps onto a second line when the window is too narrow for it. The first part decides how the timeline is shown: Orientation, Times in (UTC, Local and each zone you have set up), a spacing choice of Sequence or Proportional, Show (Compress gaps, Accuracy, Phase bands), then Display. Display is a landscape control, so it is not shown in portrait. Display is One line, the timeline as it has always been drawn, or Lanes by entity, which gives each entity its own lane with its events and durations on it, and a lane for events that name no entity. Lanes by source gives each source its own lane (see below), and States draws the durations as bars. In either lane display a card can be dragged as on the One line, and each display keeps its own place for it, so moving a card in Lanes by entity leaves it where it was in Lanes by source and on the One line. The dates and the phase names are written in two rows above the drawing, clear of the lane names and the cards, and Fit frames every card and written length, so nothing is cut off at an edge. Orientation is Landscape, the line across the canvas with the table under it, or Portrait (see below). These choices, and the spacing, are views only: they are remembered on this computer for this investigation, are not saved in the investigation file, and do not change an export. The rest of the row, beside Display, changes the record and its setup: the Undo (Ctrl+Z) and Redo (Ctrl+Y) arrows, then + Event, + Entity and Import…, then Columns (the three-column symbol) and Timeline settings (the gear: zones and clocks, setup only). Hold the pointer over a symbol for its name. Arrange and Reset layout (below) are not on this row: they sit at the right end of the map band, beside Fit and before Capture view, with the other controls that change the view and layout. Reset layout is shown in landscape on the One line and the two lane displays, where cards can be moved, and not in States. The heading line above the toolbar gives the number of events, the date and the clock the times are shown in (hold the pointer over it for a reminder of how to work the drawing), and at its right end the entity legend shows one chip per entity in use; clicking a chip opens its own small editor under the toolbar, with the name, the colour swatches, a count of how many events use it, Delete entity and Done.

Add an event with + Event for the full card, or by typing in the table. A new event inherits the last one's phase, since a sequence is usually entered a phase at a time. Create entities with + Entity; each takes a colour, its events' bubbles match, and clicking a legend entry renames, recolours or deletes it (deleting warns how many events use it and leaves those events in place with no entity). Bubbles resize from their corner handle, and every timeline edit is undoable.

The spacing toggle switches between Sequence (even spacing, readable, not to scale) and Proportional (true time gaps). The spacing is remembered on this computer for this investigation, and the portrait page export uses it. The canvas pans and zooms like the map: drag empty canvas to pan, Ctrl+wheel to zoom at the cursor, wheel to travel along the axis, and Fit to frame everything. Timelines can span days, weeks or months, and day changes are marked on the axis with dated dividers. A duration placed on the axis is drawn as a translucent bar laid over the axis in its colour, starting at its event's anchor dot (its corrected start) and ending with a small tick, with its label, such as Take-off to V1, 27 s, written just below the line after the tick. Bars that overlap in time sit on thin tracks hugging the axis, alternately just above and just below it, which is how concurrent activity becomes visible, and an approximate start fades in rather than starting sharp. A duration placed below goes on a track in a band beneath the lowest cards, starting at its event's position, with a dotted guide up to the anchor dot and a small dot repeating it, so a second or third span does not crowd the axis; overlapping bars there take a track each. Where a label would run into the next bar or another label, it is shortened to the length alone, or left off. Hover a bar for its name, start, end and length, and its event card lights up with it (hovering a card lights every one of its bars); click a bar to open the event. In Proportional spacing a bar's length is to scale; in Sequence spacing it runs to the events it overlaps, because there the position is the event's order, not its time, and a bar that outlasts every event ends in a chevron. The exported timeline image draws the same bars.

The map band, a row of its own along the top edge of the map, holds the overview strip on the left and the view controls at its right end, so nothing sits over the drawing; on a narrow window the controls drop under the strip. The zoom control there gives the same reach with your hands off the mouse: − and + step the zoom, the reading between them shows where it sits, and Fit (the four corners) returns to the whole timeline. Shorter and Taller beside it make the drawn timeline shorter or taller in steps, and Arrange and Reset layout sit between Fit and Capture view. Nothing about reading the canvas changes once you are this close to it. The bar between the drawn timeline and the table resizes the two: drag it, or click it and use the up and down arrow keys, and the height is remembered on this machine.

To read a tight cluster without shrinking everything else, focus on it. Hold Shift and drag across the canvas (starting over a card is fine) and the stretch you drag over fills the width, with the cards at their usual size and the events outside it left off. In Proportional spacing the stretch is the times under the drag, and the spacing inside it is still true; in Sequence spacing it runs from the first to the last event whose tick you dragged over, so take in at least two. Drag again inside the focus to narrow it further. A duration that started before the stretch is drawn cut at its edge with a chevron, and keeps its full length in its label. While a focus is set the header reads, for example, "Focused on 07:30:29 to 07:30:57 UTC, 9 of 13 events", and Show all (beside Focused on, in the header) or Esc with the canvas selected goes back to the whole timeline. The focus follows Times in, so changing the clock keeps the same events in view. It is a view only: it is not saved in the investigation, and it is dropped when the investigation closes.

The thin overview strip at the left of the map band is the whole record: a tick for each event and a line for each duration, in their colours, with long quiet stretches always folded so the busy part is wide enough to take hold of. The box on it is what the canvas is showing. Drag the box to move along the record, drag either edge to narrow or widen it, press outside the box to jump there, or select the strip and use the left and right arrow keys. Moving the box sets the focus, and stretching it back over everything clears it.

Show, Compress gaps (Proportional spacing only, where a gap means time) is for a record with long quiet stretches, such as an inspection the evening before. Every stretch longer than 15 minutes between two moments on the record (an event, or the end of a duration) folds into a narrow shaded break with its length written under it, such as 9 h 48 m, and a break mark across the line; the rest stays to scale. A duration that runs across a fold is drawn through the break and keeps its true length in its label, and every time shown is still the event's own. It works with a focus too, and is not remembered once the investigation closes.

Lanes by source is for comparing recordings of the same moment. A lane is made for each source that has an event: each named clock (a clock whose error is not established says so in its name), each zone, and each one-off source written in an event's Source box (the same text in a different case or spacing is one lane), with a last lane for events that have no source. A source nothing was read from has no lane. States lists every duration as a bar in a row of its own, grouped by entity and in start order, titled with its event and start time and ending in its length; events with no duration are points on an Events line above the bars. Click a bar or point to select its event and double-click to open it. States is drawn from the same record as the cards and changes nothing in it. Under Show, Accuracy marks each time that is not known to the second: a short bar for a time to the minute, a wider, fainter one for an approximate time or a day, and a dashed outline where the event was read on a clock whose error is not established. A time to the minute or the day is drawn as that minute or day, starting at the stored time; an approximate time is drawn 3 minutes either side of it. A clock whose error is not established, or whose reference has been deleted, gets the dashed outline as well as any precision mark. The width follows the scale where there is one, and is a minimum width in Sequence spacing. Accuracy is off until you turn it on. Phase bands shades each phase behind the drawing, from the Phase column: a phase runs from its first event to the start of the next, an event with no phase is not shaded, and the name is written on a band wide enough to hold it. Both work in every display, in portrait too. Neither changes an export.

Arrange tidies a crowded timeline. On the One line it moves the cards so that none overlap and no stalk runs through another card: each card stays centred on its own stalk where there is room; in a crowded stretch the cards step out like a staircase, one row per event on each side of the line, each with an edge at its own stalk, so a later event sits nearer the line and an earlier one reaches over the shorter stalk beneath it. The drawing grows taller to hold the rows. Where two events are only a few pixels apart, the nearer card steps just clear and its stalk leans that short way to it. Only a cluster of more than eight events a side falls back to slanted stalks, and only where no clear layout exists does it accept a stalk crossing a card, as few as it can. Cards never overlap, and on each side they always read in time order. Times by the line never print over each other: close times are staggered, and a cluster with no room is written as one range, such as 07:30:27 to 07:30:34 (each event's own time stays on its card's hover text). A card you have resized keeps its size. It writes the same card positions a drag does, as one step you can undo with Ctrl+Z, and it adds nothing to the Log. With a focus set it arranges the cards you can see. The positions are shared by Sequence and Proportional spacing, so arrange for the spacing you are looking at. In the lane displays Arrange packs each lane so no cards overlap as a view only, leaving any card you moved there where you put it (in States it stacks the point labels on the Events line instead): nothing is saved, and pressing it again goes back. Reset layout, beside it, puts the shown cards back where they start, also as one undo step: on the One line it clears their moved positions and resized shapes, and in a lane display it clears only where they were moved to in that display. A lane grows taller when a card needs the room. The exported timeline image draws the positions stored on the One line, except that a slid card which would sit on another in its sequence spacing is moved the shortest way clear.

Orientation, Portrait turns the timeline down the page: the line runs down the left, with the time beside each point, and the event table sits alongside it, each row joined to its point by a faint leader. It is the same table, edited the same way. In Proportional spacing each point sits at its true time and the rows are packed in order beside them, so the leaders fan out like stalks; Compress gaps keeps them close. In Sequence spacing there is one point per row and every leader runs straight across. Durations are bars beside the line. Click a row or a point to select both. Times in, the overview strip, Shift-drag down the line to focus, Escape and Compress gaps all work as they do in landscape. Arrange in portrait centres each run of crowded rows on its points so the leaders are as short as they can be, as a view only: nothing is saved, and pressing it again goes back. Portrait never moves or resizes a landscape card, so Reset layout is not shown in portrait, and the lane displays and States are landscape displays: Display and the zoom control are not shown in portrait either. Each point on the line can be reached with Tab in time order; Up and Down move between points, and Enter or Space selects a point and its row. Export, Timeline, portrait page (.png) is this layout on A4 pages, and a report can carry it as an appendix (Timeline, portrait pages).

Selecting an event on the drawn timeline, by clicking its bubble or tabbing to it and pressing Enter, highlights its row in the table without moving the page or the table, and selecting a row highlights the event on the drawn timeline and pans it into view if it was off screen. The view you are working in does not move. Following an evidence link to an event from the Evidence tab, an item's Used by list or an object's evidence panel opens the Timeline with the event selected, centred on the drawn timeline and zoomed in far enough to read if you were zoomed further out, scrolls the table to its row and pulses the event on both, as a link to an object brings it into view and pulses it on the map.

The table underneath carries the same events as plain rows: Date, Time, whose heading follows the clock the times are shown in (Time as recorded plus a shown Time column when the view converts or a correction is in play), Phase, Entity, Event and Status, then any columns this investigation has added of its own, then the row's own actions. Move earlier and Move later appear only where two events share a time, for the ordering arrows already described above; Open takes you to that event's card, and a × at the end deletes the row's event outright.

A chronology already held in a spreadsheet comes in through Import…, in three steps. First say what clock the sheet’s times are on: UTC, one of your zones, a clock you have named, a new clock (give it a name and the zone its face shows; its error is not known yet, so its events read “once aligned” in UTC until it is lined up), or “a column says, per row”, which uses a “Source, by name” column (a row naming no zone or clock you have is skipped and named) or a “Correction to UTC” column. The date for rows with no date of their own is set beside it. Second, confirm what each column holds: the app guesses from the headings, including source kind, status, location and phase, and a heading that matches one of your own columns by name goes to that column. Third, see exactly what will land, with each reading and its UTC, and every row it cannot read named with the reason, before committing. Times are read exactly as typed on an event card: 1805 is 18:05, 180622 is 18:06:22, 930 is 09:30, and 1575 is refused as minutes out of range. A cell holding 18:05 imports as a time known to the minute. A fraction of a second is read to the second. The whole import, with any new clock, is one Undo. When the sheet is on a clock, “Then line this clock up against another clock” is ticked: once the rows land, Line up opens with that clock chosen, and the line-up is its own Undo. Only the rows are taken; the file itself is not kept.

#Where the timeline goes

Into the report as the sequence of events, under phase subheadings where you use them, with the correction trail, the status, the location, the source and your own columns on each line. Into the exports as a PNG of the drawn timeline or as portrait pages with the table beside the line, and into the report's appendix as either. Into the drafting collation as the same chronology, with each event's linked extracts beneath it, so a model writing the occurrence section is working from the record rather than from a summary of it. A section whose style declares the timeline alone, a short list of the key events for instance, is drafted from this chronology and its clocks and nothing else; the Log is never part of it. The Draft report tab's topic on what each section is drafted from shows which sections those are. And into the summary, which takes the occurrence date from the timeline.

#A worked example

A ferry grounding. The investigation works in UTC, and the vessel's clock was local time, ten hours ahead. For the plotter freezing you would enter the recorded time 180706, set Source to a zone you have added for the vessel, Vessel (UTC+10) (the form names the clock UTC+10 and applies the correction -10:00), and leave the accuracy at the second, because it came from the plotter's own event log. Kind of source is Recorded data, the source is "Plotter internal event log, cause code 0x51", the status is established, the phase is "The transit", and the event is linked to the map object for the unreported plotter defect. The deckhand's account that he was sent to collect fares is a different kind of entry: enter it as about 1804, kind of source Interview or account, status reported. The report then reads "08:07:06 [Bridge] Chart plotter display freezes and does not recover (recorded 18:07:06 UTC+10, correction -10:00) (established)", and the deckhand's line reads "about 08:04 ... (reported)", which is exactly the difference between the two that a reader has to be able to see.

6AcciMap

The map itself: lanes, objects and arrows, and the object form where each object is tested against its evidence.

The AcciMap tab holds the map itself: the lanes, the objects placed in them, the arrows between them, and the form where each object is tested against its evidence. Each control is described where you meet it, together with how the method's tests and classification work inside the app. Building a first map in order is covered in Getting started.

#The canvas and lanes

The six horizontal lanes are organisational levels. The faint italic text inside each lane suggests the kinds of factors that belong there.

  • External Influences: Influences from outside the organisation: regulators, sector standards, oversight and guidance.
  • Organisational Influences: The organisation's own systems: hazard recognition, management of change, risk evaluation, training requirements, organisational communication, assurance activities.
  • Risk Controls: The defences that should prevent or catch the occurrence: procedures, alerts and warning systems, training, operational supervision, work scheduling, facilities and infrastructure.
  • Local Conditions: The conditions on the day: weather, workload, fatigue, distractions, health, knowledge and experience.
  • Individual Actions: What people did: handling, planning, inspecting, communicating, documenting, monitoring.
  • Occurrence Events: The event sequence itself, converging on the critical event.

The heavier line above Local Conditions separates the organisational levels (above) from the individual and situational levels (below). Drag any lane boundary to resize it; see Moving objects and resizing lanes. The lanes run the full width and the map is open sideways: pan left and right as far as you need. Up and down, the view stops at the top and bottom lanes, and you cannot zoom out past the point where the lanes fill the window's height.

#The toolbar

The toolbar is one row of blue buttons. Three placement tools sit at its left, in one pill: Select, + Object and + Critical event. Only one is ever active at a time, and it is shaded. Select is the ordinary tool, for clicking and dragging objects already on the map. Where a control has a standard symbol it shows the symbol rather than the word: the bin for Delete, two sheets for Duplicate, the curved arrows for Undo and Redo, the grid for Grid and four corners for Fit. Hold the pointer over any of them for its name, and its shortcut where it has one.

Delete and Duplicate stay greyed out until something on the map is selected. Duplicate makes a new hypothesis: the copy keeps the title, notes, size, type and risk direction, but starts untested and not finalised, with no linked evidence and no analysis paragraph. Right-click an object for Edit evidence…, Duplicate, Mark as critical event (Revert to ordinary object on the critical event), Bring to front and Send to back, which move it above or below whatever it overlaps, and Delete. Right-click an arrow to delete it. Undo and Redo step back and forward through the investigation's editing history, the same one Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y use on every tab (see Saving, autosave and undo), and Grid switches the alignment grid on and off (shaded while it is on). Deleting an object takes its arrows with it, and one undo brings both back.

Arrange tidies the whole map, laying it out the way you would by hand. Every object stays in its own lane, and within each lane the objects are set out in one row across the middle of the band. The map is made about as wide as its widest lane: that lane sets the columns, and every other lane fits within them, each object lined up under or over the objects it is linked to so the arrows between lanes run short and mostly straight down. On a small map Arrange may use up to two spare columns, and an object may sit half a column along, when that keeps arrows shorter or off other objects. An arrow that skips a lane is given a free slot in that lane where it can, so it is not drawn through an object. Arrows within a lane run straight across, and an object outside every lane is left where it is.

The critical event always ends up at the right-hand end of its own lane's row. It stays in its own lane, which is Occurrence Events, the bottom lane, by default, close to the objects that lead into it rather than pushed out to the map's right edge. A critical event you have put in a higher lane stays in that lane, at the right-hand end. With more than one critical event they sit together at that end, in time order where each has a time and otherwise in the order you drew them. Within each lane Arrange also prefers the order of events, left to right: earlier before later by each object's time (from the timeline event linked to it, the timeline events that share its evidence, or the clock times in its evidence text), and, where there is no time, a cause before what it leads to. This order gives way to fewer crossings. An arrow between two objects in the same lane is different: Arrange puts its source to the left of its target, even when that costs a crossing. Three kinds of arrow are left out of that rule: an arrow to or from a critical event, which keeps its place at the right-hand end; an arrow to or from a disproved object, which Arrange ignores; and arrows that run round in a loop within one lane, which are left to the order of events.

Objects that are not part of the sequence are set aside at the left of the map: an excluded hypothesis, and any object with no arrow to another object that counts. They stay in their own lanes, in a neat grid clear of the rest, in the order they stood, with any that have a time in time order. Arrange ignores every arrow to or from a disproved object (such an arrow is drawn greyed out), so it does not pull the rest of the map out of shape or move the critical event from the end of its row. An object whose only arrows run to or from disproved objects is set aside too. A critical event is never set aside. With Considered set to Hide, Arrange works on the objects drawn only: a hidden object keeps its place, takes no room in the layout, and its arrows do not count. Under Show and Fade an object closed as undetermined keeps its arrows in the sequence.

Arrange weighs fewer crossings against a compact map, and does not treat crossings as the only thing that matters. On a map that is already about as wide as its widest lane it never leaves more arrows crossing than there were (not counting the arrows of disproved objects). On a map that has spread out wider than that, it may let an arrow or two cross to bring the map back to that width, as a hand layout does. Otherwise, if it cannot do clearly better, nothing moves, so pressing Arrange a second time changes nothing. A map with a critical event out of place, objects in a row closer together than the usual gap, or an arrow within a lane pointing from right to left always changes. It writes the same positions a drag does, as one step called Arrange map, so one Ctrl+Z puts every object back where it was, and it adds nothing to the Log.

Considered, with Show, Fade and Hide at the right end of the toolbar beside the zoom controls (hover the label for its full name, Considered objects), sets how the map draws the objects that were considered and not established: excluded hypotheses and objects closed as undetermined. Show (the default) draws them as their state says; Fade draws them and their arrows faint; Hide leaves them and their arrows off the map, and a Show considered button, with how many there are, appears at the bottom left of the canvas to bring them back. Hiding moves nothing: each object keeps its place, and comes back exactly where it was. The choice is saved with the investigation, and the exported map image follows it, its edges fitted to the objects it draws. Where the organisation’s Considered objects in published outputs is set to Leave out, the exported image leaves them out whatever the map shows.

The same switch governs the report’s text and the working record. Set to Leave out, an excluded hypothesis and an object closed as undetermined lose their full account there (evidence, argument and notes) and keep one line each, under the headings that already list them, saying that it was considered, and why it was ruled out or could not be settled. An investigation with the switch on Include, the default, is written exactly as before. Changing the switch makes a drafted section out of date where it describes one of these objects.

#Placing an object

Click + Object or + Critical event to arm it, then click anywhere on the canvas to drop the object there. The tool disarms after that one placement and the toolbar returns to Select, so click + Object again for each further object. To cancel before placing, press Escape or choose Select.

An investigation usually has one critical event, placed first and conventionally at the bottom right of Occurrence Events; everything else on the map exists to explain it. Add an object for every event or condition that might have played a part, in the lane where it belongs and roughly where it sits in time. There is no need to filter at this stage: the method works by testing, and a hypothesis that is later excluded still strengthens the record.

#Moving objects and resizing lanes

Move and resize objects directly on the AcciMap. An object’s organisational level is determined by the lane containing its centre when the object is placed, moved or resized. Moving an object into another lane changes its level, which also determines where it appears in the Evidence and Argument Tables.

An object’s box grows taller to show its whole title, on the AcciMap and in the exported map image, so a long title is never cut off. Arrange allows for the taller box.

Resize lanes by dragging their lower boundaries. The lanes and objects below the adjusted boundary generally move with it, although objects near the boundary may need repositioning. Resizing a lane does not itself change an object’s recorded level.

After adjusting a lane, check objects near its boundaries. If an object’s position no longer matches its recorded level, moving or resizing it will update that level according to its centre position.

#Causal arrows

To connect two objects, hover one, drag from one of the ports on its edge, and drop on the object it led to. Build the chains back from the critical event.

Arrows carry the causal argument, and their appearance reflects its strength. An arrow is solid green only between two established objects: the object it leaves (the critical event included) has passed Existence and then Influence, and the object it reaches has passed Existence and is neither excluded nor closed as undetermined. Until then it stays grey and dashed, so an arrow into something that did not happen, or could not be shown to, never reads as proven. A map whose chains are green from organisational influences down to the critical event is a completed causal argument.

These arrows also decide reading order. Wherever a report or the Record lists factors (Analysis, Findings, Issues), it lists each cause before the effects it feeds, following the arrows rather than where a factor first happened to appear on an earlier chain.

Arrows are claims. If you cannot say in one sentence how the source led to the target, the arrow is not ready to draw.

#Title and risk direction

Title names the object. Hypothesising an event or condition that offers a choice of Increased risk, Decreased risk or Neutral, setting which way the object leans before any test below it is answered. The three are mutually exclusive. Increased-risk objects take sharp corners; decreased-risk objects become green positive events once they exist; a neutral object that exists is recorded as an event or condition with no risk impact.

#The three tests

The tests follow in order: Existence, then Influence once Existence passes, then Importance where Influence fails or cannot be settled.

  • Existence: "Did this event or condition actually exist or occur?"
    • asked of every object, the critical event included
    • a pass turns the border solid; a fail excludes the hypothesis, struck through but kept on the map
  • Influence: "Did it influence this occurrence?"
    • asked once Existence passes
    • a pass makes an increased-risk object a contributing safety factor, and its arrows turn solid green wherever the object they reach is established
    • on the critical event the step is headed Prove the significance; its Influence question, "Did it lead to the subsequent occurrence?", appears only once an arrow leaves it, and it is never asked Importance
  • Importance: "Is it important enough to address anyway?"
    • asked only if Influence fails or could not be settled (inconclusive)
    • a pass makes an increased-risk object an other risk factor, a finding in its own right; where Influence was inconclusive the report says its contribution could not be determined, never that it contributed
    • a fail leaves an increased-risk object an event or condition: it happened, so it is not struck through, but it is not a safety factor

Each test carries a ? beside its heading (while guidance is switched on in Settings > General > Preferences) for guidance on what counts as evidence, a choice of Conclusive or Argument, the text the test rests on, a confidence slider with the probability term it reads as shown beside it, the buttons Untested and Insufficient evidence for where neither answer is ready yet, and a box headed Reasoning for this test, where you sum up why the points for and against lead to the verdict (or what is missing). The reports print it as the test's one paragraph of reasoning.

The text a test rests on is a list of numbered points rather than one long box. In Conclusive mode the list is the points for (F1, F2 and so on); in Argument mode the points for sit on the left and the points against (A1, A2 and so on) on the right. Use + Add point for the next one, the arrows to reorder and the cross to remove one. Each point is one paragraph in the record, so write one idea or one piece of evidence to a point: the numbers are what the Evidence and Argument Tables print, so you and a reviewer can refer to a point by its label. Text written before this list existed opens as one point per paragraph.

The points, each test's comment and the object's Analysis paragraph take bold and italic. Select the words and press Ctrl+B or Ctrl+I, or use the small B and I buttons beside the box; pressing again on the same words takes the mark off. The record keeps the marks as ordinary text, **like this** for bold and *like this* for italic, so nothing in the file format changes, and a line under the box shows the result. The Evidence and Argument Tables and the Word exports print the emphasis. The title never takes it, and the map shows the words without the marks. Drafting with AI, the record checks and the audit read the plain words, so adding emphasis never changes what a draft says.

The Influence test adds a Reason button beside its box (the single box in Conclusive mode, each of the for and against boxes in Argument mode). It opens a panel of common reasons under six headings: the mechanism, timing and sequence, place and exposure, alternative explanations, consistency with other occasions, and the strength of the evidence. Choose a heading on the left and each reason shows with a line on when it applies, so you can read before choosing; in Argument mode each box offers only its own side's reasons. Choosing one inserts its starting sentence, which you then complete: as the first point of an empty list, or as a new point if you have already written something. The arrow keys move through the panel, Enter inserts and Escape closes it. Settings > Method and wording > Influence reasons edits the list.

#Classification: what the answers make an object

You never set an object's classification by hand. The app derives it from the risk direction and the test results, every time one changes:

  • IF Existence fails
    • THEN excluded hypothesis
  • ELSE IF Existence is untested or inconclusive
    • THEN it stays a hypothesis
  • ELSE (Existence passes)
    • IF increased risk
      • IF Influence passes: contributing safety factor
      • IF Influence fails or is inconclusive and Importance passes: other risk factor
      • IF Influence fails or is inconclusive and Importance fails: event or condition (it existed, so it is never an excluded hypothesis)
      • otherwise: safety factor, testing in progress
    • IF decreased risk
      • THEN positive event, whatever Influence and Importance say
    • IF neutral
      • THEN event or condition, no risk impact
  • A critical event stays the critical event on the map; its Existence is still established through evidence like any other object's.

Record positive events as carefully as the factors that went wrong: the defences and decisions that limited the outcome deserve reinforcement, and identifying them is part of the investigation's value. The words a report uses for these groups (causal, contributing, other findings, and the safety issue label) come from Settings > Method and wording > Finding terms. Help > Decision tree draws the same path as a diagram.

#Argument mode

#Finalising an object

When an object's evidence is settled, press Finalise at the foot of its form; the corner dot turns green. Finalise saves any unsaved edits in the form along with the lock, then locks it: the button becomes a green Finalised state with Re-open beside it, the title, tests, evidence boxes and notes become read-only, capturing and linking material is hidden, and Delete on a supporting item fades, with "Finalised. Re-open to change it." on hover. To-dos and the Analysis panel stay as they are. Re-open unlocks the form at once. A finalised object cannot be deleted from the map, by the Delete key or the right-click menu: the app names it and asks you to Re-open it first, and in a mixed selection only the unfinalised objects go. The green dot is a statement that the evidence for that object is settled and would withstand review, so finalise deliberately. Then stand back: does every contributing factor have a proven path to the critical event? Are the excluded hypotheses genuinely closed? Exports warn about anything left unfinalised.

Finalise only goes ahead once the tests say enough for what the object claims. Until then the button is greyed and the reason is shown beside it. The critical event needs a passed Existence ("Establish that the critical event occurred before finalising it."). Any other object needs Existence tested ("Test Existence first."); a failed Existence finalises it as an excluded hypothesis. Once Existence passes, Influence must be tested ("Test Influence."), and an object set to Increased risk whose Influence failed or could not be settled needs Importance passed or failed: "Decide Importance: a factor whose contribution could not be settled is either worth addressing or it is not." A positive or neutral object is not asked Importance, so an Influence there that could not be settled is never finalised as it stands: close it as undetermined, or test it again.

A test that could not be settled is not a dead end. Where Existence, or Influence after a passed Existence, is inconclusive, Close as undetermined appears beside Finalise. It needs the box under that test, Reasoning for this test, to say why the evidence could not settle it ("Say why it could not be settled, then close it as undetermined, or test again."). Closing it finalises the object as Undetermined: the banner reads Undetermined (closed), the control reads Closed as undetermined, the Log records it, and the reports list it under Unresolved matters, marked as closed as undetermined. It is never a finding and never a cause. Re-open returns it to an open object, as for any finalised one. Organisations that report only conclusions can turn Close as undetermined off in the organisation configuration (Settings > Organisation > Shared practice, under Reporting what was considered); then an inconclusive test has to be tested again until it passes or fails.

An object finalised before this check existed is left as it was. The record checks list it as an error naming the test and what to decide, so you can re-open it and settle it yourself.

#The Analysis panel

On the left, Analysis: the record (Outcome and consequences: the record, on the critical event) reproduces the object's record exactly as the Record tab would render it, read-only, so the paragraph beside it is always written against what a reader actually sees.

On the right is the paragraph itself, with Finalise, Revert to drafted and Clear. Until it is finalised a badge says where it stands (Not written yet; Draft, not finalised; or Out of date). Finalised, the control turns to a green Finalised state with Re-open beside it, and to an amber Finalised, out of date when the record behind it changes, with Finalise against the current record. 7Record

The record assembled into one document in the chosen style: the source every report and draft is written from.

The investigation as recorded: every section assembled straight from what has been entered, in the order and under the headings of the style you choose. It is not a drafted report, which is a different document written on the Draft report tab. The Record tab shows at No AI and at Copy and paste (so always on a Free licence); at Connected AI it is hidden, because Draft my report writes from the record, and its record checks appear in the Draft report tab's Is the record ready? card (under Worth checking before you draft, errors and warnings only, and they never stop a draft), and the Evidence and Argument Tables export sits in that tab's Also exported card. Switching AI access to Connected AI while you are on the Record tab takes you to the Draft report tab; switching back brings the tab back in its place. Read it here, check it, and export it. #The record is the source

Everything a report says comes from the record: the objects on the map and their tests, the evidence behind them, the timeline, the interviews and the safety actions. A paragraph or a section of prose is a reading of that record at one moment. When you finalise one, the app keeps a fingerprint of the record it was finalised against, so it can tell you later when the record has moved on and the words have not. A model, where you use one, drafts from the record and is checked against it. It never adds to the record, and nothing it writes reaches a report until you have read it and finalised it.

#What the Record tab shows, and when

The Record tab shows the record and nothing else. It is assembled again every time anything in the investigation changes, so there is nothing to refresh, and it never shows drafted text. Change the style and the page re-titles and re-orders itself into that style's layout. Under the controls row it shows, in order:

  • the fictional-case banner, where the case is marked; the Before you draft line; and a summary of the investigation details, with Edit on the Details tab
  • the line Assembled in the (style) layout, and the sections that layout produces, in order
  • a count of what is outstanding across the whole report: factors without an analysis paragraph, events without a narrative, and group and section narratives not yet written
  • every section of the layout, with each object from the map as a card under its own title and classification
    • IF the object's paragraph is finalised and current
      • THEN the card shows the paragraph
    • ELSE IF a paragraph is written but not finalised
      • THEN the card shows it in a muted dark blue, with a thin bar beside it and a small Not finalised tag; click the tag to open the object at its Analysis panel
      • the exported record leaves it out, and the object prints as its heading alone
      • and a Not yet written button under the section opens the object too
    • ELSE IF it was finalised and has since gone out of date
      • THEN the card shows it the same way, tagged Out of date, since nobody has confirmed it against the record as it now stands

      • and an Out of date button under the section opens the object
    • ELSE (nothing written)
      • the object is its heading alone, with no category, test results or evidence under it, here and in every export of the record; the empty heading shows what is still to be written

Export working record to Word, in the controls row, writes the whole record laid out once for you to draft from by hand, with what is still open marked where it appears (see Export the working record below).

There is no switch here to a drafted version and no check of drafted text. The Draft report tab's finalised sections, assembled in the style's order with a marker on any section not yet drafted, not finalised, or out of date, are read, checked and exported there.

#Analysis paragraphs, and the two kinds of finalising

The unit a report is built from is one object's analysis paragraph: the few sentences that argue what the record shows about that object. It is written on the Analysis panel of the object's form (labelled Outcome and consequences on the critical event). A report's analysis section is these paragraphs, arranged by your analysis groups (or one group per lane until you set any), with a linking paragraph for each group. The findings come from the test results themselves, so a paragraph argues a finding but never decides one.

The Analysis panel says what the paragraph is for at your tier. At No AI: "Where you write this object's analysis; finalised, it fills the record and the record export." Where none is finalised, the record prints the object's heading alone (its evidence and test results stay in the working record and the Evidence and Argument Tables), and the record checks do not ask for one. At Copy and paste: "Copy the prompt to your AI chat, paste the paragraph back and finalise it; it fills the record, which exports from the Record tab." The record checks note any finalised object without one. At Connected AI: "Optional. Draft my report writes the analysis from the record; a paragraph you finalise here is kept as your wording for this object." At Copy and paste the paragraphs you finalise are the analysis section of the record export; at Connected AI they are wording the run keeps.

An object's form has two separate Finalise controls, and they answer different questions.

  • The Finalise control at the foot of the form says the object's evidence and tests are settled, and locks the form's fields until you Re-open it. The completion dot on the map turns from amber to green, and the Export dialog stops counting the object as unfinished. It says nothing about any wording.
  • The Finalise button on the Analysis panel accepts the paragraph's wording for the report. Only a finalised paragraph reaches the exported record and the material a drafting pass is given. A paragraph that is written but not finalised is left out of both, because nobody has accepted it yet; the Record tab shows it marked Not finalised, and the review copy written beside a template export carries it in dark blue under a Not finalised comment. Once finalised, the paragraph is read-only and Draft, Revert to drafted and Clear fade, with the reason on hover; Re-open, beside the green Finalised state, is the way back to editing. The same control is on a section narrative and an analysis group's narrative.

Neither one sets the other. The usual order is to settle the evidence and tests, Finalise the object, then write the paragraph and finalise it. Finalising the paragraph first and then recording the test results in the same sitting is fine too: when you save the form, a paragraph finalised while it was open is kept current against the tests you recorded after it.

Once the record behind a finalised paragraph changes later (a test result, a piece of evidence, a causal link), the paragraph is marked Out of date. The badge says so, a note above the text says why with a Re-finalise against the current record button beside it, and the Finalise button reads Finalise against the current record. Read the paragraph against the record on the left, correct it if it needs it, and finalise it again. On the map, the small triangle in the object's bottom-right corner shows where every paragraph stands: green when it is finalised and current, amber when it is written but not finalised or has gone out of date.

#The controls at the top

Under the heading the page has two labelled blocks. Layout holds the Style picker, which sets the layout the page assembles into, and under it Analysis sub-sections, the one control for the analysis section (it is not shown under a style with no analysis section). Export holds the export button, the tick boxes for what goes with the report, and, in the Executive style, Export slides.

Export working record to Word comes first, with its Lay out by switch beside it; the drafted report is exported from the Draft report tab. Where the chosen style is the executive one, Export slides (.pptx) writes the record as a slide deck. Export slides is a finished report, a Professional feature: at Free it is greyed out, with a line saying so and that the working record and the Evidence and Argument Tables export at every level.

Analysis sub-sections answers one question: what are the analysis section's sub-sections, and in what order? It has three options. By lane, occurrence first gives one sub-section per lane of the map, starting at the occurrence events and working up to the organisational influences. By lane, organisation first gives the same lanes the other way round, from external and organisational influences down to the occurrence events, as an ATSB-style report reads. By my own groups gives one sub-section per analysis group you have set up, each with its own linking paragraph; it cannot be chosen until there is at least one group, and Set up groups… beside it opens the analysis groups panel (see Analysis groups). The style's own choice is tagged "default for the (style) layout", and choosing it again simply goes back to the style's default. Where groups are set up but a lane order is chosen, the groups option says they are not used in this order. Where the style's default is the groups and there are none yet, By lane, organisation first shows as chosen, since that is how the analysis runs until there are some, and the groups option says so; leaving it there stores nothing, so the analysis goes by your groups as soon as you set one up. Within each sub-section the objects keep their causal order. The choice is saved with the investigation, and the Draft report tab, its drafting passes and the Word export all follow it. A style sets its own default with an "Analysis order:" line. The same control sits on the analysis section's row on the Draft report tab.

The appendix ticks in the Export block say what goes with the report in the style in view, and are saved in the investigation, one choice per report style: the Draft report tab's What goes with the report card edits the same choice, so both tabs' exports agree. A style with no choice of its own starts from the default in Settings > Reports > Appendices, and Use the default from Settings returns it there. The tick beside them under Evidence and Argument Tables document, List the evidence under each table, belongs to this computer and is described under Also exported.

#Export the working record

The working record is the whole record laid out once, clearly, for drafting by hand: write your own analysis object by object, or keep it open beside you while you write into your own report layout. Nothing in it is drafted and no AI is involved. Every line is a field of the record or something the app works out from it, and the only wording in it is wording you have finalised, marked as yours. It goes to a Word file with a markdown copy beside it, named WorkingRecord, in the exports folder, and it exports at every level. Analysis written but not finalised, or out of date, is not printed; Where the record stands lists it. It takes the look in force for the app's Word documents (Settings > Reports > Look; Modern at Free): the same cover, headings, colours, header and footer as a report exported in the app's own layout, on the same page, with left-aligned text for reading beside a draft. A thin rule marks where each card starts, the contents list the cards and the evidence items as well as the headings above them, and a quiet grey line in the footer says it is a working record.

Lay out by, beside the button, has two choices, kept with the investigation:

  • Part of the record, the default: where the record stands (what is not finalised, what is unsettled, the record checks), how the chosen style uses the record, at a glance, the sequence of events, a card per object, the findings in the style's terms, the evidence register and the accounts. Each part is printed once and the shape is the same whichever style is chosen.
  • Report sections: where the record stands, then one chapter per section of the chosen style in the order the sections are drafted, each carrying the parts that section draws on. A part is printed in full the first time a section needs it, and later sections point back to it.

Each object's card is compact: its lane and status, then its category and the three tests, the evidence, significance and notes recorded, its supporting material, what it led to and from, any safety actions, and your finalised wording where there is some. Each Supporting material entry is a link, underlined, to the item under Evidence; click it in Word to go there. Each item is headed by its caption, the same words as the link, with its reference on the line below. The sequence of events is one line per timeline event, the narrative in place of the detail where one is written, with the evidence it rests on beneath. Under Evidence each text extract prints in a thin bordered box, as a figure does, and so does each image ticked for the report. Nothing prints twice: an extract attached to a safety report, or a specialist's reply under an escalation, is named there with a link back to Evidence. A card with no wording yet says nothing about it, since Where the record stands lists those. A finalised synopsis's own headings sit one level below its interview's.

An interview is printed only once its account is finalised; until then it is named as not finalised and listed under where the record stands. Where the interview has a finalised interview summary that still matches its record, the summary is printed in place of the transcript and notes; if the record has changed since, the record is printed again. Only the images ticked to go into the report are printed. The timeline image follows the sequence when its appendix tick is on. The Log and the checklist are never in it.

#Investigation details

Investigation details are edited on the Details tab, and the Record tab shows a summary of them under the controls with an Edit on the Details tab link. They hold the facts a cover carries: the reference, the launch date, the occurrence date and time (marked UTC or local), the location, the flight, voyage or service, the vehicle or vessel type and its registration or identifier, the operator, where it went from and to, the lead investigator and the other investigators, and the classification, status and version. The Details tab shows the form, then Report labelling, the Standard of proof card and the distribution list; the details save as you type, like everything else in the app, and Undo takes back one field's typing at a time. The Log records each change: a choice when it is made, a typed field once you leave it, as "Details: field changed from old to new". A date, time or reference in the wrong form is shown with its message and not saved until it is corrected; while it stands, an export that uses the details does nothing and says what to correct. Enter them once and every document the app generates fills its cover from them as far as it has a place for each one: the record and report exports as Word, the Evidence and Argument Tables. Every field is optional, and a document with no place for one simply leaves it out. The Title in the form is the investigation's own name, so changing it renames the investigation everywhere.

The occurrence date you set is the date the summary states and the summary check looks for. Until one is set, the app takes it from the timeline, which can only guess at which entry is the occurrence itself, so setting it is worth doing.

New investigation offers the same form, so the details can be set up at the start; the launch date starts as today. Once a reference is set, New investigation names the folder and file with the reference then the title, and Save as suggests the same name. A value saved before a list applied, or one the list no longer carries, still shows, marked as not in the list, and is kept until you pick another. Where a field lets you tick several, such as the external notifications, it may end with an Other tick: tick it and a box appears to name the body concerned, and that name is written into the cover and the templates in place of the word Other. Unticking Other hides the box but keeps what you typed, so ticking it again brings the name back.

#Fictional case

On the Details tab, under the form, Report labelling holds Fictional case, which marks this investigation as a case written for teaching. While it is ticked, a banner across the top of the Record tab shows the fictional-case label, and every document the investigation exports carries the same label in its running header and its footer. The tick is saved in the investigation file, so the case stays marked on any machine it is opened on; the wording of the label is set in Settings > Reports > Fictional cases. The worked example and the case studies are marked this way.

Not an investigation by this organisation." The choice is saved in the investigation file, so that wording is the same on every machine the case is opened on, whatever its Settings say.

#Standard of proof

On the Details tab, Standard of proof shows the standard this investigation is analysed to: its name, where Pass starts and Fail stops, the three bands as a bar, and how many of the recorded tests agree with it. A new investigation records this computer's default (set in Settings > Method and wording > Standard of proof) when it is created. An investigation saved before the standard was recorded says Not recorded and reads this computer's default meanwhile; Record this standard writes that default into it, and Choose another... opens the editor.

Change... opens the same editor as Settings, with Use this computer's default beside it. Recording or changing the standard is one step you can undo, and it is written to the Log (“Standard of proof changed from Likely or probable (Pass from 65%, Fail to 35%) to Balance of probabilities (Pass from 51%, Fail to 49%)”). It changes no result on its own. Where some recorded tests read differently against the new standard, a window lists them, object by object, with Re-read the tests on objects not finalised, Change the standard only, or Cancel. A finalised object is never re-read: it is listed, and changes only once you re-open it. The report states the standard in one sentence at the head of its Findings section, so a changed standard also puts a drafted report out of date.

#Record checks

Record checks, down the right of the page, lists everything the record itself can already tell you is wrong or incomplete: an object with no arrows to any other, an object recorded as influencing the event whose Existence is not established, an object finalised with a test still inconclusive, a group with no linking paragraph, a finalised object whose tests the finalise gate would not let through (say, finalised with Existence untested), named with the test and what to decide, and never changed for you; a missing outcome, a factor with no path to the critical event, a recommendation with no safety action, an object whose lane is not one of the investigation's lanes (the exports list it under Uncategorised until you move it), an Existence note worded as a measured fact while its own grading sits below virtually certain, a test whose written reasoning still states a different result or percentage from the one recorded (a note saying "Inconclusive at 50%" under a Pass at 80%), a Pass with nothing written for it, a test whose saved result its probability no longer reads as against the standard of proof, no standard of proof recorded on an investigation with tests, an arrow that points backwards in time (read from the times recorded against the objects themselves where there are any, and only from where they sit on the canvas when there are not), prose that has fallen out of date. They are grouped under Errors (n), Warnings (n) and Notes (n). Each entry that names an object is a link: click it to open that object at the point the problem sits.

#Also exported

At the foot of the page, Also exported previews everything that leaves with the record besides the report body. The Evidence and Argument Tables card holds one collapsible table per object, grouped under its lane, with Export Evidence and Argument Tables as Word beside it: the Existence, Influence and Importance results with the text recorded for each, which is the working paper behind every finding. That Word file is a document in its own right: a cover, a contents page listing every lane and every object under it, a page on how to read the tables, the map on a landscape page, then each object's table, headed by its hypothesis and kept whole on one page wherever it fits. Each table runs down the page the same way. The object's hypothesis heads the table, and a strip under it gives the result of each test that was carried out, such as "Existence: Pass, virtually certain (100%)". Each test then has a block of its own, titled Test for Existence, Test for Influence or Test for Importance, listing the points for as F1, F2 and so on and the points against as A1, A2 and so on (Nil where nothing was recorded for the factor; where nothing was recorded against it, the block simply has no points against). Only the word Hypothesis is bold, so a long hypothesis stays easy to read. A shaded row closes the block with the reasoning you wrote under the slider and the result in brackets, coloured green for a pass, rust for a fail and amber for an inconclusive result. A test with a result but nothing written under it still appears in the strip but has no block; a test with no result is left out of the strip.

The tick List the evidence under each table, in the Export block at the top, adds under each object's table a line per attached item naming where it came from rather than the material itself. The AcciMap and timeline images go into the Word export through the appendix ticks in the same block.

#Analysis groups

Set up groups…, beside By my own groups in Analysis sub-sections, opens where the analysis section's cross-factor argument lives: the linking paragraph that says how a set of factors relate to each other and lead towards the critical event, which no single factor's own paragraph can carry. With no groups defined, the analysis falls back to one group per lane, shown in the editor read-only with no linking prose.

Set up groups… opens the Analysis groups panel. Propose by lane and Propose by mechanism each build a set of groups for you to adjust; Clear groups drops them back to one group per lane with no linking prose. Within a group, its title field renames it, Move up and Move down reorder it against the others, and the picker on each factor moves that factor into a different group (a factor left in no group shows under Ungrouped in the panel and prints last in the report, under "Other safety factors"). Copy and paste

The app builds the prompt and you carry it to a chat tool your organisation has approved for investigation material, not a personal account, and the reply back. The app makes no network call of its own; you are the transport, and you see exactly what is being sent because you are the one pasting it. The copied prompt asks the model to draft the text, audit that draft against the record it was given (every claim found in the record, every probability word from the scale the prompt sets out, no test notation in the prose), correct it, and return only the corrected text. So there is one paste each way, and no separate audit to copy out. For a section, and for each part of one, the prompt can also ask for the corrected text to be polished before it is returned, using the same wording as Polish the prose, which may change how it reads and nothing it says. It is off unless you tick Ask the chat tool to polish in the same reply, under Copy and paste in Settings > AI drafting > AI access. With it on, a fresh draft is marked Polished in the reply and shows no polish button; the separate polish prompt appears as Re-polish after your edits (under More on a section) once you have changed the text by hand. With it off, Copy polish prompt for this section is there as soon as the section has text.

  1. Open the object's Analysis panel and press Copy prompt. Under the button, a line gives the prompt's size in characters and estimated tokens, with a warning when it is longer than the chat paste limit (1,000,000 characters), since a chat window may quietly take only part of a long paste. Past the limit, use Save prompt as file, which writes the prompt out as a text file, and attach the file to the chat instead; a report section's prompt is nearly all record, so splitting it into two pastes does not bring it under. Where Settings > AI drafting > AI access sets the chat to two pastes, Copy prompt is replaced by 1. Copy instructions and 2. Copy record, pasted in that order.
  2. Start a new chat for each prompt, as the line under the button says, then paste it in and wait for the reply. Every prompt carries the record, so a chat still holding the last one can run out of room before it has read this one.
  3. Paste the reply into the box under Paste the reply here and press Use this text (Cancel goes back without it). It lands as a draft, badged Pasted from chat. A chat tool often wraps the text anyway, so a lead-in line ("Here is the paragraph:"), a code fence around the text and a closing offer ("Let me know if you want changes") are taken off as it lands, and a note says what was removed. Only an unmistakable wrapper goes; anything else stays for you to read. A line under the box says Reply accepted. A reply that holds nothing but review notes is not used, and the box says why, with your pasted text left in place.
  4. Read it against the record on the left. A pasted reply gets no automatic check, because you are the one who brought it back and signs it. Correct it, then press Finalise.

That is one way to a report: paragraph by paragraph, then Export working record to Word from the Record tab and finish it in Word. The Draft report card has an Open the Record tab button for this. A section works the same way on the Draft report tab.

  1. Open Work section by section. The row marked Next among the sections is open when you arrive; click any other row to open it instead.

  3. Read the section in its row, correct it there and press Finalise. Its row closes with a tick and the next one opens.
  4. #The style line

One line at the top of the tab names the style the report is written in. Where the edition has more than one style, a Style select sits beside the line to pick another. Hover over the line to read what the style is for, in the style's own words. Where the app ships a worked example drafted in that style, an Example (PDF) link on the same line opens it in your PDF reader. The same example is under Help, Example report (PDF), from any tab and without a licence; with no example for the open style it opens the first one the app ships.

There is no separate list of what the style produces: the sections below, every row closed, are what it produces, in the order it prints them, and the analysis section's part rows are how it is arranged. Each open row has About this section, closed until you open it, holding what goes in the section, the style's own guidance points and which parts of the record it is drafted from. All of it is read out of the style itself, so an organisation's own style describes itself here the same way the shipped ones do.

The style decides the sections, their order, and what each one may be drafted from. These are the styles this edition ships with:

  • Standard investigation. The full investigation report, and the longest of these styles: executive summary, event overview, PEEPO analysis, findings summary and appendices. Written for the safety department and the people who have to act on it.
  • Preliminary investigation update. About three pages for executives while the investigation is still running: what is known, what is still being looked at, and what is already being done. Carries no findings.
  • ATSB-style final report. A full-length public report in the ATSB shape: investigation summary, the occurrence, context, safety analysis, findings, then safety issues and the action taken against them. Written for an industry readership. Modelled on published ATSB reports (CC BY 4.0).
  • NTSB-style probable cause report. A full-length public report in the NTSB shape: factual information, analysis, then conclusions carrying the findings and a single probable cause statement with its contributing factors, followed by recommendations. Modelled on published NTSB reports (US government work, public domain).
  • AAIB-style causal and contributory factors report. A full-length public report in the AAIB shape: synopsis, factual information, analysis, then conclusions listing the findings and splitting causal from contributory factors, followed by safety action and recommendations. Modelled on published AAIB reports (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence v3.0).
  • Executive brief. Three to four pages of plain language for the people who decide: what happened, why it happened, what was found, and what is being done. No factual detail beyond what carries the argument.

What one run does

Draft my report is one AI run, and the card shows its steps in plain words as it goes:

  1. Reading the investigation, which builds the outline.
  2. Writing each section in turn, in the order sections are written, which is not always the order they print: a summary printed first is written last, from the sections before it.
  3. Smoothing the sections into one report, the cohesion edit, unless it is turned off in Settings > AI drafting > Drafting options.
  4. Checking every claim against the investigation, the audit. It reads the whole report in one go, or section by section automatically where the checking model's loaded context cannot hold the whole. No question is asked.
  5. Correcting what the check found, the repair pass over the flagged sections.
  6. Checking the report's figures, findings and wording, the app's own record check, with no model.
  7. Writing the review copy and the report, both Word files, to the investigation's exports folder.

A correction lands by itself only when the repair pass's own guards pass: the re-audit is clean or better, no recorded fact was lost, there is no new record-check error and the reply holds no chatter. Each landed correction is one undo step and gets a comment in the review copy saying what the sentence said before. A correction that fails a guard is not landed, and its claims stay marked in the review copy. If you edit a section while the run is working, its correction waits on the repair panel under Work section by section instead of landing. Polish is not part of the run unless Polish the whole report at the end of Draft my report is ticked in Settings > AI drafting > Drafting options.

While it runs, the card reads Drafting the report, with the step count (Step 4 of 9), the step in those plain words, a bar, the time elapsed, an estimate of the time left and Stop. The estimate appears once there is something to base it on (the first section done, or this machine's times from an earlier run) and is worked out again every second, so a slow step lengthens it. The step count is higher than the list above because each section is its own step. Raw log, in Run details, shows the run call by call.

A Rough draft

When the model's Drafting mode is Rough draft (Settings > AI drafting > AI models, the default for a model on this computer or your own network), the run reads the investigation, writes each section and the summary, runs the record check and writes both Word files. It skips the cohesion edit and the audit, so nothing is corrected, and it tidies two slips local models make: a finding number the report has nothing to match, and stray formatting code a model writes round a word. Times and facts are never rewritten: a section that gives the record's UTC times beside local ones without saying which is flagged in the record check instead. The card says Rough draft on this computer, with the time it took last time on that model, and an amber line reminds you to check every time, figure and reference against the record before you use it, with a link to change the mode. You can still run the audit and the cohesion edit yourself under Work section by section.

If the run stops or fails

Stop asks the model to let go, which can take a moment; the run reads Stopping until it has. A stopped run shows Stopped partway, says what was drafted and says that no check ran and no Word files were written. A stop during the check keeps the draft but writes no files; a stop during the corrections keeps the corrections already landed. Draft again starts over, and Write and open the review copy (unchecked) writes the Word file from what is there, with no AI check behind it. A section not reached that has no text yet is marked NOT DRAFTED, and one that already has text keeps it. Undo takes a stopped landing back in one step. On a hosted service the tokens already generated are billed either way.

A failed run shows The draft stopped, with where it stopped and why, for example "Stopped at step 4 of 9 (Writing the Findings summary): the endpoint did not answer." The sections already drafted are kept. Try again from step 4 continues from the step that failed, keeping the sections already drafted rather than paying for them again (or, after the sections landed, keeping the draft and going on with the check). Check the endpoint in Settings opens Settings at Models. Run details opens by itself after a failure.

Work section by section

Work section by section is a disclosure under Run details, closed until you open it and remembered open on this machine. Part rows

An argued section, drafted a lane, a group or a safety issue at a time, has a row of its own for each part inside its section row, numbered under it (4.1, 4.2 and so on) and in the order the section prints them. The first part not yet in is open when you open the section; open and close the others as you like, and they stay as you left them. An open part row holds the controls that get its words, then the part's own text, edited in place like any section, so an edit there can only ever change that part. At the Copy and paste tier it holds Copy prompt for this part and its paste box (a lane in two halves shows both halves' controls in its one row). With the polish clause in the copy prompt (Settings, off by default) the part says it was polished in the same reply, and once you have edited it by hand the part offers Re-polish after your edits, with a box for the polished wording, which replaces that part alone; with the clause switched off, Copy polish prompt for this part is shown as soon as the part is in. At the Connected AI tier it holds Redraft this part, which drafts that part again just as a whole-section run would, given the parts before it as they now stand, and puts the result in place of that part only; and Polish this part, which runs the polish pass, with its audit and retention check, over that part and offers the result beside the other polish results to land. A redraft that does not complete changes nothing. Polish, at either tier, is offered only on a part wholly in, and polished wording that would drop a NOT DRAFTED marker, or that arrives after the part has changed, is refused with the reason and nothing changes. Where the section was drafted whole rather than in these parts, the row says so, and Draft this part asks before it replaces that text, since Undo cannot bring it back once the investigation is closed. Finalise stays with the section, never the part. If an edit removes a part's heading so the parts can no longer be told apart, the section says so, the part rows keep their controls, and the section's text is edited as a whole under them; redrafting or clearing the section brings the parts back. With AI access off an argued section is simply one text to edit.

Clearing a section

To start a section again, press Clear on its row. A confirmation asks first, then the section goes back to not started, exactly as if it had never been drafted: its text, your edits, its approval and its Hand-edited tag are all gone, and the next Draft starts clean. Clear all, at the head of the sections, does the same to every drafted section of the style on show and to nothing else (other styles, the whole-report document and the review notes stay as they are). Straight afterwards a line under the Sections heading offers Undo, which puts back what was cleared, and the Log records the clear and any undo. Undo never overwrites a section you have drafted again since. A clear, an edit and a draft, repair or polish landing are also ordinary steps in the investigation's history, so Ctrl+Z (outside a text box) takes back the latest of them as well.

#Your own text for a section

Any section of the chosen style can be your own text instead of a draft. This suits working at the Copy and paste tier: once the evidence is in, you might write the event overview yourself in Word, then draft the rest in chat so it agrees with what you wrote. Press Write or paste your own in the section's open row on this tab, then type or paste the section and press Save as my text. The Record tab does not offer it: a section with no objects behind it is written there as a section narrative instead, from its Not yet written button. Headings, bold, italic, lists and paragraphs come across from Word as the report's own editor keeps them, and the text-style control offers H1, H2 and H3 (a heading level 1 inside the text is saved as level 2, so it cannot start a section of its own).

  • The section is badged Investigator's text wherever it shows, and counts as finalised. The document view and the finished report, in a template or in the app's own layout, use it exactly as written, and you can go on editing it in its row or with Edit your text.
  • No draft replaces it. Draft my report keeps it as written, and the section's own Draft and Copy prompt are held back, until you tick Replace my text too, for this run. Clear all leaves it alone too. Return to drafting drops it and puts the section back to not started; Undo brings it back.
  • The prompt for every other section, copied or sent, carries your text under "Investigator's own text for:" and the section name, as wording the draft must agree with and not contradict. A prompt carries nothing extra while no section holds your text.
  • Your text is not audited against the record: it is your own wording and checking it is yours, as with anything pasted back from a chat. An audit of the other sections reads it as context they were told to agree with.

The Log records your text being set, replaced by a draft, and removed.

#Editing a section, and reading it as a document

Read the draft here, under the Your draft is ready card, and Read as a document, at the head of the sections, show every section in the style's printed order as one document, as the finished report is written, with its figures and appendix, so you can read the report through. It is read-only: Back to the sections returns to the rows. A section not yet drafted shows its heading and a marker saying so; one gone out of date carries its marker above it. Under each drafted section, here and in its row, is its actual word count. Where the style caps the section's length and the section has run well past the cap (the point at which Check the record raises it), the line names the cap too, for example "1,980 words, over the style's 1,500-word cap". While no section is drafted for the style it says so in one line instead, with how many object analysis paragraphs are finalised; those are read in the Record tab's assembled view.

Editing a section

The main path edits in Word, in the review copy; the app keeps the drafted text, which is what a redraft, a polish or a fresh copy reads. To work on the text here instead, open Work section by section (Write it here at No AI) and click into a section's text, in its open row, to edit it where it stands, as the document it is: a small toolbar appears with undo and redo, two heading levels, bold, italic, underline, bulleted and numbered lists and a block quote, and paragraphs work as they do in any word processor. There is no markdown to edit. Each section, and each part of an argued section, is its own editing area, so an edit can only ever change the section or part it is in. Closing the row keeps the edit. Behind the page the section is still stored as markdown, because that is what the model writes, what the next section receives as context, and what the checks, the audit and the exporter read; your edit is written back to it, and merely opening or reading the report changes nothing. A finalised section is read-only until you Re-open it. An edit marks the section Hand-edited in its row, and Revert to drafted, in its row, returns it to the drafted wording. Tables and figures show as they will print but are not edited here. A bracketed marker, such as [NOT ESTABLISHED], is part of the text and must be resolved before a draft goes anywhere. It is highlighted here and set in bold in every Word export, so one left behind is hard to miss.

Every formatting option reaches the Word file as the same formatting: underline prints underlined, a block quote prints as an indented quote with a rule beside it, and the two heading levels print as Word headings. Underline has no plain-markdown form, so it is stored as <u>…</u>; the record check and the audit read the words without the tags. Nothing you format changes what the checks find.

A Findings section whose list contains a finding graded at a band that may be stated with no hedge at all (by default, virtually certain) opens with one sentence saying so: "A finding stated without a likelihood term was established as fact." It is there for the reader, so a plain finding among hedged ones reads as a deliberate statement of fact rather than a missing word, and the Record tab, the Word export and the drafted report all carry it in the same place. It is fixed wording: a drafting pass is told to reproduce it exactly, and the record check and the model audit both leave it alone. Where every finding carries a term, the sentence is left out.

#Check, and the files the run writes

Inside Work section by section sits the Check band. It holds Check the record at every tier; at the Connected AI tier Audit against the record joins it, then Repair against the audit once an audit has run, and Polish the prose. At the Copy and paste tier and with AI access off the last three are not shown at all. Draft my report runs the audit, the repair and the record check as steps of its own run, so these buttons are for running one of them again by itself, after you have changed a section. Polish is never part of the run. Once there is a whole report document, the head of the Check band carries Apply to: the sections, or the whole report document. Repair and polish work over whichever it points at, and their buttons say so (Repair the sections, Polish the whole report document); the two are separate, and one never changes the other. A line under the buttons names any section whose copy in the whole report document no longer matches the section itself, so you know which one carries your latest work. Each action carries a line saying what it does.

Check the record is mechanical, not a model opinion: each factor's category and confidence word checked against what its own test result actually says, and, for a Findings section, its categories checked against the derived classification. It also reads the prose itself: more than a couple of semicolons per thousand words is flagged as a sign the text has not been edited into full sentences, a section that takes its points in turn ("The first was ... The second was ...") is flagged as reading like a list rather than an argument, and a Findings bullet that negates its subject ("No assessment was likely recorded") is flagged too, because the term then qualifies what did not happen rather than the absence itself; it belongs in front of the whole statement instead ("it is likely that no assessment was recorded"). It runs automatically once an endpoint draft lands (never on a pasted chat reply, which is yours to read before it lands), and the button runs it on demand over everything currently drafted.

A drafted analysis states each factor's conclusion at its recorded probability term once, in the sentence that concludes on it, written in the drafter's own words. Around that sentence the section may restate the conclusion plainly, as "the Investigation concluded that" or "the evidence showed that", where the factor passed its test, so the term is not repeated on every sentence. Check the record warns when no sentence concluding on a contributing factor carries its term, or one of that term's accepted phrasings, because in a report with no Findings list the analysis is the only place a reader can compare it. In such a report the summary keeps the terms too, once per factor, and Check the record reads it the same way. A factor recorded as virtually certain may be stated plainly throughout. A style whose summary grades nothing (one whose findings tables carry each factor's level) has the summary state the key findings plainly, and Check the record flags any probability term it gives. Repair against the audit is told the same, and a repair that adds a grade to such a summary is not landed.

It also reads each factor's conclusion against the map. A contributing safety factor drafted under a heading such as "Unresolved: fatigue" is flagged as an error, and so is a factor whose test failed or was inconclusive drafted under a contributing heading. A sentence saying a contributing factor was not found to have contributed, was set aside or was left unresolved is flagged as a warning, since a sentence can be about part of the factor rather than the whole. The test results are the record, so change the draft, or change the result first if it is the result that is wrong.

It also checks the figures. A number that reads as garbled (a code split by a space such as "SB51 2", a decimal comma such as "61,4", a unit run into its number such as "4m", or a lone "0" left where a figure was broken) is flagged, unless the record itself writes it that way, as a METAR does. A measurement with a unit that the record carries nowhere ("4 m" where the record says 4.5 m) is flagged for you to check; one worked out from the record, a conversion or a difference, is fine to keep, and your own text is not held to this. A report that gives some times in UTC and others in local time is flagged in each section that does, unless it states the offset between them somewhere ("UTC+9:30"). A reference to a finding that does not exist ("Finding 9" when there are seven) is flagged too. Repair against the audit acts on a garbled figure, with the record in front of it, but leaves a missing figure and the clocks to you. An analysis that is the first section to give a figure or a document reference (a teardown result, an assessment's number) is flagged too, since a reader should meet every fact in the factual sections first.

A draft states its times in local time when the whole occurrence was in one time zone and the timeline knows the local offset from UTC (Timeline settings), even when the timeline stores them in UTC, and says the basis and offset once. Where the record spans zones, or no offset is set, the draft stays in the clock the timeline stores. The cover date of an exported report is the occurrence's local date on the same basis, unless you set the date in the investigation details.

It also checks how people are referred to. A pronoun is a fact about a person, so a "he" or "her" for someone the record never refers to that way (the record calls the pilot in command "they", say, and the draft writes "he") is flagged in the section, naming the pronoun and the role it seems to be about. Words quoted from the record are left alone, and so are a "she" for a vessel or an aircraft and the generic "he or she". Each person is checked on their own: the record calling a witness "he" does not make a "he" for the crew right, and a "he" with no one named near it is flagged too. Words such as "man" or "Mr" are not checked. It reads the words around the pronoun rather than the grammar, so treat it as worth checking rather than certain, and your own text is not held to it.

Audit against the record, at the Connected AI tier, is the model pass over every drafted section, finalised or not, and is offered as soon as anything is drafted. Draft my report runs it for you and picks the shape itself; run by hand, it comes in two shapes, chosen under the button. The whole report in one read checks the report against the whole investigation, which is the only way to catch two sections that are each defensible on their own and together say different things; it needs the checking model loaded with room for the whole collation. Before it starts on a server on this machine, the app reads how much context the checking model is loaded with, and if that is too little it refuses before any call, giving both figures, with Audit section by section instead beside the reason. Section by section audits each drafted section against what it was drafted from, one smaller read each, which fits any model; each section's result shows on its own row and as a line in the Check band (such as "Analysis: 3 to resolve") that opens it. Audit this section on a row after a whole-report read replaces that section's part of it only: the whole-report audit stays, marked as partly superseded. When a draft lands over text an audit read, the audit is kept and marked Audited an earlier draft, and Repair does not act on it; audit again to check the text as it now stands. The Copy and paste tier has no whole-report audit; its drafting prompts audit themselves instead. What the audit flags is what Repair against the audit acts on, and Polish the prose is the step after it; both are described under Audit, repair and polish.

The last step of Draft my report writes two Word files to the investigation's exports folder: the finished report and the review copy. Where the edition supplies a template for the style the report is drafted in, the report goes into it; otherwise it is in the app's own layout. The review copy is always written beside it. Every drafted section goes out as drafted: no section has to be finalised first, and the report carries no NOT FINALISED marker. A section finalised and since out of date prints no notice in the finished report: the review copy comments on it at its heading, and the Your draft is ready card names it. Before the run, the card names any finalised section the investigation has changed since, and Draft them again too (ticked unless you untick it) drafts those with the rest, while a finalised section still current is kept as written; the done card then names the sections the run kept rather than drafted. A section not drafted carries NOT DRAFTED. The cover page records the report as incomplete in a Status line under its date of issue. Where any section holds a NOT DRAFTED or TRUNCATED marker (a section not drafted, a chat part not yet pasted, a pass that failed or a draft cut short), the Your draft is ready card names those sections, and the run log names them again.

What goes out behind the report itself is chosen per report: the What goes with the report card shows it for the style in view as a chip for each attachment that goes out, and Change opens the ticks (the Record tab's export edits the same choice). A chip is hollow, and says "nothing to include yet", where the investigation has nothing for it yet, such as a timeline with no events; the tick is kept for the day there is. A line under the chips says whether the choice is this report's own or this computer's default from Settings. Each report style keeps its own choice in the investigation, so an Executive summary need not carry the tables a longer report does; a style with no choice of its own starts from Settings > Reports > Appendices. The choices are an image of the AcciMap, an image of the timeline, the timeline down portrait pages with the event table beside it, the Evidence and Argument Tables and the similar events register. Each chosen one is appended after the report body under its own heading, and where the template the report goes into has no Appendices place, those choices are disabled and say so. The AcciMap image starts chosen; the rest start unchosen. The two images each take a landscape page of their own; the timeline can instead go down portrait pages with the event table beside it, in sequence spacing, as many pages as it needs. An organisation template filled from the report places the map and timeline images the same way. Its Appendices also carry the Evidence and Argument Tables when that choice is on, in the template's own headings and ahead of the landscape picture pages. The choice belongs to the investigation, one per report style, and Use the default from Settings puts a style back on this computer's default. The Draft tab's card and the Record tab's export edit the same choice. An appendix with nothing to show, a timeline with no events on it, is left out rather than printed empty, and so is one the style already prints itself: where a style's own body carries the Evidence and Argument Tables, that choice says "already in this style". The Evidence and Argument Tables option List the evidence under each table adds, under each object's table, a line per attached item naming where it came from rather than the material itself, the same option the Record tab's Also exported card carries.

The review copy is a second Word file written beside the report: the same report with what the run's checks found marked in place, for your own reading before the report goes anywhere. It is always written, whether or not anything was found. Each mark carries a Word comment by IWS written as plain advice: what is wrong with the words, what the investigation does say about it, and what to do, then who found it (the app's own check, marked as needing a fix, worth a look or for your information, or the AI check, with the model and the time). A short hidden reference at the end of each comment names the check behind it, for tracing; turn on Show hidden text in Word to see it. Audit findings are highlighted on the sentence they are about: yellow where nothing in the record supports the words, pink where the record says something different, orange where the words go further than the record, and green for one you have marked resolved, with the date you marked it. The app's own checks of the record are blue where something needs fixing and grey where it is worth checking, on the sentence naming the object they are about (narrowed to the word they quote, where they quote one), or on the section heading when they are about the section as a whole. A correction the run landed carries a comment saying what the sentence said before. Notes the AI made about the investigation while drafting are comments on their section's heading. Where the draft marked something the record does not hold, with a [NOT ESTABLISHED: ...] marker, the finished report and its markdown are written without the marker, its punctuation tidied. Where the marker was what the sentence was about ("... is [NOT ESTABLISHED: ...]."), the sentence would not stand without it, so the whole sentence is left out and its comment in the review copy quotes it, marker and all. A heading left with nothing under it is left out too; the review copy keeps that heading and has a comment where each marker was, quoting its words, and one comment on the first heading lists every gap. The same list, Gaps the draft found, is in the Check band, so you can add what is missing and redraft, or leave out what the report does not need. A finding whose sentence cannot be picked out, because it spans two sentences or the wording has changed, goes on the section heading, and its comment says so. The file is named ReviewCopy, and its footer says it is a review copy and not for distribution, as does its header where the page carries the app's own header rather than your organisation's. When the report goes into your organisation's template, the review copy is written into the same template, with the same cover, headings and tables, and the saved notice lists both files, the finished report first. Otherwise it is in the app's own layout. The markdown copy is never marked. An audit that read an earlier draft of a section is left out, as are the rows of a whole-report audit for a section since audited on its own. The Whole report document offers a review copy beside its own export, marking the whole report document; since every audit reads the drafted sections rather than that document, its comments say so.

#Figures in the report

Figures travel with the report. An image you ticked on the Evidence tab goes in after the paragraph that first says "Figure 3", and where the prose never mentions it, at the end of the section that argues the object it belongs to; a capture no object claims closes the document in a Figures appendix. It prints at the text width inside a thin black border, with "Figure 3: your caption" under it. Every drafting pass, Draft my report included, is given a short list of the figures (number, caption, and the object each supports) and asked to cite one as "(Figure 3)" where a statement relies on it, but whether the argument needs it is left to the draft and to you. In the Word file the figures are numbered in the order a reader meets them, and every reference moves with its figure, so a figure cited first in the factual sections is Figure 1 wherever the record had it. In Word the number is a real caption field and every "Figure 3" in the text is a cross reference to it, so Insert Caption, Insert Cross-reference and a table of figures all work on what the app wrote, and adding a figure by hand renumbers the rest. A reference to two figures, such as "Figures 2 and 3", is written as two cross references, "Figure 2 and Figure 3", as Word itself would insert them, so updating the fields with F9 changes nothing. The Record tab's assembled view, each section's text and the document view show the figures in the same places as the Word file, under the numbers the drafting was given. The record checks warn of a "Figure N" with no figure behind it, and note a ticked figure the whole report never cites.

Findings tables are captioned the same way. Where the report prints a table for each finding, each one sits under a Word caption, "Table F2: the finding's title", numbered as the finding is on the maps, and every finding cited in brackets in the text, "(F2)" or "(Finding 2)", is a cross reference to that caption, so Ctrl+click in Word goes to the table. A finding cited in brackets that has no table in the report stays as plain words: the record checks list it, and an organisation template's export notes say so too.

A drafted section says "Figure 3" and nothing more, and what Figure 3 is gets settled again when the report is exported. So if you tick another image onto a factor the report argues earlier, every number below it shifts and sentences written about one picture now point at another. The record checks say so: a section drafted against a different numbering is reported as out of step, naming what each number now points at, and you redraft the section or correct the numbers yourself. Nothing is silently rewritten, because only you can tell which the sentence meant.

#The whole report

Whole report document (advanced), the last item under Work section by section, is closed until you open it (it opens by itself while a held whole report is waiting). It holds the report written as one piece. The cohesion step of Draft my report lands its result here: with an endpoint that has the context for the whole investigation, the report is written as one argument rather than assembled from sections drafted apart. Each of its sections also lands in its own row as a draft. A section you have already finalised is never overwritten; the run log, under Raw log in Run details, names any it kept, and says which of those were finalised before the record changed and still need redrafting or finalising again. Any claim the audit marks unsupported or contradicted is the model's invention and must be fixed or cut.

Smooth the whole report instead edits your finalised sections together into one document: shared terms and tense, transitions between sections, repetition removed. It never adds, removes, or reweighs a finding, and it never replaces the section texts above it. If the model declines the edit, or sends back something that is not the report (a heading missing, or far shorter than what it was given), its reply is not used: each section is smoothed on its own instead, a section it cannot smooth stays as drafted, and the review notes say why. An audit the model declines is shown as an audit that did not complete, never as a clean one. A section finalised before the record changed is given to the smoothing only as guidance, marked out of date, and is not edited into the smoothed report; it is named under the Whole report area until you redraft it or finalise it again. The sections drafted after it are given it the same way, as guidance rather than approved text. When every finalised section is out of date, which one change to the record does, there is nothing current to smooth: the Whole report area says so and names them, and the smoothing comes back once you finalise one of them again. Either way the whole-report text is edited in place the same way as the sections, with its own Finalise, Revert to drafted (back to the last pass) and Clear (drop the whole-report document and return to the section-by-section report alone). Once finalised it is read-only, with Re-open beside the green Finalised state, and Revert, Clear and the smoothing action fade until you Re-open it. It goes out of date the moment a finalised section changes underneath it, or goes out of date against the record. Export whole report (Export smoothed report for a smoothed one) writes it out as its own Word file, separate from the finished report, so it is never confused with the section-by-section report.

Both runs land only onto the whole report they started from. If you edit the whole-report text while Draft my report or the smoothing is running, your edit is kept and nothing is put over it: the finished report waits in the Whole report area with the text the run started from, the text as it now stands and the new text side by side. Land over my edits replaces your edits with it, as one step Undo reverses; Discard keeps the whole report as you left it. A Draft my report run still lands its sections in their rows either way. Edit the text back to where the run started and a plain Land is offered again. A repair or polish of the whole report document lands the same way, one section's part of the document at a time: it goes in only where the text it was made from is still in the document exactly once. Edited since, the repair waits on its panel with Land over my edits and Discard; a part whose text appears twice in the document is never put in at a guess. While a result is held, its record check says it is about the held result, not yet in the report; discarding the result clears them.

#How a section draft is assembled

The Draft report tab writes the report in the chosen style, one section at a time. When a section is drafted, the model is given the assembled record for that section: your finalised paragraphs and narratives, marked to be kept as you wrote them (an unfinalised one is left out, though the Record tab still shows it, marked Not finalised); the outline block for any object that has none; and an out-of-date paragraph as guidance only, never as wording to keep, since nobody has confirmed it still fits the record. The model is also given the sections already finalised before this one, so it follows on from your approved text instead of repeating it.

An argued section such as the analysis is drafted a few factors at a time, by your analysis groups or by lane, and the row's status line says how many parts that is. The more paragraphs you have finalised, the less the model has to write for itself: a section drafted from a fully finalised record is mostly your own words, arranged and joined.

What comes back is a draft. The main path corrects it in Word, in the review copy; to correct it here, open Work section by section and click into the text in its row. Finalise keeps that wording across redrafts. A finalised section is read-only until you press Re-open beside it, and goes out of date when the record changes, the same as a paragraph, with Finalise reading Finalise against the current record until you have read it again.

#Audit, repair and polish

At the Connected AI tier, each factor's Analysis panel has an Audit action: it checks the paragraph against that factor's own record, never the whole investigation, and reports verdicts in the same Checked / Supported / Unsupported / Contradicted / Distorted format as the whole-report audit. It runs on the checking model, set in Settings > AI drafting > AI models as a model's Checking model (ideally a different, smaller model from the one that drafted, since a checker from the same family tends to wave through its own confident mistakes). The Copy and paste tier has no separate audit to copy out, because its drafting prompt already carries one. The result is shown inline and is never saved with the investigation: it is a second opinion, checked again by hand, not a certificate.

A paragraph drafted on Connected AI is also swept by a set of mechanical checks the moment it lands: a factor whose Influence test did not pass being written as having contributed, a probability word other than the one recorded against the test, the investigation's own test notation leaking into report prose, and American spelling. These run with no model at all, so they are instant and always available; a pasted reply skips them, on the view that the investigator reads and signs whatever they bring back themselves. The warnings sit under the paragraph and clear the moment you edit the text or finalise it; they never block Finalise.

In Draft my report

Draft my report runs the audit and a repair as two steps of its own run, so the buttons below are for running them again by hand. A model whose Drafting mode is Rough draft (the starting choice for a model on this computer or your network) skips both, and the smoothing too; see A Rough draft under Draft my report. The audit reads the whole report in one go, or section by section where the checking model's loaded context cannot hold the whole (Automatic, the default; Settings > AI drafting > Drafting options > Advanced can fix it to either), and asks nothing. The repair then goes over the flagged sections, and a correction lands by itself only when its own guards pass: the re-audit is clean or better, no recorded fact was lost, no new record-check error appears and the reply holds no chatter. Each landed correction is one undo step and gets a comment in the review copy saying what the sentence said before. A correction that fails a guard is not landed, and its claims stay marked in the review copy. A section you edited while the run worked holds its correction on the repair panel under Work section by section. Polish is not part of the run unless Polish the whole report at the end of Draft my report is ticked in Settings > AI drafting > Drafting options, and even then its wording waits under Work section by section for you to land.

Repair against the audit

Once there is an audit, Repair against the audit sends back only the sections where the audit found an unsupported or contradicted claim, with only the flagged claims to correct (a record-check warning or note alone never sends a section back, and when the audit is clean the button says there is nothing to repair), re-audits each one, and shows the result beside the text it would replace. A Findings or summary bullet is judged against the narrower seven-term scale those two sections are held to, not the full ten, so a bullet correctly narrowed to one of those terms is no longer flagged as unsupported, and a summary may also state a finding plainly, with no probability term at all, since the Findings section already carries it. Run by hand, nothing is written until you land it, section by section: the model proposes and you accept. A section the pass could not improve is shown too, with the reason, because "the re-audit was no better" is worth reading. A repair that cuts something the record carries, such as a figure or a passage quoted from an extract, is never offered, even when its re-audit counts fewer problems: the audit can be wrong, and fewer flags bought by losing a correct fact is a worse section. Each section is audited against exactly what it was drafted from, its scaffold included, so a fact the drafter was given is not flagged for want of the source.

A repair lands only onto the text it was made from. If you edit a section while its repair is running, or between the repair and pressing Land, nothing is changed: the repair stays on the panel, marked as changed, with the text it was made from, the section as it now stands and the repaired text side by side. Land over my edits replaces your edits with the repair, as one step Undo reverses; Discard keeps the section as you left it. A polished section is held to the same rule, and is refused rather than landed over an edit, so polish it again. Edit a marked section back to the text the repair was made from and a plain Land is offered again. Repair and polish work on the sections unless there is a whole report document. Then the Check band has Apply to, with the sections or the whole report document, and the buttons name which one they will work over.

Working through the audit one claim at a time

On the Draft report tab, the audit lists each claim it found a problem with on its own row, grouped by section, in the same words the review copy uses: the weight first (Fix before it goes out), then what the draft says and where it goes wrong, what the investigation says, and How to fix. The counts and the audit's full table are under Full audit. The record check beside it reads the same way: each check leads with its weight (Fix before it goes out, Worth a look or For your information), says what is wrong, and gives How to fix, with the checks to fix first in each section. A check that names an object has Go to the object, which opens the object on its Analysis panel (the message names the test to look at), and Show on the map. Each audit row has its own actions.

  • Go to the sentence opens the section the claim is in (and the part, for a section drafted in parts) and marks the sentence. A claim the app cannot place in any drafted section says so instead.
  • Repair this, at the Connected AI tier, runs the same repair on that one claim only: the section holding it goes back to the model with that claim and the record it was audited against, is re-audited, and is shown beside the text it would replace under the same rules as above. Nothing lands until you land it.
  • Copy repair prompt, at the Copy and paste tier, copies the same request for that one claim, for you to take to your chat tool. Read the reply against the record before you paste it over the section.
  • Mark resolved records that you have dealt with the claim, usually after correcting it by hand. It is saved with the investigation, so when a later audit tables the same claim it shows as resolved, it drops out of the count to resolve, and Repair against the audit leaves it alone. The mark holds only while the section reads as it did when you marked it: change the text, or clear, redraft or repair the section, and the claim counts as open again. Reopen takes the mark off. Undo reverses either.

Polish the prose

Polish the prose comes after that, and Polish this section, in a section's open row beside its Draft and Audit buttons, does one section at a time. It asks for the same meaning in better sentences, and only that: no fact, figure, name, date, probability term or finding may change, nor the order the findings are stated in, and the headings, the figure mentions and the Findings preamble come back word for word. Then it proves it. The polished section has to point at every figure the drafted one pointed at, record the same terms in the same findings, and raise nothing new in the record check. Then a second reading sets the two texts side by side and lists anything the original says and the polished wording no longer says: a fact, a figure, a name, a date, a quantity, a probability term or a qualification. The list has to come back empty. Last, a fresh audit has to leave no more claims unsupported than before, and contradict no more. Anything less and the drafted wording stays, with the reason on screen, naming what went missing. What passes is shown beside the text it would replace and landed by you, section by section.

Polish the prose sits in the Check band whenever AI access is at Connected AI, even before anything is drafted: disabled, with "Draft a section first" as its reason, rather than missing outright. A model also has Polish granularity under Advanced in Settings > AI drafting > AI models, used only for a server on this machine: set to one paragraph at a time, an experiment for small local models, the pass rewrites a paragraph at a time instead of a section, puts a paragraph that lost something back as drafted and tells you which, and still holds the whole section to every test above before it may land.

Settings > AI drafting > Drafting options has a Polish group, and its first tick, Polish each section as it is drafted, is off by default and used only at the Connected AI tier. Turned on, a section draft that lands from its row goes straight into the same polish pass, over exactly what was just drafted, and is offered the same way the Polish button offers it: nothing lands unseen, and the repair pass is never chained onto this. It applies to section drafts only: Draft my report never polishes, and you polish from the Check band, under Work section by section, once the content is right. The Copy and paste tier pastes every reply back by hand, so there is no automatic pass for it to run.

Where a draft came from

Every drafted text carries where it came from and whether it still matches the record. A badge reads "Drafted by" and the model's name, or "Pasted from chat", and a document brought in from elsewhere says that instead. When the record underneath it changes (a test result, a factor's paragraph, the interview it rested on) the text is marked Out of date and its Finalise button reads "Finalise against the current record" until you have read it again. A new Log entry does not count as a change to the record, whether you typed it or the app wrote it for a draft, a paste or an export. Only a finalised, current text feeds a later drafting pass, so neither an unreviewed generation nor one the record has moved past can quietly become the input to the next.

Drafting retries automatically on transient server errors, and What happened under Run details shows each step of Draft my report and which were retried. An interview's record is yours to write and finalise; drafting is given it only once finalised, or your finalised interview summary in its place while that still matches the record. The interview summary has its own Copy prompt and, at Connected AI, Draft with AI, both held while a name in the interview is still undecided. Check against audio, under a corrected transcript, asks a model only where the transcription is probably wrong, and changes nothing until you accept an item.

#Connecting a model

The investigation itself runs with no model involved. Drafting, and the few other AI buttons listed under What is sent to a model, are the only parts that want one, and how they reach one is AI access, a single setting you control.

What an endpoint is, in this app's terms

An endpoint is a named profile describing one model server; the Models pane lists them. Each one shows where it sends, worked out from its address (this computer, your network, or the internet with the host named), and when it last passed a test. The one marked In use is the one every Draft goes to. Add a model offers the presets under Stays on this computer (LM Studio, Ollama), Stays on your network (either on another computer) and Leaves your network (your organisation's gateway, the OpenAI or Anthropic public API), with Something else for a server you set up field by field.

Each model asks for a Name, an Address, an API key where the service needs one, a Writing model and a Checking model (picked from the list Refresh list fetches, or typed in), each with its own Reasoning, and the Factor detail to send, either the full argument or the conclusions only. Factor detail applies only at Connected AI; Copy and paste always sends the full argument. Drafting mode is Full or Rough draft: Rough draft writes the sections and summary only, with no smoothing and no automatic audit, and is where a model on this computer or your network starts (see A Rough draft). The Checking model runs the audit and the checks after repair and polish; a smaller, cheaper model is fine there, and with none set the writing model runs them, and a Checking reasoning left on Default thinks as the writing model does. Protocol, Auth header, the Anthropic API version and the experimental polish by paragraph sit under Advanced, since a preset fills them. Done checks the model before it is kept: an address that is missing or still holds a placeholder, a public service with no key, or no drafting model is shown beside its field, and a plain http:// address outside your network is flagged.

Test sends a short test prompt ("Reply with OK.") to the drafting model and, where it differs, the checking model, after asking the server for its model list. It carries no investigation content but costs a few tokens on a paid API, which the button says. It reports on the line beside it whether both models answered, or what failed (a refused key, a model that did not answer), and the result is kept with the time, so the AI access pane can show it.

Protocol is the part worth understanding, because it decides whether a given server will work at all. OpenAI-compatible posts each call to /chat/completions under the base URL, which is the shape LM Studio, Ollama and most corporate gateways expose. Anthropic Messages API posts to /messages instead and sends a version header with every call. Either way the app asks the server's /models path for the model list, and the key, if there is one, goes in the header you name, with Authorization and a Bearer prefix assumed when you leave it blank. A server that speaks neither of those two shapes needs a gateway in front of it that does.

A local model on your own machine

LM Studio or Ollama runs a model on the investigator's own computer, and the app reaches it at an address like http://localhost:1234/v1. The server needs CORS enabled and a capable model loaded with generous context: 32k tokens at minimum, more for a large investigation. On data, this is the strongest option available: nothing leaves the computer, so there is no agreement to negotiate and no provider to trust. On cost, there is none beyond the hardware you already have. On quality, it is the weakest of the three, because a model that fits on a laptop is far smaller than a hosted one, holds less of the record at once and writes less well, which is why the conclusions-only setting exists, and why a model on this computer starts on Rough draft. There is nothing to ask IT for unless the machine is locked down, in which case you need permission to install the runner.

The same server can run on another machine on the same private network. Reaching LM Studio there needs "Serve on local network" and CORS turned on in its Developer tab; reaching Ollama needs OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0 and OLLAMA_ORIGINS=* set on that machine, and the firewall left open between the two. Once either is reachable, Refresh list (or Test) fetches its model list and shows which model is loaded and at what context, so there is nothing to type from memory. The Models page never contacts a server on its own when it opens.

Your organisation's private cloud endpoint

Most organisations that allow model use at all do it this way: AWS Bedrock, Azure OpenAI, Google Vertex AI, or an internal gateway sitting in front of one of them. On data, the material goes to the cloud but stays inside the organisation's own tenancy and its own agreement with the provider, which ordinarily means no training on your content and a stated retention period. On cost, it is metered per call and paid by the organisation, and a whole-report draft is a handful of large calls rather than thousands of small ones. On quality, it is the best of the three, because these are the frontier models and they can hold the entire record. Ask IT for the base URL, whether it speaks the OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic shape, which header the key goes in, the deployment or model names for the drafting and audit roles, a key, and the retention terms in force so you can record them.

Your organisation's own hosted model

Some organisations host an open-weight model on their own servers and expose it as an internal API. On data, this is as contained as a local model while still being a proper service, since the material never leaves the organisation's own infrastructure. On cost, there is no per-call charge, but the organisation carries the hardware and the people to run it. On quality, it sits between the other two and depends entirely on which model they chose and how much context it is configured to accept. Ask IT the same questions as for a cloud endpoint, and one more: how much context the deployment accepts, because a record that does not fit is the usual reason drafting fails on an internal server.

A hosted public API

#Method and wording: Standard of proof

Standard of proof sets the standard a new investigation starts with, as three bands: Pass, Inconclusive and Fail. Start from a preset, or choose Custom and set Fail at or below and Pass at or above yourself, in steps of 1 per cent; a value that would cross the other line cannot be chosen. The bar shows the three bands as they stand, with the ten terms of the probability scale under it, and a term a line cuts through is named (“likely: Inconclusive below 70%, Pass from 70%”), so you can see which words a Pass, an Inconclusive and a Fail will be written in. A line at 50, or a standard with no Inconclusive band at all, gets a short note; it never stops you saving. See Confidence, probability terms and the standard of proof.

This page is only the default. Each investigation keeps the standard recorded in it, shown and changed on its Details tab, so opening it on a computer set to another default changes nothing. #Method and wording: Probability wording

Probability wording holds the band edges and the words a report may use for each term on the probability scale. The ten terms themselves are fixed, so findings stay comparable between investigations; what you can change is how each one may be said. "Almost certainly" means the same band as "extremely likely", "probably" the same band as "likely", and a drafting model may reach for any phrase you list. Each row starts at the wording in force, so you edit the list in front of you rather than retyping it, and clearing a row hands it back to the built-in. A phrase already accepted under another term is refused, since a phrase meaning two bands would make every check that reads it ambiguous. The report checks read every phrase in use back as its term, so a report can read like English without a single claim changing strength. The confidence slider on an object's tests moves in steps of 5 per cent from 5 to 100, and the number beside it takes any whole percentage, so every 5 per cent line of a standard of proof is a position you can record.

The pane is a table, one row per band, with three columns: From, Band and Term. The From figure is the lowest percentage the band covers, and you can change it: the app ships with the IPCC scale as the ATSB adopted it (research report AR-2007-053), with "likely" starting at 65 per cent so the default pass line sits at its start, and an organisation working to different numbers edits them here. An edge is shared with the band below, so moving one number moves one boundary and the bands can never gap, overlap or swap order; a number outside what its neighbours allow is pulled back to the nearest value that works. The Band column reads the band back to you and names the slider steps that can record it, since two bands at the bottom of the scale, 1 to 4 per cent and under 1, have no slider step at all. Reset every band puts the standard table back. A moved edge follows through everywhere at once: the word the object form shows beside a slider, the term strip on the standard of proof page, the findings scaffold and every check that reads a term from a percentage.

The Term column holds the phrases, each with a tick that takes it out of use without losing it, so an organisation that does not want "ruled out" read as a probability can switch it off and switch it back on later; a phrase out of use is offered to no drafting pass and read back by no check. The term's own word has no tick box, because a band with no word for itself leaves a check nothing to read. Type in the field itself to add a phrase or take one off the list altogether.

Each term also has a tick reading May be stated plainly, with no hedge for a statement with no hedge at all. It is on for "virtually certain" only, and should stay that way: where the investigation recorded 100 per cent, writing "the pump was unserviceable" says exactly what the record says, while the same flat sentence against a recorded "likely" claims more than the evidence supports. A drafted paragraph that drops the hedge that way is flagged after the draft lands, alongside the check for a term from the wrong band.

#Method and wording: Finding terms

Finding terms applies at every AI access tier, the Record tab's export included, not only to drafting.

Finding terms holds the words a Findings section uses: the three group headings, the safety issue tag, and the lane at which a safety factor becomes a safety issue. The pane opens with the rule those words are attached to, so an organisation can see what it is customising. An object that fails Existence is an excluded hypothesis and is reported under the other-findings heading. One that passes Existence and Influence and increased risk is a contributing safety factor. One that passes Existence, does not settle Influence (it fails, or stays inconclusive) and then passes Importance is an other factor that increased risk. A factor that reduced risk goes under other findings as well. An object that existed but fails Influence and Importance is an event or condition, and is not a finding. The lane then decides the tag: either kind of safety factor sitting in Risk Controls, or in a lane above it, is also a safety issue. It keeps its own heading, carries the tag, and is listed again in the report's Safety issues section; below that lane it is a safety factor and nothing more. The threshold lane is a setting like the words are, so an organisation using its own lane names can move it. Three layers decide each word, and the nearest one wins. The app has a built-in; a report style can declare its own instead, which most do, so different bodies' formats read as they should; and anything you type here overrides both, for every style on this machine. Leave a field empty to let the style decide it, and Reset hands it back. The word you set is used by the findings scaffold, by the report check that reads a drafted Findings section, and in the instruction a drafting model is given, so the three can never disagree.

Contributing factors heading, Increased-risk factors heading, Other findings heading, Safety issue tag and Safety issues start at this lane are each marked overridden and carry a Reset once you set them.

When the checklist template changes, an open investigation's checklist can be brought up to it from the Dashboard, with its answers kept (see the Dashboard's checklist). Template overrides are stored on the machine, not in the investigation, and Reset to built-in returns any template to the edition's default.

Checklist

Seeds the investigator's checklist in a new investigation. Each uses a simple line format - # Section starts a checklist section, - item adds a question or checkbox, > text adds a guidance note or a statement to read aloud, and two leading spaces nest an item; background fields are one entry per line, with # heading heading a group of them.

A checklist question or note may end in a key in braces, such as - Has the scope been written down? {#scope}. The key is not shown and is not part of the question. It names the question for good, so when a later template rewords it under the same key, updating an investigation's checklist carries the answer across to the new wording. A question with no key is matched by its wording alone.

Interview preparation

Seeds the preparation checklist copied into every new interview: the scheduling, conduct and follow-up checks to work through before and around it. - item is a question answered Yes, No or N/A, two spaces indent it a level, and > text is a note. The Interview tab leaves out a question starting "After the interview".

Interview opening

Seeds the interview guide at the top of every new interview. It has its own line format:

  • - item is a question that must be covered, answered Yes, No or N/A; two spaces indent it a level
  • > text is a dot point that is not answered
  • # Section starts a section of the interview guide, and ? text under it is the If stuck line, the words to say
  • lines before the first section are the reminder at the top
  • a list without sections reads as a plain checklist

Interview background

Seeds the background details recorded for each interviewee, one field label per line. A line starting # is a heading over the fields after it, for a group only some interviewees fill in (the pilots' hours, say); it is shown as a heading, holds no value, and is left out of the exported table when nothing under it is filled in.

A label ending in {name}, such as Interviewee {name}, is a field that holds a person's name. The marker is not shown. What you type there goes into the exported interview synopsis and never to AI: it is left out of every prompt and of the drafting record, and where the record mentions that name anywhere else, the interviewee's role is sent in its place.

#Saving, autosave and undo

Save with Ctrl+S; a dot beside the project name marks unsaved changes, and autosave writes every five minutes once the file has a home. The dot clears only once the latest change is on disk, so an edit made while a save is still writing keeps it showing until the next save writes that edit too. If a save ever fails the app says so plainly, with a red notice rather than silence.

A save never writes over the file directly. It writes the new version to a temporary file in the same folder and only then puts it in place, so a save that fails part-way, or that cannot replace the file because another program has it open, leaves the file as it was and your changes still unsaved in the app. How well this protects the file still depends on the drive, OneDrive and network folders in particular, so keep your own backups.

Before writing over the open file, a save checks it is still the version this window opened or last saved. If the file cannot be checked at all, because it has been moved or deleted or its drive is not available, nothing is written: the red notice says the file could not be checked, and your changes stay open and unsaved. Save again once the file is back, or use File, Save as… to keep a copy somewhere else.

Save as, and a copy saved from a shared-file conflict, never replace a file that is already there unless it is an investigation this version can open; the replace question your computer asks is not enough on its own. If you choose another program's file, a damaged or incomplete one, or an investigation saved by a newer version, nothing is written and the app says why: Choose another name or location… opens the save dialog again in the same folder, and Cancel leaves your changes open and unsaved, with the next Save still going to the investigation's own file.

An open, a save or an export that takes more than a moment shows a small window, Opening…, Saving… or Exporting…, with a moving bar and the step it is on, such as Writing the new version or Replacing the file. It appears only if the job is still running after about half a second, so quick ones never flash it, and it closes when the job has really finished: for a save, once the file is in place. The bar does not fill, because the computer does not report how far a write has got; the step is what tells you it is still working. While it is showing you cannot edit, and if the job fails or the file is refused, the window gives way to the message about it. The automatic save every five minutes does not use it; it shows only a small Still saving… at the foot of the window if it runs long, so typing is never interrupted.

Closing the window writes any unsaved changes first, waiting for a save already under way; a save that takes more than a moment shows the Saving… window, which says the window will close once it has finished. If that save fails, the window stays open and the error is shown, so nothing is lost. Close again and you are asked what to do: Retry Save tries again and closes only once it works, Cancel (the default) keeps the window open so you can use File, Save as… to put the investigation somewhere else, and Discard Changes closes without saving them. An investigation that has never been saved anywhere is asked about at once: Save As… chooses where to put it and then closes, Cancel keeps it open, and only Discard Changes closes without it. Pressing Escape or closing the window again never discards anything.

Opening or starting another investigation while this one has unsaved changes asks first, before any file is chosen: Open, Reopen last, New investigation and the worked example all ask the same question. Save writes your changes and carries on only once the save has worked; if it fails you are told why and asked again. Cancel (the default) keeps this investigation open with its changes, and Don't Save carries on without them. An investigation that has never been saved offers Save As… in place of Save. Escape, or a click outside the question, is Cancel.

Deleted objects are recoverable from Edit, Recycle bin, and File, Reopen last restores the previous investigation at the start of the next session.

Open, Reopen last and the imports check a file before anything else happens. A file saved by a newer version of the app, one whose file format version is not recognised, one that is incomplete or damaged, and one that is not an investigation at all are each refused with a message saying which, and nothing changes: the investigation already open stays open with its unsaved changes, Reopen last still names the file it named, and the refused file is left exactly as it was. An investigation saved by an earlier version opens as normal and is written in the current file format the next time you save it.

Every edit to the investigation is undoable, on every tab, through one history of the last 100 steps. Ctrl+Z undoes the most recent step and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes it; the Undo and Redo buttons on the map, the Timeline and the findings map do the same, and their tooltips name the step. A burst of typing in one box is one step, ending when you pause for about a second, move to another box or tab, or do anything else. A model result landing in the Draft report is a step of its own too.

While the cursor is in a text box, Ctrl+Z undoes your typing in that box only; click outside it to undo the investigation. Nothing is undone while a form is open. When the step undone belongs to another tab, the app switches to that tab, and a brief note at the foot of the window says what was undone or redone. Undo marks the investigation unsaved, so the next save writes the undone state; the history survives a save, and starts afresh when another investigation is opened or the version on disk is taken in a shared-file conflict. The Log is never undone (see Reading an entry).

#Investigations on a shared folder

An investigation file on OneDrive or a network drive can be open on two machines at once, and every save writes the whole file, so the last writer would otherwise win without either person noticing. Two guards sit under that. The app notes the file's size and modification time each time it reads or writes it, and if the copy on disk has changed since, the save is held and you choose what happens: take theirs and reload, save yours alongside as a copy, or write over theirs. There is no merge, because a whole investigation file cannot be merged safely, so the choice is deliberate and it is yours. Overwrite never loses their version: before writing over it, the app keeps it, exactly as it was, in a new file beside the investigation named for when it was kept, such as Tarlton (their version 13-10-2026 1405).iwsx (an older.sfm keeps its own ending), and the Log entry for the overwrite names that file. If the copy cannot be made, nothing is written over, the question stays open and your changes stay unsaved. Beside the file the app also keeps a lock file named after the investigation file with .lock added (Tarlton.iwsx.lock, or Tarlton.sfm.lock for an older file) while the investigation is open, refreshed about every minute and removed on close (one not refreshed for three minutes is treated as left over and ignored), so whoever opens it next is told you have it. The lock only warns: a synced folder can delay it by minutes, and a stale one from a crash or a sleeping laptop is ignored, so the size-and-time check is the guard that actually holds.

If the copy on disk has become a file this version cannot open, for example because a colleague saved it with a newer version of the app, theirs can be neither reloaded nor written over. The app says so and offers only Save mine as a copy…, which keeps your changes in a new file, and Decide later; nothing is written to their file, autosave waits, and the window will not close on unsaved work while it is held. Overwrite is refused in the same way whenever the file on disk is not an investigation this version can open.

12Licence and levels

Free, Professional and Organisation: what each includes, the trial, entering a licence, and what happens when one lapses.

A licence decides a level, and the level decides which features are available. Licensing controls features, never your access to your own investigation data.

#The three levels

  • Free conducts and structures an investigation: the map, evidence, the timeline, the checklist, interviews, the log and the to-dos, and exports of the working record to Word, the Evidence and Argument Tables, the map and timeline images, the report-drafting collation and the project snapshot.
  • Professional completes it: AI drafting (an endpoint you set up), AI drafting by copy and paste (copying a prompt out and pasting the reply back), finished Word reports and slides, built-in looks and their options for every Word document (at Free every Word document uses the Modern look), and editing the shared organisation configuration (reading and using one works at every level). The AI is yours to choose; Professional allows every AI access tier you configure.

With no licence, the app runs at the Free level, and what it exports goes out clean, with no watermark. A control that needs Professional stays in view, disabled, with the line "Available with Professional." beside it. One that needs Organisation says "Available with Organisation."

#90-day Professional trialNot yet available

Not yet available: the trial is switched on once the licence server is live. A free 90-day Professional trial will be offered the first time the app runs, on one quiet line under the header; Not now puts it away, and Settings > General > Licence offers it again. Give a name and email and the app asks the licence server for a signed trial licence for this email and this installation, once. Only the name, the email, the edition and a random number identifying the installation are sent; no investigation data. Offline, keep working in Free and start the trial later. A build with no licence server configured makes no trial offer anywhere. During the trial the header chip reads "Trial" with the days left.

#Entering a licence

A licence arrives as a block of text beginning IWS1.. Open Settings, choose General > Licence, paste the whole thing into the box (line breaks and all, they do no harm) and press Apply licence. Remove licence takes it off this computer, and the app goes back to Free. The pane then shows the level in force, who the licence names, and when it runs out. It is checked on this machine against a key built into the app; nothing is sent anywhere, and it works with no network at all. If a paste is refused, the pane says which of the problems it is: not a licence, not issued for this application, issued for a different edition, expired, or a computer clock set earlier than the last date the app saw or the licence's issue date. The app header carries a chip reading Free, Professional, Organisation, or Trial: n days left; hover it for who the licence names and its expiry date (on an unlicensed copy, for what Professional adds), and click it to open this pane.

#When a licence lapses

Nothing is ever locked. When a licence lapses or a trial ends, the app drops to Free and only the Professional actions wait: text drafted while you were licensed stays in the file, readable and editable, and goes out in the project snapshot and the collation as before. Nothing is deleted or hidden, and the app writes nothing to your investigation because the level changed. A stored Word template is kept, unapplied, until a licence allows it again.

A paid licence keeps working for 14 days after its expiry date, so a renewal that arrives late by email does not interrupt an investigation; the pane says when one is in that period. A trial has no such period. One thing to know about the clock: if the machine's date is set more than a day earlier than the last date the app saw, the licence is treated as expired until the date is put right; a timezone or daylight-saving change does not trip it.
