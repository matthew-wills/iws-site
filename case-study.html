Case study, worked end to end

An investigation from the notification to the published report

Tarlton Springs is a rejected take-off and runway overrun at a regional aerodrome at night, investigated in the application from the first checklist item to the reports that were issued. Every stage below is a real screen from that investigation. Click any picture to see it at full size.

Everything in this case study is fictional: the operator, the aerodrome, the aircraft, the people and every record. The documents at the end are published exactly as the application produced them, faults and all.

Notification and triage

Open the investigation and triage what came in

The notification arrives, the occurrence is risk rated, and the decision to investigate is made. File > New investigation creates a folder with the investigation file inside it, and the first checklist section is the one that covers notification, triage and the terms of reference.

The checklist runs from there through evidence gathering, interviews, analysis and reporting. Each item is answered yes, no or not applicable, and the tab shows how much is still open.

What the app recordsThe occurrence title, the checklist with every answer, and the distribution list the report will eventually go to.

Work the checklist, and keep one to-do list

Anything that needs chasing goes on a single list. A job raised while filling in an object's form appears here with a link back to that object, so the list can be read in one place and handed to a colleague.

The checklist is a template, not a fixed list. An investigator edits it in Settings to suit how they work; an organisation's own edition ships with its own, so every investigation starts from the same standard.

What the app recordsEvery to-do with its source, and which checklist items are still open.

Collecting the evidence

File every piece of material once

Screenshots, extracts and documents go into one register with a caption, its significance, a category and a reference. Each item is linked to whatever rests on it, so when a source turns out to be unreliable it is clear what has to be revisited.

What the app recordsThe capture itself inside the investigation file, why it matters, and every object, interview or finding that depends on it.

Take in the safety reports as they were written

The reports submitted by the people involved are pasted in as written, with who submitted them by role, when, and the reference in the reporting system. They feed the picture the same way an interview does.

One tick says the report came in under a confidential or just-culture scheme. From then on drafting is told the account may inform the investigation but must not be quoted into a document that will be distributed.

What the app recordsThe account verbatim, its reference and time, the submitter's role, and the protection it was given.

Turn the interviews into settled records

Import the Teams transcript, as the .docx or .vtt download, or paste it; correct what the transcription got wrong; add your notes; finalise. From then on the account is settled and report drafting treats it that way.

De-identification is built in. Each speaker in a transcript is mapped to a role, and it is the role, never the name, that reaches the record, the collation, or any drafting model.

What the app recordsThe corrected transcript, the role for every speaker, the investigator's notes beside it, and whether the account has been finalised.

Build the sequence of events

Each event carries its source and its time basis, so the sequence can be defended when two records disagree about when something happened. The timeline exports as an image beside the report.

What the app recordsThe ordered events, the evidence behind each one, the time basis it rests on, and which event is the critical one.

Building the causal map

Place the critical event

The occurrence goes in the bottom lane, with what happened and the proof of its significance recorded. Everything else on the map exists to explain this one object.

What the app recordsThe critical event as an object, in its lane, at its point in time.

Put every hypothesis on the map

An object for every event or condition that might have played a part, in the lane it belongs to and roughly where it sits in time. Nothing is filtered at this point: the method works by testing, not by intuition, and a hypothesis that is later excluded still strengthens the record.

What the app recordsEach hypothesis as an untested object, shown with a dashed white border until someone has tested it.

Adjust the map live as the evidence comes in

An investigation does not arrive in order. A transcript changes what a factor means, a record arrives that closes a line of enquiry, a new hypothesis appears in week three. Objects are moved, tested, connected and reconnected as that happens, and the map is never more than the current state of the evidence.

Every arrow is a claim that one thing led to another. It stays grey until the object it comes from has passed its tests, and turns green when it has, so the picture builds itself as the work is done.

What the app recordsEvery object and every connection, with the state of each test behind them.

The three tests

Nothing is a finding until it has passed its tests

Three questions are asked of every object on the map, in order, and a confidence is recorded for each answer. What the object becomes on the map follows from the answers.

Existence

Did this event or condition actually exist?

If it did, the border turns solid and the next question is asked. If it did not, the hypothesis is excluded: it stays on the map, struck through, with the reason recorded.

PassFailInconclusive
Influence

Did it influence anything else, and if so in what way?

If it did, it is a contributing safety factor and its outgoing arrows become proven causal paths. Where the evidence cuts both ways, the test is argued with evidence for and against.

PassFailArgued
Importance

If it did not influence anything, is it still important enough to raise in the report?

A factor that played no part in this occurrence may still be a risk worth addressing. If so, it is a finding in its own right, an other risk factor, and it carries no arrows.

PassFail
  • Blue, solid border: a contributing safety factor. It existed and it influenced the occurrence.
  • Orange: an other risk factor. It existed, it did not influence this occurrence, and it is still worth raising.
  • Struck through, dashed: an excluded hypothesis. It did not exist, and the reason is on the record.
  • Green, rounded: a positive event, something that reduced the risk or limited the outcome.
  • Red, rounded: the critical event, the occurrence itself.
  • White, dashed: not yet tested, or a test left inconclusive.
  • Green arrow: a proven causal path. The object it comes from has passed Influence.
  • Grey dashed arrow: a claim still under test.
  • Green dot: the object's investigation is complete and its evidence finalised.
  • Amber dot: still being investigated.

Testing each factor

Record the evidence and answer three questions

  • Did this event or condition actually exist?
  • Did it influence anything else, and if so in what way?
  • If it did not influence anything, is it still important enough to raise in the report?

Each answer carries a confidence, recorded as a percentage and read back in the calibrated verbal probability terms the report will use. The standard of proof a factor must reach before it counts as established is set for the investigation, from the balance of probabilities upwards, and the form warns when a pass falls short of it.

What the app recordsEach verdict with its confidence and its evidence, the investigator's reasoning, and the amber dot that turns green when the object's own investigation is finished.

Argue the contested ones

When the evidence cuts both ways, record it for and against and let the confidence say how far the argument reaches. The report will carry that confidence word, not a certainty the record does not support.

What the app recordsBoth sides of the argument as separate evidence, and the confidence the investigator settled on after weighing them.

The finished map

Read the whole investigation in one image

Left to right is time. Bottom to top is organisational depth. Green arrows are the proven chains; a grey dashed arrow is a claim still under test; struck-through objects are the closed lines of enquiry. A green dot marks an object whose investigation is complete, an amber dot one still being investigated.

What the app recordsThe map as the analysis, exported as an image beside the report, with the findings derived from the tests rather than typed in.

Drafting, checking and publishing

Draft the report a section at a time, or in one run

Choose a style and work through its sections: draft one, correct it, finalise it, and nothing regenerates over a section that has been finalised. A whole-report run is there for a model with the context for the entire investigation.

With AI access off, the tab says so and the report is assembled from the record by hand instead. Nothing is sent anywhere until an endpoint has been chosen.

What the app recordsEach section with the model and the version of the record that produced it, so a draft goes visibly stale when the record moves on.

Choose where the model lives

The application speaks the standard model APIs. This investigation was drafted through a model on the investigator's own machine; the same pane takes a hosted API with your own key, or an organisation's own gateway. Keys are stored on the machine and never written into the investigation file or an export.

What the app recordsThe endpoint and the drafting and audit models, on this machine only, and how much of each argument is sent with a safety factor.

Audit the draft, check it mechanically, then finalise it

A second pass reads the finished draft against the whole record and reports every claim it could not find or found contradicted. A mechanical check with no model in it then compares each factor's category and confidence word in the text against its recorded test, and the findings against the derived classification.

Both are published with the reports below, because what the audit caught is the reason a draft is never final until an investigator has read it.

What the app recordsThe audit and the record check beside the draft, the review notes the model made about the record itself, and the investigator's decision to finalise.
