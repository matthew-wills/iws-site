What it is

One application, and one file, for the whole investigation

Most investigations are run across a dozen tools: notes in one application, evidence in a folder, interviews in another, a diagram drawn by hand, and a report written from memory of all of it. The record and the report drift apart, and the reasoning that connects a piece of evidence to a finding lives only in the investigator's head.

This is one application, and one file, for the whole of that work. Every hypothesis is an object on the AcciMap that must pass its tests before it can be called a finding, and the report is a view of the record, not a separate document. The method is a systemic one: the map uses the AcciMap layout, with organisational levels as lanes, and each factor is tested for Existence, Influence and Importance with a recorded confidence.

It runs on the investigator's own computer. Nothing is uploaded, no account is needed, and the file can sit on a secure shared drive while the work runs and be archived when it is closed.

The workflow

The investigation runs in loops, not a straight line

One file, no hand-off between tools, but no fixed order either. The record builds up in rounds, and the report at the end is assembled from what those rounds produced.

The investigator is notified of the occurrence and decides whether an investigation is required. From there the checklist starts, and whatever evidence is immediately on hand goes in. The timeline and the AcciMap are started early too, often from very little.

The rest of the work runs as a loop rather than a sequence. More evidence is gathered to fill in the timeline and the map, and to fill out the objects placed on it. The checklist, the log and the to-do list are kept up to date as that happens, and the loop continues until the timeline and the map are complete.

An object on the map carries its own to-dos, and needs more evidence before it can be finalised. A to-do raised on an object goes straight onto the main to-do list, so what is still outstanding, whether that is evidence to chase or research to do, is visible at any point in the investigation.

One list, one evidence store

A to-do written on an object's form is the same list as the Checklist tab's to-do list, not a copy of it, with a jump back to the object it came from. Evidence pasted into an object's form is held once, and it shows in the Evidence tab grouped by what it is used for, so the investigator can see what rests on what without leaving the map.

Why it helps

The direction of the investigation in a single image

AI drafting

A model can draft the report, but only from the record and only under check

A capable language model writes fluent prose from a large body of material in minutes, and it reads a record more consistently than a tired investigator at the end of a long week. For a report that is mostly the record retold under headings, that is real value.

The same model, left to itself, will also fill gaps from its own general knowledge, round "likely" up to "virtually certain", and write a factor that failed its Influence test back in as contributing. None of that is visible in the prose, which is the problem: the errors read exactly like the truths. So the question is how to use a model such that every sentence it writes can be traced to the record and checked against it.

What the model produces is a first draft of each section, built from the record. The investigator works from that head start: reading it against the record, correcting what is wrong or overstated, and finalising it. The report that gets published is the investigator's, not the model's.

What goes wrong without control
  • 1Facts and figures invented to fill a gap, in the same confident voice as the real ones.
  • 2Confidence overstated: "very likely" becomes "virtually certain", and an argued test becomes a settled fact.
  • 3Evidence reweighed: a factor the investigator excluded is quietly written back in as contributing.
  • 4Recommendations the investigation never made, because reports usually end with some.
  • 5A protected report quoted as if it were an ordinary statement.
  • 6People named where a role was all the report needed.
  • 7Investigation material sent to a service that keeps it, trains on it, or is outside the organisation's control.
  • 8No record of which model, which prompt, or which version of the evidence produced the text.

The drafting pipeline

Run the same way whichever model is behind it. Each stage exists to catch one of the failures above.

The whole record, never a fragment

The model is sent every object with its tests and confidences, the timeline, the finalised interviews and the evidence, with roles in place of names.

Grounding rules on every prompt

Nothing that is not in the record, no invented figures, no recommendation the record does not carry, and the confidence word the test actually produced.

Audited, then checked

A second pass reports every claim it cannot find in the record. A mechanical check then compares each factor's category and confidence word against its test.

Provenance and staleness

Every draft records the model and the version of the record it came from, and is marked out of date when the record changes.

Local

A model running on the investigator's own machine (LM Studio, Ollama). Nothing leaves the computer.

Hosted, your key

A public API such as Anthropic's or OpenAI's, with a key the investigator holds. The pane states what will be sent before it is sent.

Organisation gateway

An organisation's own secured endpoint, such as a private model deployment, so the material never leaves its control.

Reports and exports

Several reports, and the working behind them, come out of the one record

A report style is a set of headings with guidance for each, and the record is assembled or drafted under them. Every report is written to a Word template, with the organisation's own layout where a custom template has been supplied.

Preliminary update

Issued while the investigation runs: what has been established, the risk exposure, the open lines of enquiry. No findings.

Standard investigation

The full report: executive summary, event overview, analysis, findings summary and appendices.

Executive brief

The occurrence, the findings and the actions on a few pages for the people who decide.

ATSB-style final report

Modelled on the structure and conventions of published ATSB final reports: summary, occurrence, context, safety analysis, findings, safety issues and actions.

NTSB-style probable cause report

The NTSB-shaped style: factual information, analysis, conclusions carrying the findings, the probable cause and the contributing factors, then recommendations.

AAIB-style causal and contributory factors report

The AAIB-shaped style: synopsis, factual information, analysis, conclusions carrying the findings and splitting causal from contributory factors, then safety action and recommendations.

Your organisation's

A style written to your report template, with your headings, cover and findings tables, in a custom build.
